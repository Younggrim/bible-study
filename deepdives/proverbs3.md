*Setting:* The third of the father's talks to his son in Proverbs 1–9 (see Proverbs 1 on authorship). Chapter 2 taught how to get wisdom: dig for it like silver, and receive it as God's gift. Chapter 3 shows what life looks like when a person walks with God in it. The first half (vv1–12) is a run of short commands, each with a promise attached, and it holds one of the best-loved verses in Scripture, "Trust in the LORD with all your heart." The second half praises wisdom as better than gold, the tree of life and the very tool God used to make the world, and then turns to how the wise treat their neighbors.

#### Trust in the LORD with All Your Heart (vv1–12)
- **"Do not forget my teaching, but let your heart keep my commandments" (v1).** "Teaching" is *torah*, and "commandments" is the word used for God's commands in Deuteronomy. The father's teaching is not his private opinion. He passes on God's ways, and he wants it kept in the heart, not just remembered.
- **"Length of days and years of life and peace" (v2).** "Peace" is *shalom*: wholeness, well-being, things as they should be. The promise of long life echoes the fifth commandment (Exod 20:12; Deut 5:16). Proverbs gives general truths, not guarantees. Wise living tends toward long life, but Ecclesiastes and Job are also in the Bible, and both know that the righteous sometimes die young (Eccl 7:15).
- **"Let not steadfast love and faithfulness forsake you" (v3).** This is *chesed* and *ʾemet*, the pair God used to describe himself at Sinai: "abounding in steadfast love and faithfulness" (Exod 34:6). The son is to show people the same loyal love and reliability God shows him. JFB notes that God's faithfulness to his promises "is often expressed by these terms."
- **"Bind them around your neck; write them on the tablet of your heart" (v3).** "Bind" echoes Deuteronomy 6:8, where God's words are to be bound on the hand and forehead. "Tablet" is the word for the stone tablets of the law. Jeremiah promised a new covenant in which God would write his law "on their hearts" (Jer 31:33), and Paul says the Spirit writes on "tablets of human hearts" (2 Cor 3:3).
- **"So you will find favor and good success in the sight of God and man" (v4).** The same pairing is said of Samuel (1 Sam 2:26) and of the boy Jesus, who "increased in wisdom and in stature and in favor with God and man" (Luke 2:52).
- **"Trust in the LORD with all your heart" (v5).** "Trust" is *batach*, to rely on, to feel secure leaning on someone. Some teachers say the word pictures lying face down before a conqueror. That comes from an Arabic word that may be related, which the lexicon mentions only as a comparison. In Hebrew usage the word simply means to rely on or be confident in. "With all your heart" means with the whole self, holding nothing back (Deut 6:5).
- **"Do not lean on your own understanding" (v5).** This is not a ban on thinking. Proverbs is full of calls to understand. It means not resting the weight of life on one's own judgment as if it were the final word.
- **"In all your ways acknowledge him" (v6).** "Acknowledge" is *yadaʿ*, to know. It means to know God personally and take him into account in everything, not just in religious moments. **"All"** appears twice in vv5–6, all your heart and all your ways.
- **"He will make straight your paths" (v6).** The verb (*yashar*) means to make straight or level, clearing the obstacles from a road (Isa 40:3–4; 45:2). JFB: literally "make plain." The promise is not mainly that God will reveal a hidden plan, though he guides. It is that he will make the road of the person who trusts him straight and passable, morally and practically.
- **"Be not wise in your own eyes; fear the LORD, and turn away from evil" (v7).** This is the negative side of v5. Pride in one's own wisdom is the opposite of fearing the LORD. Job was described in these words: he "feared God and turned away from evil" (Job 1:1).
- **"Healing to your flesh and refreshment to your bones" (v8).** "Flesh" is literally "navel," a way of saying the center of the body. "Refreshment" (*shiqquy*) is literally a drink, moisture for dry bones. Living in the fear of God brings wholeness, body and soul. JFB: "True piety promotes bodily health."
- **"Honor the LORD with your wealth and with the firstfruits of all your produce" (v9).** This is one of the few places Proverbs speaks of worship at the temple. The firstfruits were the first and best of the harvest, given to God before the rest was used (Exod 23:19; Deut 26:1–11). Giving first is a way of saying that everything comes from him.
- **"Then your barns will be filled with plenty" (v10).** This echoes the covenant blessings of Deuteronomy 28:8. God promises provision to those who honor him (Mal 3:10). It is a principle of God's generosity, not a formula for getting rich, and Paul puts it the same way: "God loves a cheerful giver… God is able to make all grace abound to you" (2 Cor 9:7–8).
- **"Do not despise the LORD's discipline or be weary of his reproof" (v11).** The section's final teaching is about hardship. "Discipline" is *musar*, training that may involve correction. After promises of health and plenty, the father adds that walking with God also includes painful correction. That is not a sign God has gone away.
- **"The LORD reproves him whom he loves, as a father the son in whom he delights" (v12).** The father speaking to his son points to God as the better Father. Hebrews 12:5–6 quotes these verses from the Greek translation (the Septuagint), which reads "and he chastises every son whom he receives." The Greek translators read the Hebrew letters for "as a father" (*ukeʾav*) as a verb meaning "to cause pain." The point is the same either way: discipline is proof of sonship (Heb 12:7–11).
- **Commentators:** Henry on v5: "Those who know themselves cannot but find their own understanding to be a broken reed." On v6 he writes, "Our eye must be ever towards God; to him we must, in every thing, make our requests known." Guzik stresses that half-trust is not trust, and lists the ordinary ways God directs our paths: his word, his Spirit, wise counsel, common sense and circumstances. Following Duane Garrett, he reads God's discipline as more like an athlete's or soldier's training than punishment. JFB calls trust in God "the center and marrow of true wisdom" and says afflictions "do not contradict" the promised blessing of the godly. The Tyndale notes add that long life is a general pattern, not a promise for every case.

#### Blessed Is the One Who Finds Wisdom (vv13–35)
- **"Blessed is the one who finds wisdom" (v13).** "Blessed" is *ʾashre*, the word that opens the Psalter (Ps 1:1): deeply happy, well off. This short hymn (vv13–18) begins and ends with that word (v18, "called blessed").
- **"Better than gain from silver… better than gold… more precious than jewels" (vv14–15).** Wisdom is pictured as a merchant's best investment. Chapter 8:11 repeats v15 almost exactly. Job 28:15–19 says wisdom cannot be bought with any gold at all.
- **"Long life is in her right hand; in her left hand are riches and honor" (v16).** Wisdom is pictured as a gracious lady holding out gifts in both hands. Egyptian art showed the goddess of truth and order, Maat, holding the sign of life in one hand and a scepter in the other, and many scholars think Israel's wise men may have used similar images. But here wisdom is not a goddess. She comes from the LORD (v19).
- **"Her ways are ways of pleasantness, and all her paths are peace" (v17).** Folly promises pleasure and delivers death (2:18; 9:17–18). Wisdom delivers what folly only promises.
- **"She is a tree of life to those who lay hold of her" (v18).** This points straight back to Eden. After the fall, the way to the tree of life was barred by cherubim and a flaming sword (Gen 3:22–24). Proverbs uses the phrase three more times (11:30; 13:12; 15:4). The idea is that life as God meant it, the life lost in the garden, can be tasted again by walking in his wisdom. The Bible ends with the tree of life restored "for the healing of the nations" (Rev 22:2).
- **"The LORD by wisdom founded the earth" (v19).** The reason wisdom is worth so much is that it is the same wisdom God used to make the world. The world is built according to God's wisdom, so living wisely is living with the grain of creation. Chapter 8:22–31 will develop this at length, with Wisdom beside God at creation "like a master workman."
- **"By his knowledge the deeps broke open, and the clouds drop down the dew" (v20).** The "deeps" (*tehomot*) are the waters below the earth, the "deep" of Genesis 1:2. "Broke open" (*baqaʿ*) is the verb used of the fountains of the great deep bursting at the Flood (Gen 7:11). The likely sense here is the springs and rivers God made to water the earth, along with the gentle dew from above. From the depths below to the dew above, God's wisdom provides.
- **"My son, do not lose sight of these" (v21).** The father begins a new talk. "Sound wisdom" is *tushiyyah*, practical, effective wisdom (as in 2:7).
- **"Then you will walk on your way securely… your sleep will be sweet" (vv23–24).** Day and night, walking and lying down. The same pairing is in Deuteronomy 6:7, about talking of God's commands "when you walk by the way, and when you lie down." Psalm 4:8 gives the same promise: "In peace I will both lie down and sleep."
- **"Do not be afraid of sudden terror… for the LORD will be your confidence" (vv25–26).** "Confidence" (*kesel*) can also mean the loins or flank, the seat of strength. The wise are not promised that disaster will never come, but that God will keep their foot from the trap (Ps 91:3–7).
- **"Do not withhold good from those to whom it is due" (v27).** Literally "from its owners." The good one is able to do belongs, in a sense, to the person who needs it. This covers wages owed (Deut 24:14–15) and also help for the needy. JFB: "Promptly fulfil all obligations both of justice and charity" (Jas 2:15–16).
- **"Do not say to your neighbor, 'Go, and come again, tomorrow I will give it'—when you have it with you" (v28).** Delay can be a quiet form of unkindness. The father moves through five "do nots" toward the neighbor (vv27–31): don't withhold, don't delay, don't scheme, don't quarrel, don't envy.
- **"Your neighbor, who dwells trustingly beside you" (v29).** "Trustingly" is from *batach*, the word for trust in v5. To harm a neighbor who feels safe beside you is a double betrayal.
- **"Do not envy a man of violence" (v31).** Violent people often seem to win, and Psalm 37 and 73 wrestle with exactly that. The answer comes in v32.
- **"The devious person is an abomination to the LORD, but the upright are in his confidence" (v32).** "Abomination" (*toʿevah*) is a strong word for what God finds detestable, used about twenty times in Proverbs. "Confidence" here is a different word, *sod*, the inner circle of friends who share secrets. "The friendship of the LORD is for those who fear him" (Ps 25:14). The violent man may have power, but the upright have God's friendship.
- **"Toward the scorners he is scornful, but to the humble he gives favor" (v34).** God treats people the way they treat him (Ps 18:25–26). Both James and Peter quote this verse from the Greek translation: "God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble" (Jas 4:6; 1 Pet 5:5). The Greek version made the scoffers "proud," but the meaning is the same. Those who mock God shut themselves out, and those who bow low receive grace.
- **"The wise will inherit honor, but fools get disgrace" (v35).** The chapter ends where the book's choice always ends: two ways, two destinations.
- **Commentators:** Henry identifies the wisdom of v19 with Christ: "Christ is that Wisdom, by whom the worlds were made and still consist" (John 1:3; Col 1:16–17). On the tree of life he writes: "True grace is that to the soul which the tree of life would have been." Guzik follows Allen Ross in saying that the wisdom that runs the universe is the same wisdom that should guide daily life. He reads "the deeps broke open" as possibly a reference to the Flood, though most commentators take it as creation's springs. On v34 he stresses that grace flows downhill, to the humble. JFB says wisdom is "allegorized as" the tree of life, "whose fruit preserves life," and that its "place… in the economy of creation and providence" commends it to people.

#### Pointing to Christ
"Trust in the LORD with all your heart" describes the life Jesus lived perfectly. He did not lean on his own understanding but said, "I can do nothing on my own… I seek not my own will but the will of him who sent me" (John 5:30). He is the Son in whom the Father delights (Matt 3:17), and he learned obedience through what he suffered (Heb 5:8). He is also the Wisdom by whom God founded the earth (John 1:3; Col 1:16), and in him the way to the tree of life opens again (Rev 2:7; 22:14). The God who opposes the proud gives grace to the humble through him.
