*Setting:* Still at Sinai, a few weeks before the camp sets out (10:11). Chapter 3 counted every male Levite from a month old (22,000) and gave each of the three clans, Kohath, Gershon and Merari, a camp site and a general charge. Chapter 4 is the working roster. It counts only the men of working age, thirty to fifty, and spells out exactly what each clan does when the cloud lifts and the tabernacle has to be taken down, packed and carried. It reads like a moving company's manual, and in a sense it is. But it is a manual for carrying the dwelling place of a holy God through the desert, and every detail is there to keep people alive in his presence.

#### Duties of the Kohathites (vv1–20)
- **"From thirty years old up to fifty" (v3).** These are men in their prime, strong enough for heavy carrying and mature enough to be trusted with holy things. Numbers 8:24 gives the starting age as twenty-five, and later, in David's day, when the work changed from carrying a tent to serving in a temple, it became twenty (1 Chr 23:24–27; Ezra 3:8). The usual explanation is that twenty-five to thirty was an apprenticeship, or that the ages were adjusted as needs changed. Jesus began his public ministry "about thirty years of age" (Luke 3:23).
- **"All who can come on duty" (v3).** The Hebrew is literally "everyone who enters the host to do the work." The word is *tsaba*, the root of "army" and "LORD of hosts." In v23 the phrase is fuller: "to war the warfare," which the lexicon gives here in its special sense of serving at the sacred tent. The Levites were exempt from military service, yet their work at the sanctuary is described in military language. Women who "ministered at the entrance to the tent of meeting" are described with the same verb (Exod 38:8; 1 Sam 2:22).
- **Kohath first (v4).** Kohath was Levi's second son, but his clan is listed before Gershon, the eldest, because they carried **"the most holy things."** Moses and Aaron came from this clan (Exod 6:18–20).
- **"Aaron and his sons shall go in and take down the veil… and cover the ark" (v5).** The Kohathites do not pack the holy things. The priests do it first, inside, and the first covering on the ark is the very curtain that hid it in the Most Holy Place. Even folded for travel, the ark stays veiled.
- **"A covering of goatskin" (v6).** The Hebrew word *tachash* is uncertain. Older versions say "badgers' skins." The lexicon calls it a kind of fine leather, perhaps from the dugong or sea cow, which lives in the Red Sea; others suggest dolphin, porpoise, or goat. Whatever it was, it was tough and weatherproof. Ezekiel 16:10 mentions sandals of the same leather.
- **"A cloth all of blue" (v6).** Here is a striking detail. Every other piece of furniture has the leather on the outside. The ark alone has the blue cloth on top, so that as the procession moved, the one object everyone could pick out was the ark under its blue. Blue was the color of the high priest's robe (Exod 28:31) and of the tassels every Israelite wore to remember the commandments (15:38–39).
- **The table, lampstand and altars (vv7–14).** The table of the bread of the Presence gets blue, then **scarlet**, then leather, and the bread stays on it even on the move (v7). The lampstand and its tools go into blue and leather on a **"carrying frame"** (v10), a pole frame carried between men. The golden incense altar gets blue and leather (v11). The bronze altar has its ashes cleared and receives **purple** (v13), the color of royalty. Colors in the tabernacle are not decoration only. Blue, purple and scarlet were costly dyes, and they marked these objects as belonging to the King.
- **What about the laver?** The bronze basin for washing is not mentioned. The Greek translation and the Samaritan Pentateuch add a line about covering it in purple. The Hebrew text, followed by the ESV, has no such line.
- **"They must not touch the holy things, lest they die" (v15).** The Kohathites carried the furnishings on poles on their shoulders (7:9), handling only the coverings and poles. The danger was real. Generations later, the ark was put on a new cart, the oxen stumbled, Uzzah put out his hand to steady it, and he died (2 Sam 6:6–7). David later admitted the mistake: "the LORD our God broke out against us, because we did not seek him according to the rule" (1 Chr 15:13), and the next time the Levites carried it on their shoulders with the poles, "as Moses had commanded" (1 Chr 15:15).
- **"Eleazar… shall have charge of the oil… the fragrant incense, the regular grain offering, and the anointing oil" (v16).** Aaron's eldest surviving son personally oversaw the supplies of worship and the whole tabernacle. The things that kept worship going day by day were not left to chance.
- **"Let not the tribe of the clans of the Kohathites be destroyed" (v18).** This is the heart of the section. The rules are given so that the Kohathites "may live and not die" (v19). The priests had a duty to protect them by appointing **"each to his task and to his burden."** No one was left to guess.
- **"They shall not go in to look on the holy things even for a moment" (v20).** The Hebrew is literally "as a swallowing," an idiom for the briefest glance, "as long as it takes to swallow." The men of Beth-shemesh later died when they looked into the ark (1 Sam 6:19–20).
- **Commentators:** Guzik sees God's freedom in putting the second son's clan first, and he takes the years from twenty-five to thirty as a training period. He notes that the Kohathites' calling was real even though it was limited: they carried what they were never allowed to see. JFB links the age of thirty to John the Baptist and Jesus, and says that after fifty the Levites still attended the tabernacle without the heavy labor (8:25–26). JFB also explains the stern rule about touching as meant to build deep reverence, and says the superintending priest would bear the guilt for any careless handling. Henry describes the men counted as those "in the prime of their time for service, betwixt thirty and fifty years old." The Tyndale notes read the coverings' colors as signs of the royalty of these objects.

#### Duties of the Gershonites (vv21–28)
- **"In serving and bearing burdens" (v24).** The Gershonites, Levi's eldest line, carried the fabrics: the curtains of the tabernacle, its outer coverings including the leather, the entrance screen, the court hangings and the cords (vv25–26). These were bulky and heavy but less holy than what Kohath carried. Chapter 7 shows they were given two wagons and four oxen to help (7:7).
- **"And they shall do all that needs to be done" (v26).** Their job included not only carrying but setting up and taking down whatever belonged to their charge.
- **"At the command of Aaron and his sons" (v27).** Even the eldest clan serves under the priests. **"Under the direction of Ithamar"** (v28), Aaron's youngest son, who had earlier kept the records of the tabernacle's materials (Exod 38:21).
- **Commentators:** Guzik draws on the military word *tsaba* in v23: even humble service, like hauling curtains, is a kind of warfare, because serving God and others involves real spiritual conflict. JFB notes that the Gershonites' load included the goats' hair covering and the entrance hangings, under Ithamar's direction.

#### Duties of the Merarites (vv29–33)
- **"The frames of the tabernacle, with its bars, pillars, and bases" (v31).** The Merarites had the heaviest load: the wooden frames overlaid with gold, the crossbars, the pillars, and the silver bases, each weighing a talent, about 75 pounds (Exod 38:27). They were given four wagons and eight oxen, twice the Gershonites' share (7:8).
- **"And you shall list by name the objects that they are required to carry" (v32).** Every peg and every cord was assigned to a named man. Nothing was lost, and everyone knew his part. The Tyndale notes compare Paul's "all things should be done decently and in order" (1 Cor 14:40).
- **Commentators:** Guzik estimates the whole tabernacle at around nineteen thousand pounds, and says naming each item showed that even the smallest pieces mattered. JFB makes the same point, that the inventory shows God values even the small details of his service.

#### Census of the Levites (vv34–49)
- **The working totals (vv34–48).** Kohath **2,750**, Gershon **2,630**, Merari **3,200**, for **8,580** in all. The numbers check exactly. JFB notes the ratio: about half the Merarites were of working age, but only about a third of the other two clans.
- **"Moses and Aaron and the chiefs of the congregation listed" (v34).** The tribal leaders who helped with the census in chapter 1 help here too. The count was public and verified.
- **"Each one with his task of serving or carrying" (v49).** The chapter's last line sums it up. No one was counted without being given a job, and no job was left without a man.
- **Commentators:** Guzik closes on interdependence. No clan could move the tabernacle alone, and he connects that to Paul's picture of the church as one body with many members, none unnecessary (1 Cor 12:12–26). Henry's outline is simple: the serviceable men "are ordered to be numbered, and their charges are given them," then "the sum total at last."

#### Pointing to Christ
The Kohathites carried the ark on their shoulders through the wilderness for forty years without ever seeing it. The holy things had to be veiled, because no one could look on them and live. In Christ the veil is gone. When Jesus died, the very curtain that wrapped the ark on the march "was torn in two, from top to bottom" (Mark 15:38), and believers now enter the holy places "by the new and living way that he opened for us through the curtain, that is, through his flesh" (Heb 10:19–20). He is himself the true meeting place of God and man, the one in whom "we have seen his glory" (John 1:14). And the careful roster of this chapter still describes his church: every member given a task and a burden, none too small to be listed by name.
