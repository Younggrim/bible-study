*Setting:* Chronicles was written after the exile, around 450–400 BC. David has chosen the site of the temple, gathered the materials and charged Solomon to build (chapter 22). Now, as an old man, he organizes the people who will serve in it. Chapters 23–27 are a set of lists: Levites (ch 23), priests (ch 24), singers (ch 25), gatekeepers, treasurers and officials (ch 26), and the army and civil leaders (ch 27). Lists like these can feel dry, but they mattered intensely to the Chronicler's first readers. The Levites, priests, singers and gatekeepers of the second temple traced their duties to these arrangements (Ezra 3:10; Neh 12:24, 45–46). The lists told them that their work was not a recent invention but rooted in David's own ordering, guided by the prophets (2 Chr 29:25). This chapter counts the Levites, lists their family heads, and explains how their work changed now that God's house would no longer move from place to place.

#### David Organizes the Levites (vv1–32)
- **"When David was old and full of days, he made Solomon his son king over Israel" (v1).** This one line summarizes what 1 Kings 1 tells at length: Adonijah's attempt to seize the throne, and Nathan and Bathsheba moving David to have Solomon anointed. "Full of days" is said of people who die after a long and complete life (Gen 25:8; 35:29). The fuller account of Solomon's public crowning comes in chapters 28–29. The chapters in between show the kingdom being set in order for the new king and the new temple.
- **"The Levites, thirty years old and upward, were numbered, and the total was 38,000 men" (v3).** Thirty was the starting age in Moses' day for the heavy work of carrying the tabernacle (Num 4:3, 23, 30). At that time the Levites of working age numbered 8,580 (Num 4:47–48), so the tribe had grown more than fourfold. Unlike the census of chapter 21, this one was for service in God's house, and no judgment followed.
- **Four kinds of work (vv4–5).** **24,000** were to "have charge of the work in the house of the LORD," the daily running of the temple. **6,000** were "officers and judges," Levites who served out in the towns as administrators and teachers of the Law (26:29–32; 2 Chr 19:8–11). **4,000** were gatekeepers (ch 26). And **4,000** were musicians, offering praise "with the instruments that I have made" (v5). David is remembered as a maker of instruments (2 Chr 7:6; 29:26–27; Neh 12:36). Amos 6:5 refers to it too, though Amos criticizes idle rich men who "like David invent for themselves instruments of music" for their parties.
- **"Divisions corresponding to the sons of Levi: Gershon, Kohath, and Merari" (v6).** Levi had three sons, and the whole tribe was organized by these three clans from the beginning (Num 3:17–37). "Divisions" means courses or shifts. With tens of thousands of Levites and one temple, they served in rotation. Luke 1:5, 8 shows the same system still working in Jesus' day, when Zechariah's priestly division was on duty.
- **The Gershonites (vv7–11).** Gershon's line runs through Ladan (also called Libni in 6:17) and Shimei. Verse 9 has a puzzle: it lists "the sons of Shimei" as heads of the houses of Ladan, while v10 gives a different list of Shimei's sons. Most scholars think a name has been miscopied in v9 (perhaps originally "the sons of Jehiel"). The names Zina (v10) and Zizah (v11) are likely the same person, spelled differently. **Jeush and Beriah "did not have many sons," so they "became counted as a single father's house" (v11).** Small families were combined so that each division had enough men to serve.
- **The Kohathites (vv12–20).** Kohath's four sons, Amram, Izhar, Hebron and Uzziel, head the clan that once carried the ark and the most holy furniture (Num 4:4–15).
- **"Aaron was set apart to dedicate the most holy things" (v13).** The Hebrew can be read two ways: Aaron was set apart "to consecrate the most holy things," or he was set apart "to be consecrated as most holy." Either way, Aaron's line alone formed the priesthood. Only they could offer sacrifices, burn incense "before the LORD," "minister to him" and "pronounce blessings in his name" (Num 6:22–27). The rest of the Levites, however distinguished, assisted them.
- **"But the sons of Moses the man of God were named among the tribe of Levi" (v14).** Moses, the greatest prophet in Israel's history, founded no dynasty. His sons, Gershom and Eliezer, were ordinary Levites, not priests. The title "man of God" is used for Moses in Deuteronomy 33:1 and Psalm 90's heading. **Shebuel** son of Gershom later oversaw the temple treasuries (26:24). Of Eliezer's son **Rehabiah**, the text says his descendants "were very many" (v17). God multiplied Moses' family through his younger son.
- **The Merarites (vv21–23).** **"Eleazar died having no sons, but only daughters; their kinsmen, the sons of Kish, married them" (v22).** This follows the rule set for the daughters of Zelophehad (Num 27:1–11; 36:6–9). When a man had no sons, his daughters inherited, and they married within the clan so that the father's name and property stayed in the family. (This Kish and Eleazar are Levites, not Saul's father or Aaron's son.)
- **"From twenty years old and upward" (v24).** The count in v3 was from age thirty. Now the age is lowered to twenty for service in the temple. The Tyndale notes observe that the starting age varied with need: twenty-five in Numbers 8:24, thirty in Numbers 4, and twenty again after the exile (Ezra 3:8; 2 Chr 31:17). The Chronicler links the change to "the last words of David" (v27), his final instructions.
- **"The LORD… has given rest to his people, and he dwells in Jerusalem forever" (v25).** This is the reason for the reorganization. "Rest" picks up the promise of 22:9, 18. The verb "dwells" (*shakan*) is the root of *mishkan*, "tabernacle." The God who once "tabernacled" in a moving tent now settles in one place.
- **"The Levites no longer need to carry the tabernacle" (v26).** For centuries the Levites' main job had been to take down, carry and set up the tabernacle (Num 4). In 15:2, 15 the Levites carried the ark one last time. Now that the ark would rest in a permanent house, they needed new work, and David gave it to them.
- **Their new duties (vv28–32).** They would "assist the sons of Aaron": care for the courts and storerooms, keep the holy things clean, and prepare the **showbread** (the twelve loaves set out before God each Sabbath, Lev 24:5–9), the flour, the wafers and the baked offerings (Lev 2). "All measures of quantity or size" (v29) means they kept the official weights and measures, which the Law required to be honest (Lev 19:35–36).
- **"To stand every morning, thanking and praising the LORD, and likewise at evening" (v30).** Praise was to match the daily rhythm of sacrifice, morning and evening (Exod 29:38–42), with more on "Sabbaths, new moons, and feast days" (v31; Num 28–29). The Chronicler's own day knew this pattern well. Psalm 92 is titled "A Song for the Sabbath," and Jewish tradition assigned a psalm to each day of the week.
- **"Keep charge of the tent of meeting and the sanctuary, and to attend the sons of Aaron, their brothers" (v32).** "Their brothers" is a gentle touch. Priests and Levites came from one family, and their different roles were meant to work together without rivalry. The old name "tent of meeting" is still used for the house of worship, linking the new temple to Moses' tabernacle.
- **Commentators:** Henry sees wisdom in organizing by families: "When those of the same family were employed together, it would engage them to love and assist one another." On the lowered age he writes, "When more work is to be done, it is pity but there should be more workmen," and he notes that "every Israelite who brought an offering might find a Levite ready to help him." He also observes, "To the spiritual man every service will yield satisfaction." JFB counts the fathers' houses as nine for Gershon, nine for Kohath and six for Merari, twenty-four in all, matching the twenty-four priestly divisions of chapter 24. It calls David "an inventor of many of the musical instruments used in the temple" and explains the lower age by the lighter work now that the tabernacle no longer traveled. Guzik draws four lessons from Aaron's priestly duties in v13, including incense as a picture of intercession and blessing others as the fruit of nearness to God. He also points out, citing 2 Chronicles 29:25, that these arrangements came with the counsel of Gad and Nathan, not from David's preferences alone.

#### Pointing to Christ
The chapter divides holy work carefully. Aaron's sons alone offer sacrifices and pronounce God's blessing, the Levites assist them, and the whole tribe serves in turns, morning and evening, year after year. Hebrews looks at that system and sees both its order and its limits: "every priest stands daily at his service, offering repeatedly the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins. But when Christ had offered for all time a single sacrifice for sins, he sat down" (Heb 10:11–12). He is the priest who does not rotate off duty, because "he always lives to make intercession" (Heb 7:24–25). The rest the Levites enjoyed when they no longer had to carry the tabernacle points to the rest Christ gives his people. And the morning and evening praise continues in a new form: through him, "let us continually offer up a sacrifice of praise to God" (Heb 13:15). In him every believer has a share in the work once divided among the tribe of Levi, as "a royal priesthood" (1 Pet 2:9).
