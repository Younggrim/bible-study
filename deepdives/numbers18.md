*Setting:* Chapter 17 ended with the people terrified: "Everyone who comes near… to the tabernacle of the LORD, shall die. Are we all to perish?" (17:13). After Korah's revolt, the fire, the earthquake and the plague, they had finally grasped that God's holiness is dangerous. Chapter 18 is God's answer to that cry. He sets out who guards the sanctuary, who may come near, and how the people who serve there will be supported. It is a chapter of laws, but every rule in it serves one purpose, stated in v5: "that there may never again be wrath on the people of Israel." Unusually, God speaks directly to Aaron here (vv1, 8, 20), rather than through Moses.

#### Duties of Priests and Levites (vv1–32)
- **"You and your sons… shall bear iniquity connected with the sanctuary" (v1).** To "bear iniquity" here means to carry responsibility for any wrong done in the holy place, and to bear the consequences. If anyone violated the sanctuary, the priests and Levites would answer for it. That is the answer to the people's fear: they will not die for coming near, because someone else has been appointed to stand guard and carry the risk. Exodus 28:38 says something similar of Aaron's golden plate, "Holy to the LORD."
- **"Bring your brothers also, the tribe of Levi… that they may join you" (v2).** "Join" is the Hebrew *lavah*, a pun on the name Levi. When Leah named her son Levi she said, "Now this time my husband will be attached (*lavah*) to me" (Gen 29:34). The tribe lives up to its name by being attached to the priests.
- **"They shall keep guard… but shall not come near to the vessels of the sanctuary or to the altar" (v3).** Two circles of service. Priests serve at the altar and inside the tent. Levites guard the tent and do the work around it, carrying and setting it up (chs 3–4), but they must not touch the holy things directly, **"lest they, and you, die."** Korah, a Levite, had tried to cross that line (16:10).
- **"No outsider shall come near you" (v4).** "Outsider" (*zar*) means anyone not authorized for that task. For the altar, that included Levites who were not priests. For the tent, it included the other tribes. The circles of holiness are carefully guarded.
- **"That there may never again be wrath on the people of Israel" (v5).** This is the heart of the chapter. The rules are not barriers meant to keep people away from God out of coldness. They are protections, so that the whole people can live near God's presence without being destroyed.
- **"They are a gift to you, given to the LORD" (v6).** The Levites were given to God in place of Israel's firstborn (3:12–13; 8:16–19), and God gives them in turn to the priests as helpers. Service in God's house is described as a gift, not a burden.
- **"I give your priesthood as a gift" (v7).** Literally "a service of gift." The priesthood was not earned, seized or inherited by right. It was a gift of grace. That is exactly what Korah missed: you cannot grab something God gives. **"Within the veil"** refers to the most holy place, where only the high priest could enter, once a year (Lev 16:2).
- **"I have given you charge of the contributions made to me" (v8).** The second part of the chapter (vv8–20) turns to how the priests will live. They had no farms and no land, so they ate from the offerings brought to God. "Contributions" (*terumah*) means what is lifted off and set aside, the portion "raised" for God from a larger whole.
- **"The most holy things" (vv9–10).** The priests' share of the grain offerings, sin offerings and guilt offerings was "most holy." Only male priests could eat it, and only in the sanctuary precincts. These offerings dealt with sin, so they were handled with special care.
- **"The wave offerings… to your sons and daughters with you" (v11).** Other gifts were "holy" rather than "most holy," and the priests' whole families could eat them at home, so long as they were ceremonially clean. The "wave offering" was the portion of a peace offering lifted or moved before the altar to show it was given to God, then handed back to the priest (Lev 7:30–34).
- **"All the best of the oil… wine… grain" (v12).** Literally "all the fat" of the oil and grain, meaning the very best. God's share was never the leftovers. The **firstfruits** of every crop (v13), the first of the harvest given in faith before the rest came in, also went to the priests.
- **"Every devoted thing in Israel shall be yours" (v14).** "Devoted" (*herem*) means given over completely to God. It could mean set apart for destruction, as in war (Josh 6:17), or given permanently to the sanctuary (Lev 27:28). Here it means the second.
- **The firstborn (vv15–18).** Every firstborn belonged to God, because he had spared Israel's firstborn on the night of Passover (Exod 13:2, 11–15). Firstborn calves, lambs and kids were sacrificed, and their meat went to the priests. But **"the firstborn of man you shall redeem"**: human sacrifice was never permitted, and firstborn sons were bought back for **five shekels** of silver at a month old (v16), the same price paid earlier for the firstborn who outnumbered the Levites (3:46–47). A shekel weighed roughly 11–12 grams (about 0.4 oz) of silver. Unclean animals such as donkeys, which could not be sacrificed, were also redeemed. Mary and Joseph presented the baby Jesus at the temple for this very reason (Luke 2:22–23).
- **"It is a covenant of salt forever" (v19).** Salt preserves food and does not decay, so a "covenant of salt" means a lasting, unbreakable agreement. In the ancient Near East, eating salt together sealed friendship and loyalty. Every grain offering was salted with "the salt of the covenant" (Lev 2:13). David's kingship is called a covenant of salt in 2 Chronicles 13:5. God binds himself to provide for his priests permanently.
- **"I am your portion and your inheritance" (v20).** This is one of the most beautiful sentences in the law. Every other tribe would receive land. Aaron would receive God. "Portion" (*heleq*) is a share of land or of booty. Later believers took this promise as their own: "The LORD is my chosen portion and my cup" (Ps 16:5); "God is the strength of my heart and my portion forever" (Ps 73:26; also Ps 142:5; Lam 3:24). It was not a demotion. It was the best inheritance of all.
- **"To the Levites I have given every tithe in Israel for an inheritance" (v21).** The third part (vv21–32) turns to the Levites. A tithe is a tenth. Abraham gave Melchizedek a tenth (Gen 14:20), and Jacob promised God a tenth (Gen 28:22). Now the tenth of Israel's produce becomes the Levites' "inheritance," in place of land. It was not charity. It was **"in return for their service"**, wages for their work.
- **"So that the people of Israel do not come near the tent of meeting, lest they bear sin and die" (v22).** The Levites stand between the people and the danger. That is why the people pay them. **"They shall bear their iniquity"** (v23): the Levites carry the responsibility for the people's approach to God.
- **"No inheritance among the people of Israel" (vv23–24).** The Levites would be given forty-eight towns scattered across the other tribes, with pastureland, but no tribal territory (35:1–8; Josh 21). Spread throughout the land, they could teach the law in every region (Deut 33:10).
- **"A tithe of the tithe" (v26).** The Levites must give a tenth of what they receive to the priests. Those who receive God's people's gifts must give too. No one in Israel was exempt from giving to God.
- **"Counted to you as though it were the grain of the threshing floor" (v27).** The Levites had no fields or vineyards, so God counts their tithe of the tithe as if it were their own harvest. Their giving is just as real as a farmer's.
- **"From each its best part is to be dedicated" (v29).** The Levites' gift must also come from the best, not the leftovers. Once they have given the best, **"the rest shall be counted to the Levites"** (v30), and they may eat it **"in any place, you and your households"** (v31). It is their honest wages, not a holy thing restricted to the sanctuary.
- **"You shall bear no sin by reason of it… But you shall not profane the holy things… lest you die" (v32).** The chapter ends where it began: holy things must be handled with care. Withholding God's portion, or treating gifts given to God as common property, is a serious offense. Malachi later calls withholding tithes robbing God (Mal 3:8–10).
- **How does this apply today?** The New Testament does not command Christians to give the Levitical tithe, since the tabernacle system has been fulfilled in Christ. But Paul applies its principle directly: "those who are employed in the temple service get their food from the temple… In the same way, the Lord commanded that those who proclaim the gospel should get their living by the gospel" (1 Cor 9:13–14). Christians differ on whether a tenth remains the standard. Many treat it as a good starting point. All agree that giving should be regular, generous, from the best, and cheerful (2 Cor 9:7).
- **Commentators:** Guzik draws the principle that God never gives authority without accountability: the priests had special privilege, and also bore special responsibility. He sees the line between priests and Levites as a call not to covet someone else's calling (1 Cor 12), and quotes G. Campbell Morgan on the priesthood as a gift of grace. On "I am your portion," Guzik applies it to every believer, now a royal priesthood (1 Pet 2:9). He argues that the New Testament stresses principles of giving more than a fixed percentage, and suggests a tenth as a starting goal rather than a ceiling. He also warns that congregations should not keep pastors poor to keep them humble. Henry says those whose portion is God should not covet worldly estates, and on the tithe of the tithe, that "ministers must be charitable out of what they receive," for "the more freely they have received the more freely they must give." JFB says the chapter reassures the people after their fear in 17:12. It calls the Levites "gifts" and the priesthood "a service of gift," explains the covenant of salt as "a perpetual ordinance" and a "pledge of fidelity," and notes that spreading the Levites among the tribes let them teach the whole nation.

#### Pointing to Christ
The people cried, "Everyone who comes near… shall die," and God answered by appointing priests who would bear the iniquity of the sanctuary so the people could live. That is the shape of the gospel. Jesus is the priest who did not just guard the holy place but bore our iniquity himself (Isa 53:11; 1 Pet 2:24), and who went "within the veil" once for all (Heb 6:19–20; 9:12). Through him, every believer is now a priest (1 Pet 2:9), and every believer can say what was promised to Aaron: "I am your portion." Firstborn sons were redeemed for five shekels of silver. We were "ransomed… not with perishable things such as silver or gold, but with the precious blood of Christ" (1 Pet 1:18–19).
