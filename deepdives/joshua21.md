*Setting:* The land has been divided among the tribes (chs 13–19), and the cities of refuge have been named (ch 20). One tribe is still waiting: Levi. From the start God had said the Levites would receive no territory, because "the LORD God of Israel is their inheritance" (13:33; Num 18:20–24). Instead, Moses had commanded that the other tribes give them towns to live in, with grazing land around each one (Num 35:1–8). Joshua 21 records how that was done, at Shiloh where the tabernacle now stood (18:1). It is mostly a list of forty-eight towns, but it ends with three of the most important verses in the book: a declaration that God kept every promise he made.

#### Cities and Pasturelands Allotted to Levi (vv1–42)
- **"The heads of the fathers' houses of the Levites came" (v1).** The Levites do not wait to be remembered. Like Caleb (14:6–12) and the daughters of Zelophehad (17:3–4), they come forward and claim what God had promised. They come to the same three authorities who handled every allotment: Eleazar the priest, Joshua, and the tribal heads.
- **"The LORD commanded through Moses" (v2).** Their claim rests on a word already given (Num 35:2). The Levites were supported by the tithes (Num 18:21), but they still needed homes and a place to keep animals. They received the last allotment of all, after every other tribe was settled.
- **"Cities to dwell in, along with their pasturelands" (v2).** The Hebrew *migrash* means the open common-land around a town, used for grazing. Numbers 35:4–5 sets it at a thousand cubits (about 1,500 feet) out from the wall, inside a square two thousand cubits on each side. Leviticus 25:34 forbids it ever being sold. The word appears with nearly every town in the list, which gives the chapter the feel of a legal deed.
- **"By command of the LORD… by lot" (vv3–4, 8).** The Levites did not choose their own towns, and the tribes did not choose which towns to part with. The lot left the decision with God (Prov 16:33). Numbers 35:8 asked that larger tribes give more and smaller tribes fewer, and the list mostly follows that: four towns from most tribes, but nine from Judah and Simeon together.
- **The four groups (vv4–7).** Levi had three sons, Kohath, Gershon and Merari (Num 3:17). Aaron came from Kohath, so the Kohathites are split in two: Aaron's descendants, the priests, receive 13 towns (v4), and the rest of Kohath receive 10 (v5). Gershon receives 13 in the north (v6), and Merari 12, mostly east of the Jordan and in Zebulun (v7). In the wilderness these clans carried different parts of the tabernacle (Num 4). Now they carry God's law to different parts of the land.
- **"The lot fell to them first" (v10).** The priests' towns came first and lay in Judah, Simeon and Benjamin, close to where Jerusalem and the temple would later stand. No one at Shiloh could have planned that. JFB points out that it proved wise centuries later, when the kingdom split under Rehoboam and the priests were within the territories of Judah and Benjamin, the southern kingdom that kept the temple (2 Chr 11:13–14).
- **Hebron and Caleb (vv11–12).** Hebron went to the priests as a city of refuge (20:7), but "the fields of the city and its villages" stayed with Caleb, who had been promised Hebron long before (14:13–14). Both promises are kept. The priests got the town and its pasture, and Caleb kept the farmland and outlying villages.
- **Familiar names among the priests' towns (vv13–18).** Several turn up later. **Beth-shemesh** is where the ark came back from the Philistines (1 Sam 6:13–15). **Gibeon**, the home of the Gibeonites who tricked Israel (ch 9), later held the tabernacle (1 Chr 16:39). **Anathoth** was the hometown of the priest Abiathar (1 Kings 2:26) and of the prophet Jeremiah, "of the priests who were in Anathoth" (Jer 1:1).
- **Towns not yet taken (vv21, 25).** **Gezer** and **Taanach** are on the list, though the tribes had not driven out the Canaanites living there (16:10; 17:11–12; Judg 1:27, 29). Gezer stayed Canaanite until an Egyptian pharaoh captured it and gave it to Solomon as a wedding gift (1 Kings 9:16). Some of these grants were a title deed waiting to be claimed, which is a theme the whole second half of Joshua keeps raising.
- **Six cities of refuge among the forty-eight (vv13, 21, 27, 32, 36–38).** Hebron, Shechem, Golan, Kedesh, Bezer and Ramoth-gilead, the six of chapter 20, are all Levitical towns, just as Numbers 35:6 required. People whose lives hung on careful judgment were placed among the men who taught the law.
- **Two verses missing from some Bibles (vv36–37).** The verses giving Reuben's four towns are missing from the Leningrad Codex, the oldest complete Hebrew Bible, and from some printed Hebrew editions, so a few older translations leave them out. They are found in many other Hebrew manuscripts and the Greek Septuagint, and the same towns appear in 1 Chronicles 6:78–79. The ESV includes them, and the total of forty-eight needs them.
- **The parallel list.** 1 Chronicles 6:54–81 repeats this list with a number of spelling differences and a few different towns, the kind of variation that comes from centuries of copying and changing place names. Most scholars think the list in Joshua is old. Some date it to the time of the judges, others to the early monarchy, when towns like Gezer were finally in Israel's hands.
- **Why scatter the Levites?** Jacob had cursed Levi's violence: "I will divide them in Jacob and scatter them in Israel" (Gen 49:7). The scattering happens, but it is no longer a punishment. When Levi stood with Moses against the golden calf (Exod 32:26–29), the tribe was set apart for God. Now the same scattering puts a teacher of God's law within reach of every tribe (Deut 33:10; Mal 2:7).
- **"Forty-eight cities with their pasturelands" (vv41–42).** Forty-eight towns among more than four hundred listed in chapters 15–19 is a small share, but every tribe gave some. JFB explains that the number seems large only because every Levitical town is named, including small places. Some Levites also lived outside these towns. The Levite of Judges 17:7 lived in Bethlehem, and the priests of 1 Samuel 21–22 were at Nob.
- **Commentators:** Matthew Henry notes that the Levites were "not suffered to live all together in any one part of the country," so each tribe had teachers near at hand: "They were all God's people, and therefore they all had Levites among them." He adds that Hebron later became "the city in which David began his reign." Guzik makes the same point about Genesis 49, setting Simeon beside Levi: Simeon's scattering swallowed it up inside Judah, while Levi's scattering spread blessing across Israel. He also notices that the Levites received their towns last, a picture of servants who let others go first, and he applies the chapter to believers, who are meant to be spread through the world as witnesses rather than gathered into a separate enclave. JFB adds the point about Rehoboam and the priests' towns.

#### The LORD Gives Israel Rest (vv43–45)
- **"Thus the LORD gave to Israel all the land that he swore to give to their fathers" (v43).** The promise goes back to Abraham: "To your offspring I will give this land" (Gen 12:7; 15:18–21), repeated to Isaac (Gen 26:3) and Jacob (Gen 28:13). After more than six hundred years, slavery in Egypt and forty years in the wilderness, it has come true. The subject of the sentence is God, not Israel. Israel fought, but "the LORD gave."
- **"They took possession of it, and they settled there" (v43).** Both sides are stated. God gave, and Israel took and settled. The land was a gift, but it still had to be lived in.
- **"The LORD gave them rest on every side" (v44).** "Rest" (*nuach*) is a key word in Joshua and Deuteronomy. Moses had promised that God would give Israel "rest from all your enemies around" (Deut 12:10), and Joshua told the eastern tribes the same (1:13–15). Rest means a settled life without war, and in Deuteronomy 12:10–11 it is also the condition for the place God would choose for his worship.
- **"Not one of all their enemies had withstood them" (v44).** This sits in tension with other verses. The book has already admitted that Canaanites remained in Jerusalem, Gezer and the valleys (15:63; 16:10; 17:12–13), Joshua will speak of "nations that remain" (23:4–7), and Judges 1 lists many cities not taken. The two kinds of statement are not a contradiction. No enemy army could stand against Israel when it fought, and the power of Canaan was broken. But God had said he would drive them out "little by little" (Exod 23:29–30; Deut 7:22), and Israel failed to finish the job. God's part was complete. Israel's was not.
- **"Not one word of all the good promises… had failed; all came to pass" (v45).** Literally "not a word fell from every good word." The word for "promise" is just *dabar*, "word." A promise from God is simply his word, and his word does not fall to the ground. Joshua repeats it at the end of his life (23:14), and Solomon echoes it at the dedication of the temple: "Not one word has failed of all his good promise" (1 Kings 8:56).
- **Commentators:** Henry calls these verses "a receipt entered in full of all that God had promised to his people Israel," and he sees in the earthly Canaan a pledge of the "heavenly Canaan," just as sure because it rests on "the promise of him that cannot lie." On the Canaanites who remained, he says they were left by God's own plan of gradual conquest, and grew strong later only because of Israel's cowardice and toleration of idols. In doing so Israel "put thorns into their own beds and their own eyes." Guzik takes the same line: whatever Israel failed to possess was Israel's failure, not a gap in God's provision, and the same is true of believers who fall short of what God has given them. He also connects rest with Passover, once eaten in haste by travelers and now eaten by a people at home. JFB simply sets v43 beside God's oath to the fathers. All three agree that vv43–45 sum up the book, and none of them treats them as exaggeration.

#### Pointing to Christ
The Levites had no land because God was their portion, and Peter calls the church "a royal priesthood" (1 Pet 2:9), scattered like Levi among the nations so that every place has someone to make God known. Joshua's rest was real, but it did not last. Hebrews 4:8 says that "if Joshua had given them rest, God would not have spoken of another day later on." The rest of Joshua 21 points ahead to the rest Jesus gives (Matt 11:28) and to the Sabbath rest that "remains for the people of God" (Heb 4:9). And the claim of v45 is made complete in Christ: "all the promises of God find their Yes in him" (2 Cor 1:20).
