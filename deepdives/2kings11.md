*Setting:* 841–835 BC, in Jerusalem. The scene shifts from the north back to Judah. In the year of Jehu's coup, Judah lost its king Ahaziah (9:27) and then forty-two princes of the royal family (10:13–14), and earlier Philistine and Arab raiders had carried off his older brothers (2 Chr 21:17; 22:1). Before that, Ahaziah's father Jehoram had killed all his own brothers (2 Chr 21:4). David's royal line, which God promised would last forever (2 Sam 7:16), is down to a handful of boys. Their grandmother is Athaliah, daughter of Ahab and Jezebel, who married into Judah's royal house as part of Jehoshaphat's alliance with Ahab (8:18, 26). Her parents, her brother Joram and her son are now all dead. This chapter tells how the lamp of David (8:19) came within one small child of going out.

#### Athaliah Reigns in Judah (vv1–3)
- **"When Athaliah the mother of Ahaziah saw that her son was dead, she arose and destroyed all the royal family" (v1).** "The royal family" is literally "all the seed of the kingdom." She is killing her own grandsons. Her motives can be guessed: she wanted power for herself, her Baal religion needed protecting now that Jehu had wiped it out in the north, and a grandson on the throne would mean a regent and a mother-in-law out of power. Whatever she intended, the effect was an attack on the promise to David. If David's line ended, so did the hope of the Messiah.
- **"Jehosheba, the daughter of King Joram, sister of Ahaziah" (v2).** "King Joram" here is Jehoram of Judah. Jehosheba was probably the daughter of another wife, making her Ahaziah's half-sister and not Athaliah's own child. Chronicles adds the key detail: she was the wife of Jehoiada the priest (2 Chr 22:11). A princess married to the high priest was in exactly the right place.
- **"Took Joash the son of Ahaziah and stole him away from among the king's sons who were being put to death" (v2).** The word "stole" fits the danger. She takes him in the middle of the slaughter. Joash was a baby, probably under a year old (he is seven in v21, after six years of hiding). His mother was Zibiah of Beersheba (12:1), perhaps a lesser wife, which may help explain how he was overlooked.
- **"She put him and his nurse in a bedroom" (v2).** The word means a room where bedding was stored, more like a linen closet than a bedroom. A wet nurse would be needed for an infant.
- **"He remained with her six years, hidden in the house of the LORD" (v3).** The temple had side rooms and storerooms built around it (1 Kgs 6:5–6). Athaliah worshiped Baal and would hardly visit. The safest place for David's heir was God's house. For six years Judah is ruled by the only person not descended from David who ever sat on its throne between David and the exile.
- **Commentators:** Henry sees God's providence in the marriage of a princess to the high priest, and treats the hidden child as a picture of God keeping a remnant when it seems almost gone. He also observes, "Happy the men of inferior birth, who live below envy and emulation!" JFB says Athaliah acted partly to keep David's family from outliving hers but "chiefly from personal ambition to rule, and a desire to establish the worship of Baal." Guzik quotes Adam Clarke, "How dreadful is the lust of reigning!" and stresses that God always has a Jehosheba ready, a little-known person whose courage keeps his purposes alive.

#### Joash Anointed King in Judah (vv4–20)
- **"In the seventh year Jehoiada sent and brought the captains of the Carites and of the guards" (v4).** Jehoiada was the chief priest. The Carites were probably foreign mercenaries serving as royal bodyguards, perhaps from Caria in southwest Asia Minor, or related to the Cherethites who guarded David (2 Sam 20:23). "Guards" is literally "runners," the men who ran before the king's chariot. These are the palace troops, the very men who serve Athaliah.
- **"He made a covenant with them and put them under oath… and he showed them the king's son" (v4).** He binds them first, then reveals the secret. Chronicles adds that the Levites and the heads of families from all Judah's towns were brought in too (2 Chr 23:1–3). It was a revolution organized from the temple.
- **"Those who come off duty on the Sabbath… those who come on duty" (vv5–9).** The guards worked in rotating shifts that changed on the Sabbath. Jehoiada's plan was to keep both shifts, the ones leaving and the ones arriving, so that he doubled the force without anyone noticing large troop movements. The exact placement of the three groups at the palace, "the gate Sur" and "the gate behind the guards" is hard to reconstruct, and Chronicles uses slightly different names (2 Chr 23:4–5). The point is clear: every approach is covered, and the boy is surrounded by armed men "when he goes out and when he comes in" (v8).
- **"The spears and shields that had been King David's" (v10).** These were probably trophies David dedicated to the LORD (2 Sam 8:7–11), kept in the temple for over a century. The son of David is crowned by men holding David's weapons. Nothing could say more clearly that this is the restoration of David's house.
- **"He brought out the king's son and put the crown on him and gave him the testimony" (v12).** "The testimony" (*'edut*) is God's law, the covenant document. The law said the king must keep a copy of it and read it all his life (Deut 17:18–20). The child is handed the crown and the Scripture in one act. Then **"they proclaimed him king and anointed him, and they clapped their hands and said, 'Long live the king!'"**, the same acclamation as at Solomon's coronation (1 Kgs 1:39).
- **"When Athaliah heard the noise of the guard and of the people" (v13).** She hurries into the temple courts alone. She sees **"the king standing by the pillar, according to the custom"** (v14). This may be one of the two great bronze pillars at the temple's entrance, Jachin and Boaz (1 Kgs 7:21), or a raised platform. Either way, it was the place a king of Judah stood at his public installation (compare 23:3, Josiah).
- **"Athaliah tore her clothes and cried, 'Treason! Treason!'" (v14).** The word is "conspiracy." It is the same word used of Jehu's coup (9:14). The woman who seized the throne by murdering children cries "conspiracy" at the boy she failed to kill. The irony is complete.
- **"Let her not be put to death in the house of the LORD" (v15).** Jehoiada will not have the temple defiled with her blood. She is marched out between the ranks, and anyone who follows her is to die.
- **"She went through the horses' entrance to the king's house, and there she was put to death" (v16).** Her mother Jezebel died trampled by horses (9:33). Athaliah dies at the palace's horse gate. With her, the last of the house of Ahab in power is gone.
- **"Jehoiada made a covenant between the LORD and the king and people, that they should be the LORD's people" (v17).** This is a renewal of the covenant, like Joshua's at Shechem (Josh 24) and Josiah's later (23:1–3). There are two covenants here: one vertical, between God and his people, and one horizontal, between king and people. The king is bound to God's law, and the people to the king. In the ancient world a king was often answerable to no one. In Israel he was answerable to the LORD and his covenant.
- **"All the people of the land went to the house of Baal and tore it down" (v18).** Athaliah had brought her mother's religion to Jerusalem, with its own temple and priest. Chronicles adds that her sons had broken into the LORD's temple and used its holy things for Baal (2 Chr 24:7). The people smash the altars and images and kill **Mattan the priest of Baal** before his altars. As Jehu had done in Samaria, Judah does in Jerusalem, but here it is done openly, after a covenant, led by the priest and the people together.
- **"The priest posted watchmen over the house of the LORD" (v18).** Chronicles says he restored the Levites' and priests' order of service according to David's arrangements (2 Chr 23:18–19).
- **"He took his seat on the throne of the kings" (v19).** A procession brings the seven-year-old from the temple down to the palace through the guards' gate. The throne of David has a son of David on it again.
- **"All the people of the land rejoiced, and the city was quiet" (v20).** "The people of the land" were the landowning citizens of Judah, who often stepped in when the royal succession was in danger (21:24; 23:30). The writer contrasts two moods: the countryside rejoices, and the capital, where Athaliah's party had been strongest, is quiet.
- **Commentators:** Henry: "Those that are themselves most guilty are commonly most forward to reproach others." On the covenant he writes, "Covenants are of use both to remind us of and to bind us to those duties which are already binding on us," and he reads the second covenant as setting limits on royal power. JFB says Joash "possessed an inherent right" to the throne that no usurper could take away, and that Athaliah was taken out "that the sacred place might not be stained with human blood." JFB does not comment on the Carites, the Sabbath arrangements or the testimony. Guzik calls the coup a godly "treason" against a murderous usurper. Following Wiseman, he notes that British coronations still include presenting the monarch with a Bible.

#### Jehoash Reigns in Judah (v21)
- **"Jehoash was seven years old when he began to reign" (v21).** Jehoash and Joash are two forms of the same name, meaning "the LORD has given." In the Hebrew Bible this verse is 12:1, the opening of the formal account of his reign. A boy of seven could not rule, so in practice Jehoiada governed as his guardian, and for as long as Jehoiada lived, Joash did what was right (12:2). Whether he would remain faithful after his protector died is the question chapter 12 takes up.

#### Pointing to Christ
Once again a wicked ruler tries to wipe out the royal line, and once again one child is hidden and saved. Pharaoh tried it with Israel's sons, and Moses was hidden. Herod tried it in Bethlehem, and Jesus was carried to Egypt (Matt 2:13–16). Behind each attempt the Bible sees the old enemy trying to destroy the promised seed (Gen 3:15; Rev 12:4–5). God's promise to David hung by a single thread for six years, and the thread held. Joash was crowned with the law in his hand. The true Son of David would not only receive the law but keep it perfectly, and he too was hidden for a while in obscurity before he was revealed as King.
