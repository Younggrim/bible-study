*Setting:* Paul's third missionary journey, about AD 52–55. Ephesus was the capital of the Roman province of Asia (western Turkey today), a city of perhaps 200,000, a major port, and home of the famous Temple of Artemis. Back in 16:6 the Spirit kept Paul from preaching in Asia. Now the door swings wide open, and he stays here longer than anywhere else on his journeys, about three years in all (20:31).

#### Paul in Ephesus (vv1–10)
- **"While Apollos was at Corinth" (v1).** Apollos waters what Paul planted (1 Cor 3:6). Different men, one work, and no rivalry on Paul's side.
- **"Some disciples" (v1).** About twelve men stand where Apollos stood in 18:25. They know John's baptism of repentance, but not the full story of Jesus' death and resurrection or the Spirit he poured out.
- **"Did you receive the Holy Spirit when you believed?" (v2).** Paul expects real believers to have the Spirit, and he senses something is missing. Their answer probably means "we haven't heard that the Spirit has been *given*", not that they had never heard of the Spirit at all. John himself preached about the Spirit (Luke 3:16). John 7:39 uses the same idiom: "the Spirit had not been given."
- **"Into what then were you baptized?" (v3).** Paul goes straight from the Spirit to baptism. In Acts, faith in Jesus, baptism in his name and receiving the Spirit belong together (2:38).
- **"Believe in the one who was to come after him, that is, Jesus" (v4).** John's ministry was a signpost. Paul shows them where it points.
- **Baptized, hands laid on, tongues and prophecy (vv5–6).** This is the only rebaptism in the New Testament. John's baptism looked forward to Christ, while Christian baptism is into him. Luke shows the Spirit coming visibly at key frontier moments: Jews (ch 2), Samaritans (ch 8), Gentiles (ch 10), and now leftover followers of John. Each one marks a group fully joined to the one church.
- **Readings differ.** Pentecostal and charismatic readers see a second experience after conversion here. Most Reformed and Baptist readers see incomplete believers made complete in one step. Both take the text seriously. Plainly, these men's faith was missing something, and Paul filled it in.
- **"For three months spoke boldly… about the kingdom of God" (v8).** This is Paul's longest recorded synagogue stay before opposition came.
- **"Speaking evil of the Way" (v9).** "The Way" was an early name for the faith (9:2). Paul doesn't fight for the building. He takes the disciples and moves.
- **"The hall of Tyrannus" (v9).** A lecture hall, probably rented from a teacher. One early manuscript tradition (Codex Bezae) adds "from the fifth hour to the tenth," 11 a.m. to 4 p.m., the hot midday break. Most scholars think that is a scribe's addition, but it fits: Paul made tents in the morning (20:34) and taught in the afternoon.
- **"All the residents of Asia heard" (v10).** Paul did not visit every town himself. He trained people who went out. Epaphras probably planted Colossae this way (Col 1:7), and the seven churches of Revelation 2–3 are all in this region.
- **Commentators:** Guzik and Matthew Henry both stress how incomplete these men's faith was, and Guzik presses the point that God always means to take believers deeper. Henry: "Let us often consider unto what we were baptized, that we may live up to our baptism." JFB reads "some were hardened" (v9) as implying many others believed, and suggests Tyrannus was a converted teacher of rhetoric. Guzik ties v10 to Ephesians 4:11–12, where leaders equip the saints for the work of ministry.

#### The Sons of Sceva (vv11–20)
- **"Extraordinary miracles" (v11).** Literally "miracles not of the ordinary kind." Luke has already told many miracle stories, and even he flags these as unusual. Note that it says *God* was doing them, "by the hands of Paul."
- **Handkerchiefs and aprons (v12).** These were probably Paul's sweat-rags and work aprons from his leather trade. Compare Peter's shadow (5:15) and the woman who touched Jesus' garment (Luke 8:44). Ephesus was a superstitious city, and God met people where they were. Luke reports it as something God did then, not a method to market later.
- **"I adjure you by the Jesus whom Paul proclaims" (v13).** Traveling exorcists were common, and Jewish ones had a reputation for powerful names. Magical papyri from this era really do mix "Jesus" into their spells. To these men Jesus' name was one more formula.
- **"Seven sons of a Jewish high priest named Sceva" (v14).** No high priest named Sceva appears in Jewish records. He was probably from a priestly family, or the title was self-promotion, which would fit men in the magic trade.
- **"Jesus I know, and Paul I recognize, but who are you?" (v15).** Two different Greek verbs: *ginōskō* for Jesus and *epistamai* for Paul. The demon knows exactly who carries authority. These men are borrowing a name they do not belong to.
- **"Fled out of that house naked and wounded" (v16).** The irony is sharp: seven against one, and they lose everything, clothes included. Jesus' name is not a magic word. It belongs to people who belong to him (Matt 7:22–23).
- **"The name of the Lord Jesus was extolled" (v17).** Their humiliation turned into honor for Christ.
- **"Confessing and divulging their practices" (v18).** These are *believers* coming clean about secret practices they had not yet let go of. Magicians believed a spell lost its power once it was revealed, so confessing it meant giving it up.
- **Burning the books (v19).** The "Ephesian letters" (*Ephesia grammata*) were famous magic formulas across the ancient world. **Fifty thousand pieces of silver** means drachmas. A drachma was roughly a day's wage, so this was something like 50,000 days' wages. No one sold the books to recover the money. They destroyed them so no one else could use them.
- **"The word of the Lord continued to increase and prevail mightily" (v20).** This is one of Luke's progress reports (6:7; 12:24). The gospel prevails over the occult.
- **Commentators:** All three treat Sceva's sons as men using Jesus' name without knowing Jesus. Henry calls them "vagabond Jews" who went town to town "to get money by conjuring." JFB likens the scene to Baal's prophets shamed on Carmel (1 Kings 18). JFB reads v15 as "Paul I know *intimately*," but the lexicon gives *epistamai* only as "know, understand." The ESV's "recognize" is fair, and the difference between the verbs should not be pressed. On the silver, Guzik puts a drachma at about a quarter of a day's wage, lower than most estimates, though either way it was a fortune. His application is direct: get rid of whatever ties you to darkness, don't just stop using it.

#### A Riot at Ephesus (vv21–41)
- **"Resolved in the Spirit… I must also see Rome" (v21).** This is the hinge of Acts. From here the book heads to Jerusalem and then Rome. Paul will see Rome, but in chains (ch 28).
- **Timothy and Erastus sent ahead (v22).** Paul writes 1 Corinthians around this time (1 Cor 16:8: "I will stay in Ephesus until Pentecost"). He also mentions fighting "beasts at Ephesus" (1 Cor 15:32), probably a figure for danger like this.
- **Demetrius the silversmith (vv24–27).** He made miniature silver shrines of Artemis for pilgrims, religious souvenirs. His speech starts with money ("from this business we have our wealth") and then dresses it in piety ("the great goddess… may be deposed"). Paul's message that "gods made with hands are not gods" (v26) was hurting sales.
- **Artemis of the Ephesians.** She was not the slim Greek huntress but a local mother and fertility goddess, carved covered in egg-like or breast-like shapes. Her temple was four times the size of the Parthenon and one of the Seven Wonders of the ancient world. It also served as the region's bank.
- **"Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!" (vv28, 34).** This was a real festival chant, shouted here for **two hours**.
- **The theater (v29).** It seated about 24,000, and its ruins still stand. **Gaius and Aristarchus** are seized. Aristarchus later sails with Paul to Rome (27:2) and shares his imprisonment (Col 4:10).
- **"The disciples would not let him" (v30).** Paul wants to go in, but wise friends hold him back, and so do **the Asiarchs** (v31). These were wealthy provincial officials who funded games and the emperor's cult. Paul had friends in high places, so the faith was not only for the poor.
- **"Most of them did not know why they had come together" (v32).** Luke's dry humor: a mob shouting for hours without knowing why.
- **Alexander (vv33–34).** The Jewish community puts him forward, probably to say "we're not with Paul." It backfires, because Jews refused idols too. Some identify him with "Alexander the coppersmith" (2 Tim 4:14), but that is only a guess.
- **The town clerk (vv35–41).** He was the city's chief local official and its link to Rome. He calls Ephesus "temple keeper" (*neōkoros*) of Artemis and of the "sacred stone that fell from the sky," possibly a meteorite. He then clears Paul's men: neither temple-robbers nor blasphemers (v37). The courts are open for complaints (v38), and Rome may punish an illegal assembly (v40).
- **"He dismissed the assembly" (v41).** The word is *ekklēsia*, the same word as "church." One *ekklēsia* in the theater shouts for Artemis while another quietly grows. Again and again in Acts, Roman officials find Christians innocent (Gallio in 18:14–16, and now here).
- **Commentators:** Henry calls Paul "a man of vast designs for God." He also passes on Lightfoot's suggestion that Emperor Claudius's death (AD 54) opened the way to Rome, which is a scholar's guess, not the text. Guzik argues Paul never ran a campaign against Artemis. He preached Christ, and the idol trade collapsed as a side effect. JFB explains the shrines as souvenirs kept as charms and notes how prized the Asiarchs' office was. The Tyndale notes add that Strabo records temples of Artemis as far away as Spain and France.

#### Pointing to Christ
Half-formed faith is completed, a borrowed name fails, hidden sins are confessed, and an idol economy shakes. Underneath it all, the name of Jesus carries power only for those who actually belong to him. Paul later writes to this same church that "we do not wrestle against flesh and blood" (Eph 6:12).
