*Setting:* This is the second-shortest book in the Bible, a single papyrus sheet. It comes from "the elder," whom the early church identified as the apostle John, probably writing from Ephesus late in the first century. Traveling teachers moved from church to church, often staying in believers' homes. Some of them were spreading the error John fought in 1 John: that Jesus Christ did not truly come "in the flesh." 2 John holds two things together that are easily pulled apart, **truth and love**. "Truth" appears five times in the first four verses.

#### Greeting (vv1–3)
- **"The elder to the elect lady and her children" (v1).** Who is the "elect lady" (*eklektē kyria*)? There are two main views:
  - **A local church and its members.** This is the majority view today. Verse 13's "children of your elect sister" would then be the members of John's own church. Peter similarly calls a church "she who is at Babylon, who is likewise chosen" (1 Pet 5:13). The shifts between "you" singular and plural fit this reading.
  - **An actual Christian woman and her family.** *Kyria* could even be a name, "Kyria," and this was the older common view. Some suggest John avoided naming a church or a person because of persecution.
  - Either way, the instructions apply to every church and every household.
- **"Whom I love in truth, and not only I, but also all who know the truth" (v1).** Christian love isn't a private fondness. It is shared by everyone who shares the truth. "In truth" means both "truly" and "within the truth" of the gospel.
- **"Because of the truth that abides in us and will be with us forever" (v2).** Truth here is not just a set of facts. It is almost personal. Jesus is the truth (John 14:6), and the Spirit of truth dwells in believers forever (John 14:16–17).
- **"Grace, mercy, and peace will be with us… in truth and love" (v3).** Unlike most greetings, this one is a promise: "will be." "Jesus Christ the Father's Son" is an unusually full title, and it anticipates the false teaching about Christ in v7.
- **Commentators:** JFB notes that in a familiar letter John uses the humbler title "elder" rather than "apostle," as Peter did (1 Pet 5:1). It reports Bengel's suggestion that *Kyria* is a proper name, and quotes Alford: "The communion of love is as wide as the communion of faith." Henry takes the lady as "a noble Christian matron" and her children, and observes that "religion turns compliments into real expressions of respect and love." Guzik allows both readings, a woman or a congregation, and suggests the anonymity may reflect the danger of persecution.

#### Truth and Love (vv4–6)
- **"I rejoiced greatly to find some of your children walking in the truth" (v4).** "Some" may hint that not all were. "Walking" means daily living, not just believing (3 John 3–4).
- **"Not as though I were writing you a new commandment, but the one we have had from the beginning—that we love one another" (v5).** Jesus called it "a new commandment" (John 13:34). By now it was the oldest teaching the church had (1 John 2:7–8). John never got tired of repeating it. Early tradition says that as a very old man in Ephesus he was carried into church and said only, "Little children, love one another."
- **"And this is love, that we walk according to his commandments" (v6).** John defines love by obedience, not just feeling (John 14:15; 1 John 5:3). Then he turns it around: the commandment is to walk in love. Love and obedience define each other. Love without truth turns into sentiment. Truth without love turns into harshness.
- **Commentators:** Guzik stresses that love shows itself in obedience to God's commands, not sentiment alone. He sees John's joy as a pastor's joy. Henry notes that John "condescends to beseech" rather than command, out of "apostolical meekness." He says the duty of mutual love "must every where attend Christianity." JFB says love "performs both tables of the law, and is the end of the law and the Gospel alike."

#### Watch for Deceivers (vv7–11)
- **"Many deceivers have gone out into the world, those who do not confess the coming of Jesus Christ in the flesh" (v7).** This is the error John fought in 1 John 4:2–3. Some taught that the divine Christ only *seemed* to have a body, an idea later called Docetism, from Greek *dokeō*, "to seem". Others taught that the Christ-spirit came on the man Jesus at his baptism and left him before the cross. Either way, God hadn't truly become human, and there was no real death for sin. "Coming… in the flesh" is a present participle. It may mean the incarnation is a permanent fact: he came and remains fully human. **"Such a one is the deceiver and the antichrist."** "Antichrist" in John's letters means anyone who opposes or replaces the true Christ (1 John 2:18, 22), whatever is still to come.
- **"Watch yourselves, so that you may not lose what we have worked for, but may win a full reward" (v8).** Believers can lose ground and reward by drifting into error, though not by that their salvation (1 Cor 3:12–15). Some manuscripts read "what you have worked for."
- **"Everyone who goes on ahead and does not abide in the teaching of Christ, does not have God" (v9).** "Goes on ahead" (*proagō*) means runs ahead. The false teachers claimed to be "progressive," with deeper knowledge that moved beyond the apostles. John says that moving beyond Christ means losing God. **"Whoever abides in the teaching has both the Father and the Son."** You can't have the Father while rejecting the Son (1 John 2:23).
- **"Do not receive him into your house or give him any greeting" (v10).** Churches met in homes (Rom 16:5; Col 4:15), and traveling teachers depended on hospitality. John is forbidding giving false teachers a platform and a base: hosting them, funding them, or greeting them as fellow workers. "Greeting" (*chairein*) was the standard welcome, a public blessing on their mission. **"Whoever greets him takes part in his wicked works" (v11).** Supporting a mission makes you a partner in it, for good (3 John 8) or for evil. This is about teachers actively spreading denial of the incarnation. It is not about being unkind to anyone who holds a mistaken belief, or refusing ordinary courtesy to unbelievers.
- **Commentators:** Guzik limits the command to those actively spreading false teaching about essential truths, not everyone with a mistaken belief. He suggests it concerns official welcome into the church more than private courtesy. He warns that defending truth must not become unloving. JFB notes the oldest manuscripts read "have gone forth," that is, from the church, and that the second "of Christ" in v9 is missing from them. On v10 it reads the visitor as coming "as a teacher or brother." Henry's concise commentary balances the command: believers must "show kindness and a good spirit to those who differ from us in lesser matters," while holding firmly to "the all-important doctrines of Christ's person, atonement, and holy salvation." The Tyndale notes say the false teachers include Docetists. They take "the antichrist" as a strong title that marks the seriousness of the error.

#### Final Greetings (vv12–13)
- **"I would rather not use paper and ink… talk face to face, so that our joy may be complete" (v12).** Paper is papyrus. "Face to face" is literally "mouth to mouth." Christian joy is completed in fellowship, not in isolation (1 John 1:4).
- **"The children of your elect sister greet you" (v13).** If the "lady" is a church, this is a greeting from John's own congregation to a sister church. If she is a woman, it is from her nephews and nieces.
- **Commentators:** Henry, reading the lady as a person, says "grace was abundant towards this family; here are two elect sisters, and probably their elect children." Guzik notes the closing shows believers belong to a wider family.

#### Pointing to Christ
2 John stands guard over the incarnation. Everything depends on Jesus Christ truly "coming in the flesh." Only a Savior who truly took on our humanity could truly die in our place and rise in a real body. That truth isn't a cold doctrine. It is the ground of love, since "we love because he first loved us" (1 John 4:19). Truth without love, or love without truth, is not the Jesus who is "full of grace and truth" (John 1:14).
