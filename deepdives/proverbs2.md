*Setting:* The second of the father's talks in Proverbs 1–9 (on authorship and the book's setting, see Proverbs 1). In chapter 1 the father warned his son against a violent gang, and Wisdom called out in the streets for the simple to turn. Chapter 2 answers the question that raises: how does someone actually get wisdom, and what will it do for them? In Hebrew the whole chapter is one long, carefully built sentence: "if… then… then… so." It also has twenty-two verses, the number of letters in the Hebrew alphabet. It is not an acrostic, but many scholars think the number was chosen on purpose to suggest a complete lesson, and the first half turns on lines that begin with the first letter, *aleph* (vv5, 9), and the second half on lines that begin with *lamed*, the middle letter (vv12, 16, 20). The ESV gives the chapter one heading.

#### The Value of Wisdom (vv1–22)
- **"My son, if you receive my words" (v1).** The chapter begins with three "if" clauses (vv1, 3, 4), and each one rises in intensity. Receiving and treasuring the father's words is the first step. "Treasure up" (*tsaphan*) means to store something away carefully, hidden for safekeeping. The same verb appears in "I have stored up your word in my heart" (Ps 119:11).
- **"Making your ear attentive… inclining your heart" (v2).** Ear and heart work together. Hearing is not enough without the inner will bending toward what is heard. In Hebrew thought the heart is not mainly the emotions but the center of thinking, choosing and desire.
- **"If you call out for insight and raise your voice" (v3).** In chapter 1 Wisdom called out in the streets (1:20–21). Now the son is told to call out for her. Wisdom is gained by asking, and the Bible's model is Solomon's own prayer (1 Kgs 3:9; Jas 1:5).
- **"If you seek it like silver and search for it as for hidden treasures" (v4).** Mining in the ancient world was hard, dangerous work, and Job 28:1–11 describes men tunneling into the earth for silver and gold. Then it asks, "But where shall wisdom be found?" (Job 28:12). Wisdom has to be dug for. Jesus' parable of the treasure hidden in a field (Matt 13:44) has the same urgency and joy.
- **"Then you will understand the fear of the LORD and find the knowledge of God" (v5).** This is the first "then." The search for wisdom ends not in a technique but in God. The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge (1:7), and it is also what the seeker finds at the end. "Knowledge of God" in the Old Testament means knowing him personally, not just knowing facts about him (Hos 6:6; Jer 9:24).
- **"For the LORD gives wisdom; from his mouth come knowledge and understanding" (v6).** The key verse of the chapter. The son must search hard, yet wisdom is a gift. Effort and grace are not rivals. God gives wisdom through his words, the things that come "from his mouth."
- **"He stores up sound wisdom for the upright" (v7).** "Sound wisdom" is *tushiyyah*, a word found mostly in Proverbs and Job. It means practical success, effective wisdom, what actually works. JFB says it is literally "substance," as opposed to what is fictitious. The verb "stores up" is the same as "treasure up" in v1. The son stores up his father's words, and God stores up wisdom for those who walk uprightly.
- **"He is a shield to those who walk in integrity" (v8).** God is not only the source of wisdom but the protector of the wise. "His saints" (*chasidim*) means his loyal, faithful people, those who return his covenant love (*chesed*).
- **"Then you will understand righteousness and justice and equity, every good path" (v9).** The second "then." These three words repeat the book's purpose statement (1:3). Fearing God (v5) leads to living rightly with people (v9).
- **"For wisdom will come into your heart, and knowledge will be pleasant to your soul" (v10).** Wisdom moves from outside to inside, from a teacher's words to the son's own delight. Once it is "pleasant," it no longer has to be forced.
- **"Discretion will watch over you, understanding will guard you" (v11).** Wisdom now does for the son what God does in v8. These are the same verbs, "watch" and "guard." God protects the wise partly through the wisdom he gives them.
- **"Delivering you from the way of evil, from men of perverted speech" (v12).** Now the chapter turns to two dangers, and each begins with "delivering you" (vv12, 16). The first is evil men, the gang of chapter 1 grown up. They are marked first by their speech: "perverted" (*tahpukot*) means twisted or upside-down, words that turn right into wrong.
- **"Who forsake the paths of uprightness to walk in the ways of darkness" (v13).** Note that they forsake. They once knew the right road. **"Who rejoice in doing evil"** (v14): they are not dragged into sin but delight in it (Rom 1:32). **"Men whose paths are crooked"** (v15): the opposite of straight paths (3:6).
- **"The forbidden woman… the adulteress with her smooth words" (v16).** The second danger. "Forbidden woman" is literally "the strange woman" (*ʾishah zarah*), and "adulteress" is "the foreign woman" (*nokhriyyah*). The two words can mean a foreigner, but in Proverbs they mainly mean a woman outside the bounds, someone who belongs to another man and is off limits. She will return at length in chapters 5, 6 and 7. Again the danger begins with speech: "smooth words," literally words that "make smooth," flattery (Ps 5:9).
- **"Who forsakes the companion of her youth and forgets the covenant of her God" (v17).** "Companion" (*ʾalluph*) means a close friend or partner, here her husband (Jer 3:4). "The covenant of her God" most likely means her marriage, made before God as witness (Mal 2:14: "the LORD was witness between you and the wife of your youth… your wife by covenant"). Some think it means God's covenant with Israel at Sinai, including "You shall not commit adultery." Either way, breaking faith with a spouse is breaking faith with God. Note the matching verb: the wicked men "forsake" the paths of uprightness (v13), and she "forsakes" her husband.
- **"Her house sinks down to death, and her paths to the departed" (v18).** "The departed" is *rephaʾim*, the shades, the shadowy dead in Sheol (Ps 88:10; Isa 14:9). Her house looks like a place of pleasure, but it is the entrance to the grave. This is not only about disease or a jealous husband's revenge, though Proverbs mentions both (6:26–35). Adultery is a road that leads away from life with God.
- **"None who go to her come back, nor do they regain the paths of life" (v19).** This is strong language. It does not mean God cannot forgive adultery. David's story and Psalm 51 show he does. It means the road itself does not lead back. No one stumbles home by going further down it. The way back is repentance, a different road.
- **"So you will walk in the way of the good" (v20).** The positive goal. Avoiding evil is not the end. The son is to walk with the righteous.
- **"The upright will inhabit the land… but the wicked will be cut off from the land" (vv21–22).** "The land" is the promised land, and these lines echo Deuteronomy: obedience means life in the land, unfaithfulness means being uprooted (Deut 28:63; compare Ps 37:9, 11, 29). Israel's exile in 586 BC was that warning come true. Jesus widens the promise: "Blessed are the meek, for they shall inherit the earth" (Matt 5:5, quoting Ps 37:11).
- **Commentators:** Henry sees the chapter teaching "both how to get wisdom and how to use it when we have it," and says God gives knowledge and grace to those who diligently use the means of grace. JFB: "There must be earnest prayer and effort." He calls the woman's husband her "lawful husband" and reads "the covenant of her God" as the covenant "of marriage made in God's name." He takes "the dead" as the "shades of the departed." Guzik insists that wisdom is never given to the lazy or casual, and he quotes G. Campbell Morgan that wisdom's way is "never revealed to triflers." He also follows Bruce Waltke in reading the word for "paths" in v9 as wagon ruts, the grooves a life wears through repeated choices. The lexicon gives "track" or "path" for that word (*maʿgal*), so the picture fits even if it should not be pressed. Guzik notes that Scripture does not treat the woman as the only guilty party. The young man is just as responsible for where he goes.

#### Pointing to Christ
The father tells his son to dig for wisdom like silver, and then says the LORD gives it. In the New Testament the treasure has a name. In Christ "are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" (Col 2:3), and God made him "our wisdom and our righteousness and sanctification and redemption" (1 Cor 1:30). Seeking wisdom becomes seeking him, and finding the "knowledge of God" (v5) is knowing the Father through the Son (John 17:3). He also guards the paths of his people (v8), and the meek who walk his way will inherit the earth.
