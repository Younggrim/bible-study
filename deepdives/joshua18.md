*Setting:* Judah and Joseph have their land, but seven tribes still have none. Israel moves its center from Gilgal, by the Jordan, to Shiloh in the hill country of Ephraim, and sets up the tabernacle there. Something has gone slack. The land has been "subdued," yet the seven tribes have not moved to claim their shares, and Joshua confronts them. He sends out survey teams, the remaining land is mapped and divided into seven portions, and the lots are cast before the LORD at Shiloh. The first lot goes to Benjamin, Jacob's youngest son, whose small territory turns out to hold some of the most important places in Israel's history.

#### Allotment of the Remaining Land (vv1–10)
- **"The whole congregation… assembled at Shiloh and set up the tent of meeting there" (v1).** Shiloh lay about 20 miles north of Jerusalem in a quiet valley off the main road, between Bethel and Shechem, near the center of the land. The tabernacle, which had traveled through the wilderness, now has a settled home, as Deuteronomy 12:10–11 anticipated: when God gave them rest, he would choose a place for his name to dwell. It stayed at Shiloh through the time of the judges until the days of Eli and Samuel, when the Philistines captured the ark (1 Sam 4). Later Jeremiah warned Jerusalem, "Go now to my place that was in Shiloh… and see what I did to it for the wickedness of my people Israel" (Jer 7:12). Excavations at Shiloh show the town was destroyed around the time the Bible places that disaster.
- **"The land lay subdued before them" (v1).** The verb is *kabash*, "to bring under control." It is the word God used at creation: "fill the earth and subdue it" (Gen 1:28). Israel, settling the land with God's dwelling at its center, is in a small way doing what humanity was made to do. Numbers 32:22, 29 also uses the word: the land would be "subdued before the LORD." The promise has come true.
- **"How long will you put off going in to take possession of the land?" (v3).** "Put off" translates a form of *raphah*, "to be slack, let the hands drop." It is the same verb the Gibeonites used when they begged Joshua, "Do not relax your hand from your servants" (10:6). Joshua did not go slack for Gibeon. Now Israel is going slack about its own inheritance. Why the delay? The text does not say. Commentators suggest several reasons: the war was over and the people were comfortable; they had lived as a camp for forty years and were used to it; settling meant more fighting; or the tribes were waiting for someone else to do it for them. JFB lists "a love of ease, and reluctance to renew the war."
- **"Which the LORD, the God of your fathers, has given you" (v3).** The rebuke turns on that word "given." The land is a gift already made. Their failure is not a lack of land but a failure to receive what God has given.
- **"Provide three men from each tribe" (v4).** Twenty-one men from the seven tribes. Each tribe has its own representatives on the survey, so no one could later say the work was rigged.
- **"They shall write a description of it" (v4).** They are to "go up and down" (literally "walk about") the land and write it down town by town. The same phrase, walk the land, is what God told Abraham to do: "Arise, walk through the length and the breadth of the land, for I will give it to you" (Gen 13:17). Writing was well known in Canaan by this time, and the Tyndale notes point out that this record may lie behind the town lists of chapters 18–19. JFB notes Josephus's claim that the survey was done by men "expert in geometry."
- **"Judah shall continue in his territory on the south, and the house of Joseph… on the north" (v5).** The two big tribes are already in place. The seven portions will be shaped from what is left: the land between them, and the lands to the far north and the southwest. Matthew Henry notes that with Judah to the south and Joseph to the north, Shiloh was guarded on both sides.
- **"The Levites have no portion among you, for the priesthood of the LORD is their heritage" (v7).** The reminder from 13:14, 33 and 14:3–4 returns here. Earlier their inheritance was called the offerings and the LORD himself. Here it is "the priesthood of the LORD," the privilege of serving him.
- **"I will cast lots for you here before the LORD our God" (vv6, 8, 10).** "Before the LORD" is said three times. The lots are cast in front of the tabernacle, God's dwelling. This was not a game of chance. It was an appeal to God's decision. JFB mentions an old suggestion that two containers were used, one with tribe names and one with the seven portions, drawn together. The Bible does not say how it was done.
- **"Wrote in a book a description of it by towns in seven divisions" (v9).** The survey was done and returned. Joshua's rebuke worked. In verse 10 the land is apportioned "to each his portion."
- **Commentators:** Henry applies v3 to believers: God "has given us a title to a good land, the heavenly Canaan, but we are slack to take possession," not living by "faith, and hope, and holy joy" as we could. He adds that while Israel delayed, the Canaanites were "recovering strength and spirit." He also mentions the old idea that Shiloh's name (which may mean "peace" or "place of rest") echoes "Shiloh" in Jacob's blessing of Judah (Gen 49:10), though the connection is uncertain. JFB says Joshua's reproof shows the seven tribes "were dilatory to a criminal extent," and that the survey "required learning and intelligence" probably brought from Egypt. Guzik suggests that after a generation of living in tents, the tribes may have been afraid of something new, even something good, but whatever the cause, the root was neglect of what God had called them to do.

#### The Inheritance for Benjamin (vv11–28)
- **"The lot of the tribe of… Benjamin… fell between the people of Judah and the people of Joseph" (v11).** Benjamin was the youngest of Jacob's twelve sons and the second son of Rachel, Joseph's full brother. His tribe receives a small strip of land, about 25 miles east to west and 12 miles north to south, between the two great tribes. Moses had blessed Benjamin: "The beloved of the LORD dwells in safety. The High God surrounds him all day long, and dwells between his shoulders" (Deut 33:12). The ridge of Benjamin lay right beside Jerusalem, where God's temple would one day stand. JFB says Moses' prophecy was here "remarkably accomplished."
- **The northern border (vv12–13).** From the Jordan up past Jericho to "the wilderness of Beth-aven," then to Bethel and down to Ataroth-addar near Lower Beth-horon. This is the same line as Joseph's southern border in 16:1–3. Beth-aven ("house of wickedness") was a place near Bethel and Ai (7:2). Centuries later, when the northern kingdom set up a golden calf at Bethel, the prophet Hosea mockingly called Bethel ("house of God") "Beth-aven" (Hos 4:15; 10:5).
- **The western and southern borders (vv14–19).** The line turns south to Kiriath-jearim, "a city belonging to the people of Judah," then east along the same border described from Judah's side in 15:5–9, but in reverse. It passes the spring of Nephtoah, the Valley of Hinnom "south of the shoulder of the Jebusites," En-rogel, the stone of Bohan, and down to the north end of the Dead Sea. Because the line runs south of the Jebusite city, Jerusalem lies in Benjamin's territory.
- **"The Jordan forms its boundary on the eastern side" (v20).** Benjamin's land runs from the Jordan valley up to the central ridge. It was crossed by the main roads from the Jordan into the hills and from north to south along the ridge, making it a crossroads, and a battleground, for much of Israel's history.
- **The eastern cities (vv21–24).** Twelve cities, starting with Jericho, the first city taken in the land, now handed to the smallest tribe. Bethel (v22) appears here, though it is also linked with Joseph's territory, and later it belonged to the northern kingdom (1 Kings 12:29). Geba (v24) marked the northern edge of the kingdom of Judah in later times ("from Geba to Beersheba," 2 Kings 23:8).
- **The western cities (vv25–28).** Fourteen more cities, a remarkable list:
- **Gibeon (v25)**, the city that tricked Israel into a treaty (ch 9) and later became an important place of worship (1 Kings 3:4–5).
- **Ramah (v25)**, a few miles north of Jerusalem. Jeremiah pictured "Rachel weeping for her children" at Ramah as the exiles were led away (Jer 31:15), and Matthew applies those words to the children of Bethlehem killed by Herod (Matt 2:18).
- **Mizpeh (v26)**, where Samuel gathered Israel to repent and where Saul was chosen king (1 Sam 7:5–6; 10:17–24).
- **"Jebus (that is, Jerusalem)" (v28)**, still held by the Jebusites (Judg 1:21), assigned to Benjamin but taken by David of Judah (2 Sam 5:6–9), and so standing between the two tribes.
- **"Gibeah" (v28)**, later the hometown and capital of Saul, Israel's first king, a Benjamite (1 Sam 10:26). The Hebrew text reads "Gibeath, Kiriath," while the Greek supports "Kiriath-jearim." The count of fourteen fits taking them as two separate towns.
- **Benjamin's later story.** This small tribe gave Israel its first king, Saul. It was nearly wiped out after the terrible crime at Gibeah (Judg 19–21). When the kingdom split, Benjamin stayed with Judah, and the two together formed the southern kingdom (1 Kings 12:21). And from Benjamin came the apostle Paul, "of the tribe of Benjamin, a Hebrew of Hebrews" (Phil 3:5), who carried the gospel to the nations.
- **Commentators:** JFB explains the lot as coming up "between" Judah and Joseph and connects it with Moses' blessing. Henry notes that Shiloh lay in Ephraim but "close upon the lot of Benjamin." Guzik offers no separate comment on Benjamin's list. The Tyndale notes point out that Benjamin's land, though small, carried economic and military weight far beyond its size, since it sat on the main routes between Judah and the north.

#### Pointing to Christ
"How long will you put off going in to take possession of the land, which the LORD… has given you?" The question still lands. The land was given and the enemy was subdued, but seven tribes sat in camp. Hebrews uses Israel's story to warn believers about the same failure: "Let us therefore strive to enter that rest, so that no one may fall by the same sort of disobedience" (Heb 4:11). In Christ, God has already given his people "every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places" (Eph 1:3), and Peter writes that "his divine power has granted to us all things that pertain to life and godliness" (2 Pet 1:3). What is given still has to be received and walked in. And at the center of it all stood the tent of meeting at Shiloh, God dwelling among his people. That tent pointed ahead to the Word who "became flesh and dwelt [literally, pitched his tent] among us" (John 1:14).
