*Setting:* Chronicles was written after the exile, around 450–400 BC. David is old and near death (23:1), and the long lists of chapters 23–27 have set every Levite, priest, singer, gatekeeper, soldier and steward in place. Now the whole leadership gathers for David's farewell. First Kings 1–2 tells the same handover with a crisis: Adonijah tries to seize the throne, and David, bedridden, has Solomon crowned in a hurry. Chronicles does not deny that story, but it shows a different moment, probably soon after, when David stands before the assembly and publicly hands Solomon two things: the throne and the plans for God's house. For readers living around a modest second temple, this chapter tells them that the temple was God's idea from the start and that its pattern came from him.

#### David's Charge to Israel (vv1–8)
- **"David assembled at Jerusalem all the officials of Israel" (v1).** Every group listed in chapters 23–27 is here: tribal leaders, army divisions, commanders, the king's stewards, palace officials, the mighty men. This is the whole nation's leadership, gathered to hear the king's last word.
- **"King David rose to his feet" (v2).** An old king rising to stand before his officials was a striking act. Kings sat; subjects stood. David stands, and he calls them **"my brothers and my people,"** words of shared belonging under God rather than of command (compare Deut 17:20, where the king must not consider himself above his brothers).
- **"A house of rest for the ark… the footstool of our God" (v2).** "Rest" is a key word in Chronicles. God promised Israel rest in the land (Deut 12:9–10), and the ark that had moved from place to place would finally settle. David's own psalm prays, "Arise, O LORD, and go to your resting place, you and the ark of your might" (Ps 132:8). The ark is called God's **"footstool"**: the LORD was pictured enthroned above the cherubim in heaven, with the ark as the place his feet touched earth (Ps 99:1, 5; 132:7). Even the temple would not contain God (2 Chr 6:18). It was where heaven's King rested his feet among his people.
- **"You are a man of war and have shed blood" (v3).** David already told Solomon this (22:8). It is not a charge of murder, though David had blood on his hands in the case of Uriah. It means his calling was battle, and the house of rest belonged to a man of rest. Solomon's name, *Shelomoh*, sounds like *shalom*, "peace" (22:9).
- **"The LORD God of Israel chose me" (v4).** The word "chose" (*bachar*) runs through vv4–6 and v10: God chose Judah (Gen 49:10), then Jesse's house, then David, the youngest son (1 Sam 16:1–13), then Solomon. The kingdom was never David's achievement. **"He took pleasure in me"** shows that choice was grace.
- **"The throne of the kingdom of the LORD over Israel" (v5).** This is a phrase only Chronicles uses. Solomon would not sit on his own throne but on the LORD's (also 29:23; 2 Chr 9:8). The king of Israel was a steward of God's kingship.
- **"I have chosen him to be my son, and I will be his father" (v6).** This repeats God's promise to David in 17:13 (2 Sam 7:14). In the ancient world kings often claimed to be sons of gods. Here the relationship is covenant adoption: God takes the king as his son to rule for him. Hebrews 1:5 applies this same promise to Jesus.
- **"If he continues strong in keeping my commandments" (v7).** God's promise of David's dynasty was unconditional in one sense (17:14), but each king's own enjoyment of it depended on obedience. Solomon would begin well and end badly (1 Kings 11:1–13), and the kingdom would be torn in two.
- **"Observe and seek out all the commandments" (v8).** The charge is not only to keep God's commands but to "seek" them, to study and look into them. It is given "in the sight of all Israel… and in the hearing of our God": God himself is a witness. The goal is "that you may possess this good land and leave it for an inheritance to your children." The Chronicler's readers had lost the land once and were back on a small piece of it. They knew exactly what was at stake.
- **Commentators:** Henry reads David's standing as humility and reverence: he "rose out of his chair, and stood up upon his feet," and spoke to them as brothers, a model for leaders who speak "with affection and condescension." On v3 he says, "He must serve the public with the sword; another must do it with the line and plummet." On v8 he warns, "God's commandments will not be kept without great care." JFB notes that "the grace of God… chose the tribe, the family, the person," and calls Solomon a type of Christ. Guzik points out that Israel had no tradition of a son inheriting the throne, since Saul's line did not continue, so God's choice of Solomon mattered.

#### David's Charge to Solomon (vv9–10)
- **"Know the God of your father" (v9).** "Know" (*yada*) in Hebrew means more than knowing facts. It is personal, experienced knowledge, the word used for knowing someone closely. Solomon must not inherit his father's faith secondhand. He must know David's God for himself.
- **"Serve him with a whole heart and with a willing mind" (v9).** "Whole heart" (*lev shalem*) is a heart complete and undivided, and the adjective is from the same root as *shalom* and Solomon's name. "Willing mind" is literally a "delighting soul" (*nefesh chafetsah*). God wants service that is both undivided and glad. The irony is painful: 1 Kings 11:4 says Solomon's heart "was not wholly true to the LORD his God, as was the heart of David his father," using the same word, *shalem*.
- **"The LORD searches all hearts and understands every plan and thought" (v9).** The Hebrew verb for "searches" (*darash*) is the same verb as "seek" in the next sentence. God seeks out every heart, so Solomon should seek God. Outward service will not fool the one who reads the heart (1 Sam 16:7; Jer 17:10).
- **"If you seek him, he will be found by you, but if you forsake him, he will cast you off forever" (v9).** This is the theme of the whole book. The prophet Azariah says nearly the same to King Asa (2 Chr 15:2), and the kings of Chronicles rise or fall by whether they seek the LORD. The promise was also for exiles: "You will seek me and find me, when you seek me with all your heart" (Jer 29:13).
- **"Be strong and do it" (v10).** David's preparations meant nothing unless Solomon actually built. The house is "for the sanctuary," the holy place where God would meet his people.
- **Commentators:** All three join knowing God to serving him. Henry: "We cannot serve God aright if we do not know him," and, on the warning, "God never casts any off till they have first cast him off." JFB says "know" means "that experimental acquaintance with God which is only to be obtained by loving and serving Him." Guzik puts it in terms of balance: some bring heart without mind and others mind without heart, and God wants both. He also notes, with another writer, that Solomon's later divided heart led to a divided kingdom.

#### The Plan for the Temple (vv11–21)
- **"Then David gave Solomon his son the plan" (v11).** "Plan" is *tabnit*, "pattern" or "model," the same word used when God showed Moses the pattern of the tabernacle on the mountain: "according to the pattern… so you shall make it" (Exod 25:9, 40). Chronicles presents David as a second Moses, receiving the design for God's house, and Solomon as a second Joshua, who carries it out.
- **What the plan covered (vv11–13).** The porch, the rooms, the treasuries, the upper rooms and inner chambers, and **"the room for the mercy seat"**, the Most Holy Place, where the ark's gold cover (*kapporet*, "place of atonement") was sprinkled with blood on the Day of Atonement (Lev 16:14–15). The plan also covered the courts, the storerooms, and the divisions of priests and Levites. The building and the people who served in it were planned together.
- **"All that he had in mind" (v12).** The Hebrew is literally "all that was by the spirit with him." It can mean David's own mind and intentions (ESV) or "by the Spirit," as the KJV has it. Verse 19 makes clear that whatever David's part, the design came from God.
- **The weights of gold and silver (vv14–18).** Lampstands, tables for the showbread, forks, basins, cups and bowls were each given a weight of metal. Solomon's temple had ten lampstands and ten tables (2 Chr 4:7–8), more than the tabernacle's one each, and some silver as well as gold. The detail shows that nothing in God's house was left to guesswork.
- **"The golden chariot of the cherubim" (v18).** The two great cherubim Solomon would set in the Most Holy Place, with wings spread over the ark (1 Kings 6:23–28). Calling them a "chariot" (*merkavah*) links them to the picture of God riding on the cherubim (Ps 18:10) and to Ezekiel's vision of God's mobile throne (Ezek 1; 10). God's throne was not tied down to a place; he could move, and in Ezekiel's day he did leave his temple (Ezek 10:18–19).
- **"In writing from the hand of the LORD" (v19).** The plan came to David as a written document under God's guiding hand. "The hand of the LORD" is the phrase used of prophets moved by God (2 Kings 3:15; Ezek 1:3). Exactly how David received it is not said. The point is clear: the temple was revealed, not invented.
- **"Be strong and courageous… He will not leave you or forsake you" (v20).** These are the words Moses spoke to Joshua and God then spoke to Joshua himself (Deut 31:6–8; Josh 1:5–9). The book of Hebrews applies them to every believer: "I will never leave you nor forsake you" (Heb 13:5). The promise has a goal attached: "until all the work… is finished."
- **"Every willing man who has skill" (v21).** Solomon would not build alone. Priests, Levites, skilled workers, officials and people were all ready. "Willing" picks up the "willing mind" of v9: the king's glad heart is matched by a people's glad hands, as with the tabernacle (Exod 35:21–29).
- **Commentators:** Henry says David "had this pattern by the Spirit," and that the temple, as a kind of sacrament, had to follow God's design rather than human invention. JFB thinks v12 means the plan was "with him in spirit," that is, in David's mind, and suggests the whole building was shown to him "in a vision, or trance." The ESV's "in mind" sits closer to JFB. Guzik passes on a comment that the cherubim were "the chariot in which God rode," with the warning that God's presence could leave if provoked, and on v20 urges simply getting on with the work instead of dreaming over the plans. Henry praises the workers "who are both willing and skilful."

#### Pointing to Christ
David passes to his son a throne that is really the LORD's, a promise that God will be his father, and a pattern for the place where God would dwell with his people. Solomon received all three and fell short of the "whole heart" his father asked for. Jesus, the greater Son of David, is the one to whom God says without condition, "You are my Son" (Heb 1:5), and he builds the true house of God. He called his own body the temple (John 2:19–21), and he is building his people into "a dwelling place for God by the Spirit" (Eph 2:22). The mercy seat David planned found its meaning when God put Christ forward "as a propitiation by his blood" (Rom 3:25), where the Greek word is the one used for the mercy seat. And the promise "he will not leave you or forsake you" now rests on everyone who belongs to him until the work is finished.
