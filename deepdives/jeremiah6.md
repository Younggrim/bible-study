*Setting:* Jerusalem, probably in the last years of Josiah or the first years of Jehoiakim (around 610–605 BC), as Babylon took over Assyria's empire and began moving toward Judah. Chapters 4–6 form one long warning of the invasion "from the north." Chapter 4 sounded the alarm, and chapter 5 searched the city for one honest person and found none. Chapter 6 brings the siege right to Jerusalem's walls. It ends Jeremiah's first collection of sermons with a refiner's verdict: the fire has been lit, the bellows blown, and no silver has come out.

#### Impending Disaster for Jerusalem (vv1–30)
- **"Flee for safety, O people of Benjamin, from the midst of Jerusalem!" (v1).** Jerusalem sat right on the border between Benjamin and Judah, and Jeremiah himself was from Benjamin (1:1). In 4:5–6 people from the countryside were told to run into Jerusalem. Now even Jerusalem is not safe, and they are told to run out of it, south, away from the army coming from the north.
- **"Blow the trumpet in Tekoa, and raise a signal on Beth-haccherem" (v1).** Tekoa, Amos's hometown, lay about ten miles south of Jerusalem on the edge of the wilderness. There is a pun in the Hebrew: *biTqoaʿ tiqʿu shofar*, "in Tekoa blow the trumpet." Beth-haccherem ("house of the vineyard") was a hilltop a few miles south of the city, probably at the site now called Ramat Rachel, a good place for a fire signal. A small inscribed letter found at Lachish from Jeremiah's time mentions watching for fire signals.
- **"The lovely and delicately bred I will destroy, the daughter of Zion" (v2).** The ESV footnote mentions another possible reading: "I will liken the daughter of Zion to a lovely pasture." Both readings lead into v3.
- **"Shepherds with their flocks shall come against her" (v3).** The "shepherds" are enemy generals, and their "flocks" are their troops, camped around the city and eating the land bare. Each one "pastures… in his place."
- **"Prepare war against her" (v4).** The Hebrew is literally "sanctify war." Armies in the ancient world began a campaign with sacrifices and rites to their gods (1 Sam 7:9; Joel 3:9). **"Let us attack at noon!"** Armies usually rested in the midday heat, but these attackers are too eager to wait. Then they complain that the day is ending, **"the shadows of evening lengthen!"**, and decide to attack by night (v5). The scene is overheard from the enemy camp.
- **"Cut down her trees; cast up a siege mound against Jerusalem" (v6).** Besiegers cut trees for siege ramps, towers and battering rams, and piled earth into a ramp up to the walls. The Babylonians did this at Jerusalem (32:24; 2 Kings 25:1). The order comes from **"the LORD of hosts"**, the commander of heaven's armies. The enemy is God's instrument.
- **"There is nothing but oppression within her" (v6).** The reason for the siege. "Must be punished" is literally "must be visited," God coming to inspect and act.
- **"As a well keeps its water fresh, so she keeps fresh her evil" (v7).** A well that keeps producing water is a picture of life (2:13). Jerusalem keeps producing evil just as steadily. Violence, destruction, sickness and wounds flow out of her day after day.
- **"Be warned, O Jerusalem, lest I turn from you in disgust" (v8).** Even with the siege ramps going up, God gives one more warning. The verb pictures a soul pulling away in revulsion, the opposite of the bond of love.
- **"They shall glean thoroughly as a vine the remnant of Israel" (v9).** In Israel the law said to leave some grapes for the poor at harvest (Lev 19:10). This harvest leaves nothing. The gatherer goes back over the branches again and again. Babylon would deport Judah in waves, in 605, 597, 586 and 582 BC, until almost no one was left (52:28–30).
- **"To whom shall I speak and give warning, that they may hear?" (v10).** Jeremiah's frustration. **"Their ears are uncircumcised, they cannot listen."** In 4:4 he called for circumcised hearts. Now the ears are covered over, closed to God's word (Acts 7:51 picks up the phrase). **"The word of the LORD is to them an object of scorn"**: they mock it.
- **"I am full of the wrath of the LORD; I am weary of holding it in" (v11).** Jeremiah has absorbed God's anger, and it presses to get out. God tells him to **"pour it out"** on everyone: children in the street, young men in their groups, husbands and wives, the old and the very old. No age is spared.
- **"Their houses shall be turned over to others, their fields and wives together" (v12).** The covenant curse of Deuteronomy 28:30: "You shall betroth a wife, but another man shall ravish her. You shall build a house, but you shall not dwell in it."
- **"From the least to the greatest… everyone is greedy for unjust gain" (v13).** The phrase for greed (*botseaʿ batsaʿ*) means cutting off a piece, taking a cut by violence or fraud. **"From prophet to priest, everyone deals falsely."** The religious leaders are the worst, because they should know better.
- **"They have healed the wound of my people lightly, saying, 'Peace, peace,' when there is no peace" (v14).** The most famous lines in the chapter, repeated nearly word for word in 8:11. The nation has a deep, infected wound, and the prophets put a bandage on it and call it healed. "Peace" is *shalom*, wholeness and well-being, not just the absence of war. The false prophets promised safety without repentance. Ezekiel says the same: they whitewash a wall that is about to fall (Ezek 13:10–16). Paul warns that the day of the Lord will come when people are saying "There is peace and security" (1 Thess 5:3).
- **"They did not know how to blush" (v15).** Shame is a gift. It shows a conscience still works. These people have lost it (3:3). **"They shall fall among those who fall"**: they will be among the bodies when judgment comes.
- **"Stand by the roads, and look, and ask for the ancient paths, where the good way is" (v16).** A traveler at a crossroads, unsure which way to go, asks someone who knows the land. "Ancient paths" (*netivot olam*, literally "everlasting paths") means the way God laid down at Sinai, the covenant their fathers had walked in. It is old not because old is always better, but because God's way does not change (18:15). **"And find rest for your souls."** Jesus repeats these words almost exactly when he invites the weary: "you will find rest for your souls" (Matt 11:29).
- **"But they said, 'We will not walk in it'" (v16).** Blunt refusal. They do not argue, they just decline.
- **"I set watchmen over you, saying, 'Pay attention to the sound of the trumpet!'" (v17).** A watchman stood on the wall and blew the ram's horn when an enemy appeared. The prophets were God's watchmen (Ezek 3:17; 33:1–7). **"But they said, 'We will not pay attention.'"** The second refusal, in the same words.
- **"Hear, O nations… Hear, O earth" (vv18–19).** As in 2:12, God calls witnesses. The nations and the earth itself should see that the disaster is **"the fruit of their devices"**, the harvest of what they planted. "As for my law, they have rejected it." *Torah* means instruction, the whole teaching of the covenant.
- **"What use to me is frankincense that comes from Sheba, or sweet cane from a distant land?" (v20).** Frankincense was a fragrant resin from southern Arabia, carried north by camel caravans from Sheba (modern Yemen). "Sweet cane" was probably calamus, an aromatic reed, perhaps imported from India, used in the holy anointing oil (Exod 30:23). These were costly imports. Temple worship in Jerusalem was busy and expensive, but God refuses it, because offerings without obedience are worthless (7:21–23; 1 Sam 15:22; Isa 1:11–17; Amos 5:21–24). JFB points out a play on words: their sweet cane is not "sweet" (*ʿarev*, pleasing) to him.
- **"I will lay before this people stumbling blocks" (v21).** God himself will put obstacles in their path, so they fall, fathers and sons, neighbors and friends together. Refusing the good way leads to a road full of things to trip over.
- **"A people is coming from the north country… they are cruel and have no mercy" (vv22–23).** The enemy is described again, closer now: bow and javelin, horses, an army **"set in array as a man for battle."** **"The sound of them is like the roaring sea"**, but in 5:22 God set the sand as a limit for the sea. The roaring sea of armies also obeys him. These verses are reused almost word for word in 50:41–43 against Babylon itself. The cruel conqueror will one day face a conqueror from the north.
- **"We have heard the report of it; our hands fall helpless" (v24).** The people speak. Hands hanging limp is a picture of utter discouragement (Isa 13:7). **"Pain as of a woman in labor"**, as in 4:31.
- **"Terror is on every side" (v25).** In Hebrew, *magor missaviv*. This phrase becomes one of Jeremiah's signature lines. He gives it as a new name to the priest Pashhur who beat him (20:3), his enemies mock him with it (20:10), and it returns in 46:5 and 49:29. Psalm 31:13 has the same words.
- **"Make mourning as for an only son" (v26).** Losing an only son meant losing the family's future, the deepest grief a family could know (Amos 8:10; Zech 12:10). **"Roll in ashes"**: the most extreme mourning, as Job sat among the ashes (Job 2:8).
- **"I have made you a tester of metals among my people" (v27).** God now speaks to Jeremiah about his role. The prophet is an assayer, someone who tests ore to find out how much silver is in it. The KJV has "a tower and a fortress," following a different sense of the Hebrew. Most modern translations, like the ESV, read the whole verse in light of the metal-working picture that follows.
- **"They are bronze and iron; all of them act corruptly" (v28).** Bronze and iron are cheap, hard metals, not silver. "Going about with slanders" is a picture of the talebearer moving from house to house (Lev 19:16).
- **"The bellows blow fiercely; the lead is consumed by the fire" (v29).** Ancient silver refiners melted the ore with lead in a clay dish. The bellows drove air over the fire. The lead oxidized and soaked up the impurities, leaving pure silver behind. Here the bellows blow, the lead burns away, the work is done, **"in vain."** **"The wicked are not removed."** God's discipline has been applied (5:3), and nothing pure has come out.
- **"Rejected silver they are called, for the LORD has rejected them" (v30).** The same Hebrew verb, *maʾas*, twice. Refuse silver is thrown out on the slag heap. Isaiah 1:22 made the same charge: "Your silver has become dross." Ezekiel 22:17–22 develops the picture at length. This verdict ends Jeremiah's first group of sermons (chs 2–6).
- **Commentators:** JFB notes the pun on Tekoa and "blow," and reads "sanctify war" in v4 as proclaiming it with solemn rites. On v10 Henry calls the uncircumcised ear "carnal and fleshly, indisposed to receive the voice of God," and JFB similarly speaks of the "foreskin of carnality." On v14 JFB describes a spiritual wound treated as "slight," while Guzik draws on Spurgeon's sermon on false peace. Guzik adds that a faithful preacher sometimes has to help people blush again (v15). On v16 Henry writes, "The way of religion and godliness is a good old way, the way that all the saints in all ages have walked in." JFB contrasts the "old paths" with the newer, degenerate path of idolatry. On v20 Henry says their offerings "were so far from being pleasing to God that they were a provocation to him." On v27 JFB prefers "assayer" to "fortress," since it fits the metal picture, and the ESV agrees. On v30 Henry says they "glitter as if they had some silver in them, but there is nothing of real virtue or goodness to be found among them." The Tyndale notes add that the vision of the invasion probably belongs to 605 BC, when Babylon first moved against Judah.

#### Pointing to Christ
Jeremiah called the people to the ancient paths and promised "rest for your souls," and they said, "We will not walk in it." Jesus took up the same words: "Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden… and you will find rest for your souls" (Matt 11:28–29). He is the good way itself (John 14:6). The false prophets said "Peace, peace," when there was none. Jesus made a real peace "by the blood of his cross" (Col 1:20) and healed the wound properly, at the cost of his own wounds (Isa 53:5). And where the refiner's fire found only rejected silver, God promises to make a people who will come out of the fire as gold (Mal 3:3; 1 Pet 1:7), not by their own purity, but because they are joined to the one tested and found pure.
