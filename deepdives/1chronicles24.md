*Setting:* Chronicles was written after the exile, around 450–400 BC. In his last years David is organizing everyone who will serve in the temple Solomon is about to build. Chapter 23 counted the Levites and set out their duties. This chapter turns to the priests, the descendants of Aaron, and divides them into twenty-four "divisions" or shifts that would take turns serving at the altar. It then adds a short supplement to the list of Levites. The duties of the priests are not described here, because they had been set out long before in the Law of Moses (Exod 28–29; Lev 1–7). What is new is the rotation. That rotation lasted for a thousand years. It was still running when an old priest named Zechariah, "of the division of Abijah," went into the temple to burn incense and met the angel Gabriel (Luke 1:5–9). For the Chronicler's readers, many of whom belonged to these priestly families, this chapter was their own family's charter.

#### David Organizes the Priests (vv1–31)
- **"The sons of Aaron: Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar" (v1).** Aaron, Moses' brother, was Israel's first high priest, and only his male descendants could serve as priests (Exod 28:1; Num 3:10).
- **"But Nadab and Abihu died before their father and had no children" (v2).** The Chronicler says no more, but his readers knew the story. Aaron's two eldest sons "offered unauthorized fire before the LORD," and "fire came out from before the LORD and consumed them" (Lev 10:1–2; Num 3:4). Their death left the priesthood to the younger sons, **Eleazar and Ithamar**. A sober reminder stands at the head of the priestly roster: the privilege of drawing near to God is never a license to approach him on one's own terms.
- **Zadok and Ahimelech (v3).** Each of the two priestly branches had a leader. **Zadok** came from Eleazar's line, through Phinehas (6:4–8). **Ahimelech**, son of Abiathar, came from Ithamar's line, through Eli. That branch had been warned of judgment in Eli's day (1 Sam 2:30–36), and the warning came true when Solomon removed Abiathar from the priesthood (1 Kings 2:26–27). From then on Zadok's family held the high priesthood, and Ezekiel later speaks of "the sons of Zadok" as the faithful priests (Ezek 44:15). On the names "Ahimelech the son of Abiathar," see the note on 18:16.
- **"Sixteen heads of fathers' houses of the sons of Eleazar, and eight of the sons of Ithamar" (v4).** Eleazar's branch had grown larger, so it received two-thirds of the divisions. The division was by size, not favoritism.
- **"They divided them by lot, all alike" (v5).** The order of service was decided by casting lots, probably marked stones or sticks drawn from a container. Lots were not gambling in Israel. They were a way of leaving a decision to God: "The lot is cast into the lap, but its every decision is from the LORD" (Prov 16:33). The land was divided by lot (Josh 18:10), and the apostles used lots to choose Matthias (Acts 1:26). Using lots here meant no family could claim the best slot by rank or pressure. Both branches had "sacred officers and officers of God," leaders worthy of the honor, so the lots treated them "all alike."
- **"The scribe Shemaiah, the son of Nethanel, a Levite, recorded them" (v6).** The process was public and recorded, done before the king, the princes, both chief priests and the family heads. Nothing was left to private arrangement. The phrase "one father's house being chosen for Eleazar and one chosen for Ithamar" seems to describe the lots being drawn by turns from the two branches. JFB thinks the drawing went two from Eleazar for each one from Ithamar, to match the sixteen and eight, but the text does not spell this out.
- **The twenty-four divisions (vv7–18).** The list runs from **Jehoiarib** first to **Maaziah** twenty-fourth. Jewish tradition and Josephus say each division served for one week at a time, from Sabbath to Sabbath, so each came round about twice a year, and all served together at the great festivals. A few names reappear later:
- **Jehoiarib (v7).** The first division. The Maccabees, who led the Jewish revolt against the Greek king Antiochus in the 160s BC, came from this family ("Joarib," 1 Macc 2:1). The historian Josephus said he did too.
- **Hakkoz (v10).** After the exile, the family of Hakkoz could not find its genealogy records and was barred from the priesthood for a time (Ezra 2:61–62). Later a member of the family helped rebuild Jerusalem's wall (Neh 3:4, 21).
- **Abijah (v10).** The eighth division, the one to which Zechariah, the father of John the Baptist, belonged (Luke 1:5). Luke adds that "according to the custom of the priesthood, he was chosen by lot to enter the temple of the Lord and burn incense" (Luke 1:9). The lots David's men drew still governed the temple in Jesus' generation.
- **Immer (v14).** Pashhur "the son of Immer," a priest of this family, beat Jeremiah and put him in the stocks (Jer 20:1–2).
- **After the exile.** Only four priestly families came back in the first return: Jedaiah, Immer, Harim and Pashhur, 4,289 men in all (Ezra 2:36–39). Jewish tradition says they were reorganized into twenty-four divisions again using the old names. Nehemiah 12:1–21 lists priestly families of that time, many matching names here. In 1962 archaeologists at Caesarea found fragments of a later Hebrew inscription listing the twenty-four divisions and the villages where each lived. The system David set up lasted until the Romans destroyed the temple in AD 70.
- **"According to the procedure established for them by Aaron their father, as the LORD God of Israel had commanded him" (v19).** David created the schedule, not the priesthood. What the priests did came from God through Moses and Aaron. David's arrangement served it.
- **The rest of the Levites (vv20–31).** This list fills out the one in 23:6–23. It mostly takes the Kohathite and Merarite families one generation further: **Shubael** (Shebuel in 23:16) is followed by his son **Jehdeiah**, **Rehabiah** by **Isshiah**, **Shelomoth** by **Jahath**, and so on. It adds a new Merarite line, **Jaaziah**, not mentioned before (vv26–27), and it leaves out the Gershonites entirely. Most scholars think it is a later supplement, bringing the register up to date for a following generation. The Tyndale notes take it the same way.
- **"The head of each father's house and his younger brother alike, cast lots" (v31).** The Levites drew lots for their duties too. The eldest and the youngest got the same chance. Rank in the family did not decide who served first.
- **Commentators:** Henry draws out the principle of order: "When every one has, knows, and keeps his place and work, the more there are the better." He applies it to the church, where "every member has its use, for the good of the whole," and he connects the even-handed lots of v31 with the gospel: "In Christ, no difference is made between bond and free, elder and younger." JFB says Nadab and Abihu died "during his lifetime," not in Aaron's presence, and explains that the lots were used "to remove all cause of jealousy." On v3 JFB calls Ahimelech's place beside Zadok "perplexing" but finds "no ground for regarding the text as faulty," since grandsons often carried their grandfathers' names. Guzik, following Martin Selman, describes the week-long rotation, each division serving twice a year, and cites J. Barton Payne on how few of David's original divisions were still intact after the exile. JFB has no notes on vv7–31.

#### Pointing to Christ
For a thousand years the priests of Israel came on duty by lot, a week at a time, and went home again. The rotation was orderly and fair, and it reminded Israel that no single priest could stay before God forever. Then one of them, from the eighth division, went in to burn incense and heard that his son would "make ready for the Lord a people prepared" (Luke 1:17). The forerunner of Christ was born into David's priestly roster. Christ himself was not from Levi at all. He is a priest "after the order of Melchizedek," appointed not by lot or descent "but by the power of an indestructible life" (Heb 7:14–16). He never goes off duty, and "he holds his priesthood permanently, because he continues forever" (Heb 7:24). Revelation pictures twenty-four elders around God's throne (Rev 4:4). Many interpreters think that number recalls these twenty-four divisions, the whole priesthood gathered in worship before God.
