*Setting:* Chapters 1–3 told Hosea's story through his marriage: the unfaithful wife, the children with names of judgment, and the husband who bought her back. From chapter 4 on, the book becomes a collection of Hosea's sermons to the northern kingdom of Israel, preached over some thirty years, from the end of Jeroboam II's reign (around 753 BC) to just before Samaria fell in 722 BC. The marriage picture stays in the background, but now the charges are spelled out. Chapter 4 opens like a court case: the LORD has a "controversy" with his people. It probably comes from the early years, while the country was still prosperous. The central charge is that there is "no knowledge of God in the land," and the chapter lays most of the blame on the priests who were supposed to teach it. The whole chapter is one section in the ESV.

#### The LORD Accuses Israel (vv1–19)
- **"Hear the word of the LORD… for the LORD has a controversy" (v1).** "Controversy" is *rib*, a lawsuit or legal case, the same word as "plead" in 2:2. God is the plaintiff, the people the defendants, and the covenant is the law they broke. JFB calls it a "judicial ground of complaint." Micah 6:1–2 and Isaiah 1:2 use the same courtroom form.
- **"No faithfulness or steadfast love, and no knowledge of God" (v1).** Three great covenant words are missing. *Faithfulness* (*emet*) means reliability, truthfulness, being someone others can trust. *Steadfast love* (*hesed*) is loyal, committed love, the love God promised to show his people (2:19). *Knowledge of God* (*daat elohim*) is not just information but a personal, obedient relationship. The Tyndale notes observe that the charges begin with what is missing on the inside. When these go, everything else follows.
- **"Swearing, lying, murder, stealing, and committing adultery" (v2).** Five sins, each forbidden in the Ten Commandments: taking the LORD's name in vain, false witness, murder, theft and adultery (Exod 20:7, 13–16). In Hebrew they are five bare verbs in a row, like a charge sheet. Jeremiah 7:9 gives almost the same list. **"They break all bounds"**: the verb means to break out, burst through, as water through a dam or a thief through a wall. **"Bloodshed follows bloodshed"**, literally "bloods touch bloods." JFB: "One act of bloodshed follows another without any interval between." That describes the years after Jeroboam, when four kings were assassinated in quick succession (2 Kings 15).
- **"Therefore the land mourns" (v3).** Human sin affects creation. Even the beasts, birds and fish suffer, in the reverse order of creation in Genesis 1, as if the world is being undone. Paul says creation still groans because of human sin, waiting to be set free (Rom 8:19–22). The word "languish" was used in Joel 1:10–12 of the drought.
- **"Yet let no one contend… for with you is my contention, O priest" (v4).** The Hebrew of this verse is hard, and the ESV follows a slight emendation. The traditional text reads something like "your people are like those who contend with the priest" (KJV). Either way, the point turns from the people to the priest. Israel's people blame each other; God says the real blame lies with the religious leaders. The "priest" may be a single high priest, perhaps at Bethel, standing for the whole priesthood.
- **"You shall stumble by day; the prophet also shall stumble with you by night" (v5).** The false prophets who backed the priests will fall with them. "Stumble" covers both ruin and moral failure. **"I will destroy your mother"**: the priest's family, or more likely the nation itself, the "mother" of chapter 2.
- **"My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge" (v6).** This is one of the best-known lines in the prophets. The word "destroyed" means cut off, silenced. It is not lack of brains or schooling that destroys them, but not knowing God. And this ignorance is chosen: "because *you* have rejected knowledge." The priests' main job was to teach God's law (Lev 10:11; Deut 33:10; Mal 2:7). They had stopped.
- **"I reject you from being a priest to me… I also will forget your children" (v6).** Each phrase is measure for measure. They rejected knowledge, so God rejects them. They forgot God's law, so God will forget their children. Priesthood passed from father to son, so this means the end of their line. Remember that Jeroboam I had set up his own priesthood at Bethel and Dan, appointing men who were not Levites (1 Kings 12:31), and the faithful Levites had left for Judah (2 Chr 11:13–14).
- **"The more they increased, the more they sinned against me" (v7).** Under Jeroboam II, Israel prospered and grew. God's blessing became an occasion for more sin (13:6). **"I will change their glory into shame."** Jewish tradition lists this verse among a small number of places where early scribes are said to have softened the wording; the original may have read "they changed *my* glory into shame," as in Psalm 106:20 and Jeremiah 2:11. Both readings fit Hosea's thought: they traded God for idols, and God will turn their pride into disgrace.
- **"They feed on the sin of my people; they are greedy for their iniquity" (v8).** The word for "sin" (*hattat*) also means "sin offering." Priests were given part of the sin offering to eat (Lev 6:26). So the more people sinned, the more the priests ate. They had a financial interest in sin continuing. JFB: "The priests greedily devoured them."
- **"Like people, like priest" (v9).** The priests should have raised the people up; instead they sank to their level. Now both will share the same judgment. JFB: "They are one in guilt; therefore they shall be one in punishment." Guzik reads it both ways: corrupt people produce corrupt leaders, and corrupt leaders produce corrupt people.
- **"They shall eat, but not be satisfied; they shall play the whore, but not multiply" (v10).** The punishment fits the sin exactly. Baal worship promised food and fertility, but those who serve him will be hungry and childless. The covenant curse said the same (Lev 26:26; Mic 6:14).
- **"Whoredom, wine, and new wine, which take away the understanding" (v11).** The ESV joins the end of v10 to v11: they forsook the LORD to cherish these things. "Understanding" is literally "heart," which in Hebrew is the seat of thinking and choosing, not just emotion. Sexual sin and drunkenness, both part of the Baal festivals, dull the mind until people can no longer think straight about God.
- **"My people inquire of a piece of wood, and their walking staff gives them oracles" (v12).** "My people," God's own, are asking a stick for advice. The "piece of wood" may be a carved idol or a sacred pole (Asherah). The "walking staff" points to a form of fortune-telling using sticks or rods, thrown or allowed to fall to give an answer. JFB calls it "divination by rods." The people who had the living God's word went to wood (Jer 2:27).
- **"A spirit of whoredom has led them astray" (v12).** "Spirit" here means a driving inner force, as though they are possessed by their unfaithfulness. It returns in 5:4. Sin has become a master, not just a choice.
- **"They sacrifice on the tops of the mountains… under oak, poplar, and terebinth" (v13).** Canaanite shrines were often on hilltops and in groves of large trees, seen as places where heaven and earth met. "Because their shade is good" has an edge to it: they choose worship by comfort. The law commanded Israel to destroy such shrines (Deut 12:2).
- **"Therefore your daughters play the whore, and your brides commit adultery" (v13).** When fathers and husbands lead the way into idolatry and sexual rites, the young women follow.
- **"I will not punish your daughters… for the men themselves go aside with prostitutes" (v14).** In a culture that punished women harshly and excused men, God turns the double standard upside down. He will not single out the daughters and brides when the men set the example. The Tyndale notes highlight how unusual this is. **"Sacrifice with cult prostitutes"**: the Hebrew *qedeshot* means "holy" or "set-apart women." Older scholars took them as sacred prostitutes in Canaanite temples (compare Deut 23:17–18). Some recent scholars question how widespread ritual prostitution really was, but the verse clearly links sexual sin with pagan sacrifice. **"A people without understanding shall come to ruin"** sums up the chapter.
- **"Though you play the whore, O Israel, let not Judah become guilty" (v15).** A warning to the southern kingdom: do not follow the north. The same pull toward Baal was strong there too, and Judah later fell to it (2 Kings 16:3–4; 21:3).
- **"Enter not into Gilgal, nor go up to Beth-aven" (v15).** Gilgal, near Jericho, was where Israel first camped in the land and renewed the covenant (Josh 4–5). Bethel was where God met Jacob (Gen 28:19). Both had become centres of corrupted worship (Amos 4:4; 5:5). Hosea renames *Bethel*, "house of God," as *Beth-aven*, "house of wickedness" or "house of nothing." The name was borrowed from a nearby town (Josh 7:2), and Amos had made the same pun (Amos 5:5). **"Swear not, 'As the LORD lives'"**: using God's name at an idol shrine was worse than not using it at all.
- **"Like a stubborn heifer, Israel is stubborn" (v16).** A young cow that will not accept the yoke, digging in its heels. Compare 10:11, where Ephraim is "a trained calf that loved to thresh." **"Can the LORD now feed them like a lamb in a broad pasture?"** The question is ironic. A lamb in wide open country has no protection. Israel wanted freedom from God's yoke; it will get open country with no shepherd. JFB takes the lamb picture "not in a good sense," as a lamb left alone and exposed to its foes.
- **"Ephraim is joined to idols; leave him alone" (v17).** Ephraim was the largest northern tribe, and Hosea uses its name for the whole kingdom more than thirty times. "Joined" is the word for being bound or allied together, as by a spell or treaty. "Leave him alone" is probably addressed to Judah: don't get involved. It is also a terrible picture of judgment. When God stops warning and stops restraining, people are handed over to what they chose (Rom 1:24, 26, 28).
- **"When their drink is gone, they give themselves to whoring" (v18).** The Hebrew of vv18–19 is very difficult, and translations differ widely. The sense is that their feasting ends in sexual sin, and their leaders ("rulers," literally "shields") love disgrace.
- **"A wind has wrapped them in its wings" (v19).** The wind will catch them up and carry them off, a picture of sudden exile. The word for "wind" (*ruach*) is the same as "spirit" in v12. The "spirit of whoredom" that carried them into sin will become the wind that carries them away. **"They shall be ashamed because of their sacrifices"**: the worship they trusted will be the source of their shame.
- **Commentators:** Guzik reads v1 as a lawsuit and, following Boice, suggests Paul's portrait of humanity without God in Romans 1 echoes this chapter. He presses v6 as a warning for pastors whose task is to teach Scripture, and on v17 contrasts "leave him alone" with Jesus' prayer that Peter's faith would not fail (Luke 22:31–32): the worst thing God could do is leave someone to himself. On v8, Guzik follows Leon Wood in saying the priests actually relished the people's sin, and JFB agrees they "greedily devoured" the sin offerings. JFB reads the "lack of knowledge" in v6 as "lack of piety," wilful ignorance. Henry, outlining the chapter, says the sorest judgment of all is that "they should be let alone in their sins." On v14, JFB says God's punishment falls on "the fathers and husbands" who set the example, which matches the Tyndale notes.

#### Pointing to Christ
Hosea's charge was that God's people had no knowledge of God, and the priests who should have taught them had abandoned their calling. Jesus came as the true priest and teacher who knows the Father perfectly: "No one knows the Father except the Son and anyone to whom the Son chooses to reveal him" (Matt 11:27). Unlike the priests of Bethel, he did not feed on the sins of the people. He became the sin offering himself (2 Cor 5:21; Heb 9:26). And he described eternal life in the very words Hosea found missing in Israel: "that they know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent" (John 17:3).
