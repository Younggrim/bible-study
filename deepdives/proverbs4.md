*Setting:* More of the father's talks in Proverbs 1–9 (see Proverbs 1 on authorship). Chapter 3 ended with a picture of the wise and the foolish heading toward honor or disgrace. Chapter 4 holds three short talks (vv1–9, 10–19, 20–27). In the first the father tells how his own father taught him, so the chapter becomes a picture of wisdom handed down through three generations. The second sets two paths side by side, one growing brighter and one sinking into darkness. The third moves through the body, from heart to mouth to eyes to feet, and holds one of the book's central verses: "Keep your heart with all vigilance, for from it flow the springs of life." The ESV gives the chapter one heading.

#### A Father's Wise Instruction (vv1–27)
- **"Hear, O sons, a father's instruction" (v1).** The address is plural here, "sons," rather than the usual "my son." It may be a teacher addressing pupils, a father addressing his children, or both. In Israel and the wider ancient Near East, "father" and "son" were also used for teacher and student (2 Kgs 2:12).
- **"I give you good precepts" (v2).** "Precepts" (*leqach*) is literally "what is received," teaching that is taken in and passed on. The father has received it too, as the next verse shows.
- **"When I was a son with my father, tender, the only one in the sight of my mother" (v3).** If Solomon is the speaker, this is David teaching Solomon. Solomon was not literally an only son. He had older half-brothers like Absalom and Adonijah, and Bathsheba had other sons (1 Chr 3:5). The word "only one" (*yachid*) means treasured, the special one, as Isaac was Abraham's "only son" (Gen 22:2). JFB says "as an only son," though he had brothers. "Tender" means young and soft, still impressionable. David's charge to Solomon on his deathbed sounds like this teaching (1 Kgs 2:2–4; 1 Chr 28:9).
- **"Let your heart hold fast my words; keep my commandments, and live" (v4).** The grandfather's words now begin and run, probably, to v9. "Keep… and live" is the language of the law: "the person who does them shall live by them" (Lev 18:5; Deut 30:15–16).
- **"Get wisdom; get insight" (v5).** "Get" is *qanah*, to acquire, often by buying (Gen 25:10; Ruth 4:9). Wisdom is something to be acquired at a cost. Elsewhere Proverbs says "Buy truth, and do not sell it" (23:23) and asks why a fool has money in hand "to buy wisdom when he has no sense" (17:16).
- **"Do not forsake her, and she will keep you; love her, and she will guard you" (v6).** Wisdom is pictured as a woman again, now like a bride or a beloved. "Love her" and "embrace her" (v8) are the language of courtship. Chapters 5–7 will set this love against the adulteress's false love.
- **"The beginning of wisdom is this: Get wisdom" (v7).** This sounds circular, and it is meant to. The first step toward wisdom is to decide to want it, and to want it above everything else. "Whatever you get, get insight" means even at the cost of everything you own (Matt 13:45–46). The older KJV calls wisdom "the principal thing," from the same word for "beginning" (*reshit*) as 1:7.
- **"Prize her highly, and she will exalt you" (v8).** "Prize highly" is an uncommon verb, perhaps to lift up or to cherish. What we honor, honors us.
- **"A graceful garland… a beautiful crown" (v9).** A crown for the victor or for a bride and groom at a wedding (Song 3:11). The garland of 1:9 is now a crown. JFB compares it to "the chaplet or wreath of conquerors."
- **"Hear, my son, and accept my words, that the years of your life may be many" (v10).** The second talk opens. "Accept" (*laqach*) is the verb behind "precepts" in v2. What was received is meant to be received again.
- **"I have taught you the way of wisdom; I have led you in the paths of uprightness" (v11).** The father speaks as a guide who has walked the road himself.
- **"When you walk, your step will not be hampered, and if you run, you will not stumble" (v12).** "Hampered" pictures a narrow, cramped path. Wisdom gives room to move. Psalm 18:36 says the same of God: "You gave a wide place for my steps under me."
- **"Keep hold of instruction; do not let go; guard her, for she is your life" (v13).** "Keep hold" is a strong grip, the word for seizing something and refusing to let go.
- **"Do not enter… avoid it; do not go on it; turn away from it and pass on" (vv14–15).** Six commands in two verses, pressing the same point. The safest distance from the path of the wicked is never to step onto it at all. JFB stresses avoiding the beginnings of evil.
- **"They cannot sleep unless they have done wrong" (v16).** The wicked lie awake scheming (Ps 36:4; Mic 2:1). Evil has become their appetite: **"they eat the bread of wickedness and drink the wine of violence"** (v17). This may mean they live on what they steal, or that wickedness has become their daily food. JFB allows both: "Their bread is wickedness, their drink violence."
- **"The path of the righteous is like the light of dawn, which shines brighter and brighter until full day" (v18).** This is one of the loveliest pictures in the book. Dawn starts faint and grows steadily to noon. "Full day" is literally "the day being established," high noon. The life of the righteous is not perfect at the start, but it grows in light. Paul describes believers being "transformed… from one degree of glory to another" (2 Cor 3:18). The word for "light of dawn" (*nogah*) is a bright shining light. Some teachers press it to mean light without any cloud or shadow, but the lexicon gives simply "brightness."
- **"The way of the wicked is like deep darkness; they do not know over what they stumble" (v19).** "Deep darkness" (*ʾafelah*) is thick, total darkness. The wicked stumble and cannot even see what tripped them. Jesus: "If anyone walks in the night, he stumbles, because the light is not in him" (John 11:10).
- **"My son, be attentive to my words; incline your ear" (v20).** The third talk walks through the body: ear (v20), eyes (v21), heart (v21, 23), flesh (v22), mouth and lips (v24), eyes again (v25), feet (vv26–27). Wisdom involves the whole person.
- **"They are life to those who find them, and healing to all their flesh" (v22).** As in 3:8, the father's words bring wholeness to body and soul.
- **"Keep your heart with all vigilance" (v23).** "With all vigilance" can also be read "above all that you guard" (*mikkol mishmar*). JFB offers "above" or "more than all." Both point the same way. Of everything worth protecting, the heart comes first. In Hebrew the heart is the center of thought, will and desire, the control room of a life.
- **"For from it flow the springs of life" (v23).** "Springs" (*totsaʾot*) means outlets or sources, where something comes out. Everything a person says and does flows out of the heart. Jesus teaches the same: "Out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks" (Luke 6:45), and "out of the heart come evil thoughts, murder, adultery" (Matt 15:19). This is why the heart must be guarded. It is also why outward rules alone cannot make someone good. God's promise is a new heart (Ezek 36:26).
- **"Put away from you crooked speech" (v24).** After the heart comes the mouth, because words flow from the heart. "Crooked speech" is twisted talk, and "devious talk" means words that turn aside from the truth.
- **"Let your eyes look directly forward" (v25).** The eyes are to look straight ahead, not wander to the side where temptation waits. The next chapter's warning about the adulteress is close at hand. Henry: "Let the eye be fixed and not wandering."
- **"Ponder the path of your feet" (v26).** "Ponder" (*palles*) may mean to weigh carefully or to make level. Hebrews 12:13 echoes the Greek version of this verse: "make straight paths for your feet."
- **"Do not swerve to the right or to the left" (v27).** This is the language Moses and Joshua used for keeping God's law (Deut 5:32; Josh 1:7). The wise walk a straight road.
- **Commentators:** Henry: "we must keep our hearts with more care and diligence than we keep any thing else." On the mouth, he says "all manner of tongue sins" must be put away "by constant watchfulness and stedfast resolution." Guzik notes that Solomon's fall in later life was all the more blameworthy given such careful early training. He follows Bruce Waltke in reading "get" (*qanah*) as buying, which the lexicon supports: wisdom costs something and is worth the price. He also likens the heart to a reservoir. If it is polluted, cleaning up outward behavior will not fix the water. JFB says the father was "specially regarded, and so called tender," and that the course of the just "increases in purity." On v23 he writes that the heart is "the depository of all wisdom and the source of whatever affects life and character."

#### Pointing to Christ
A grandfather teaches a father who teaches a son. The chain of wisdom runs through David's house to the true Son of David, who said, "I do nothing on my own authority, but speak just as the Father taught me" (John 8:28). He is "the light of the world," and whoever follows him "will not walk in darkness, but will have the light of life" (John 8:12). The path of the righteous that grows brighter to full day ends in the city where "night will be no more" (Rev 22:5). The heart that must be guarded is also the heart God renews by his Spirit (Ezek 36:26), so that the springs of life flow from Christ himself (John 7:38).
