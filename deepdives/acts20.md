*Setting:* About AD 56–57. Paul leaves Ephesus after the riot and makes a final loop through his churches before heading for Jerusalem. Luke's "we" returns in v5, so Luke is traveling with him again and writing as an eyewitness. Most of Acts shows Paul the evangelist. This chapter shows Paul the pastor.

#### Paul in Macedonia and Greece (vv1–6)
- **"After encouraging them" (vv1–2).** Paul revisits Philippi, Thessalonica and Berea (chs 16–17). He writes 2 Corinthians from Macedonia after Titus brings news from Corinth (2 Cor 7:5–7). During the **three months in Greece** (v3), most likely at Corinth, he writes **Romans**.
- **The collection.** Luke barely mentions it (Paul does at 24:17), but the whole trip is about carrying a relief gift from the Gentile churches to poor believers in Jerusalem (Rom 15:25–28; 2 Cor 8–9).
- **"A plot was made against him" (v3).** A ship crowded with Jewish pilgrims sailing for Passover would have been an easy place for a murder. Paul changes plans and goes the long way, overland.
- **The traveling team (v4).** Seven men from four regions: Berea, Thessalonica, Derbe and Asia. Most likely each church sent its own delegate with its share of the money, which kept everything accountable (2 Cor 8:20–21). Several reappear: Aristarchus was in the riot (19:29) and later sails to Rome (27:2). Tychicus carries Ephesians and Colossians (Eph 6:21; Col 4:7). Trophimus, an Ephesian, will unknowingly spark the Jerusalem riot (21:29).
- **"After the days of Unleavened Bread" (v6).** Paul keeps Passover week at Philippi, then hurries toward Jerusalem to make Pentecost, fifty days later (v16).
- **Commentators:** Guzik and JFB both see the seven as church representatives carrying the gift and vouching for Paul's honest handling of it. Guzik calls the collection a symbol of unity, a way for Gentile converts to recognize their debt to the Jerusalem church. JFB identifies Sopater with the Sosipater of Romans 16:21. Henry, on the companions waiting at Troas: "We should not think it hard to stay awhile for good company in a journey."

#### Eutychus Raised from the Dead (vv7–12)
- **"On the first day of the week… to break bread" (v7).** One of the clearest early signs that Christians gathered on **Sunday**, resurrection day (John 20:19; 1 Cor 16:2; Rev 1:10). "Break bread" most likely means the Lord's Supper together with a shared meal (2:42, 46). Sunday was an ordinary workday, so they met in the evening.
- **"Prolonged his speech until midnight" (v7).** The verb is *dialegomai*, "reasoning, discussing." It was more of a long teaching conversation than a lecture. Paul was leaving the next day and might never see them again.
- **"Many lamps in the upper room" (v8).** An eyewitness detail. Oil lamps, a packed room, warm air and a late hour make it no surprise that someone dozed off.
- **Eutychus (v9).** The name means "Lucky" or "Good Fortune," a gentle irony. He is a *neanias*, a young man, possibly a slave or laborer who had already worked a full day. He sat in the window for air, fell asleep, dropped three stories and was "**taken up dead**." Luke, a physician (Col 4:14), says *dead*, not "seemed dead."
- **"Bent over him, and taking him in his arms" (v10).** This echoes Elijah (1 Kings 17:21) and Elisha (2 Kings 4:34) stretching themselves over a dead boy. Peter raised Tabitha (9:40), and now Paul raises Eutychus, so the two apostles' ministries mirror each other. "His life is in him" most naturally means it has returned.
- **Back upstairs to eat and talk until daybreak (v11).** Luke tells it almost casually: a resurrection, then supper, then more teaching. "Not a little comforted" (v12) is Luke's understatement for overjoyed.
- **Commentators:** All three read this as a real death and a real raising. Guzik, following Marshall, argues Luke would not record a merely apparent death. JFB ties Paul's embrace to Elisha (2 Kings 4:34). It sees in the gathering proof that the Lord's Day was already the church's fixed practice, and suggests the many lamps added heat and drowsiness. Guzik notes the evening meeting suggests Sunday was still a working day.

#### From Troas to Miletus (vv13–16)
- **"Intending himself to go by land" (v13).** Paul walks about 20 miles to Assos while the others sail around the cape. Luke does not say why. It may have been time alone with God before Jerusalem, or one more visit on the way.
- **Island-hopping (vv14–15).** Mitylene, Chios, Samos and Miletus are the real day-stops of a coasting ship that anchored each night. Details like these are why historians take Luke's travel narratives seriously.
- **"Sail past Ephesus" (v16).** One stop there and he would never get away in time. Miletus is about 30 miles south, so the elders would need several days to travel down and back.
- **Commentators:** Guzik notes Paul was not snubbing the Ephesians, just keeping to a deadline. JFB notes the wording of v16 suggests he was genuinely unsure he would arrive in time.

#### Paul Speaks to the Ephesian Elders (vv17–38)
- **The only speech in Acts to Christian leaders.** It sounds the most like Paul's letters. It is a farewell sermon to pastors in four movements: his past (vv18–21), his present (vv22–27), their future (vv28–31) and his example (vv32–35).
- **"With all humility and with tears and with trials" (v19).** He does not start with his results but with his character, which they had watched for three years.
- **"I did not shrink from declaring… anything that was profitable" (v20).** The verb *hypostellō* means to draw back or hold back out of fear. It returns in v27. He taught "in public and from house to house": preaching plus personal care.
- **"Repentance toward God and of faith in our Lord Jesus Christ" (v21).** His whole message in a line, the same for Jews and Greeks.
- **"Constrained by the Spirit" (v22).** Literally "*bound* (*dedemenos*) by the Spirit." In ch 21 Agabus will tie his own hands to show Paul bound literally. Paul is bound inwardly before he is bound outwardly.
- **"I do not account my life of any value… if only I may finish my course" (v24).** The runner's word (*dromos*). About ten years later he writes: "I have finished the race" (2 Tim 4:7).
- **"Innocent of the blood of all" (v26).** Watchman language from Ezekiel 3:17–21 and 33:1–9. The watchman who warns has done his duty, and the one who stays silent carries the guilt.
- **"The whole counsel of God" (v27).** *Boulē* is God's full plan and purpose, including the parts people would rather not hear.
- **"Pay careful attention to yourselves and to all the flock" (v28).** The order matters: *yourselves first*. A leader who is not guarding his own soul cannot guard anyone else's (compare 1 Tim 4:16, written to Timothy in Ephesus).
- **Three words, one office.** The men called "**elders**" (*presbyteroi*, v17) are made "**overseers**" (*episkopoi*, v28, the source of "episcopal" and "bishop") to "**care for**" the church (*poimainō*, to shepherd, the source of "pastor"). Most scholars see elder, overseer and shepherd as one office in the New Testament. Later church history distinguished a bishop from the elders. Anglican, Catholic and Orthodox traditions keep that distinction and argue it developed legitimately. Many Protestant traditions return to the New Testament pattern.
- **"Which he obtained with his own blood" (v28).** The only place in Acts where Luke directly says Christ's blood purchased the church. The ESV footnotes two textual questions. Some manuscripts read "church of **the Lord**," and the Greek can also mean "with the blood of **his own** [Son]." Either way the meaning holds: the church cost God the death of Christ, and it belongs to him, not to its elders.
- **"Fierce wolves… and from among your own selves" (vv29–30).** Danger from outside and from inside, and it came true. By 1 Timothy false teachers had risen in Ephesus (1 Tim 1:3–7, 19–20). By Revelation 2:2–4 Jesus commends Ephesus for testing false apostles but warns it has "abandoned the love you had at first."
- **"For three years… night or day… with tears" (v31).** His total time in Ephesus (19:8, 10 and beyond).
- **"I commend you to God and to the word of his grace" (v32).** Paul is leaving, but God and his word are not. That is the true foundation, not any one leader.
- **"These hands ministered to my necessities" (v34).** Picture him holding up hands calloused from tentmaking. He took no salary from them, so no one could accuse him of preaching for profit (1 Thess 2:9).
- **"It is more blessed to give than to receive" (v35).** The only saying of Jesus quoted in Acts, and it appears in none of the four Gospels. The Gospels do not record everything Jesus said (John 21:25).
- **"He knelt down and prayed with them all" (v36).** Standing was the normal posture for prayer, so kneeling shows deep earnestness (Luke 22:41). These men weep openly, embrace and kiss him (v37). What hurt most was "that they would not see his face again" (v38).
- **Commentators:** Guzik reads "house to house" as pointing to house churches, each perhaps overseen by an elder, which is reasonable but not stated in the text. He warns against preachers who tell audiences only what they want to hear. Through John Stott he defines shepherding first as leading the flock to pasture and feeding it. Henry on v24: Paul would rather give up his life than lose God's favor. On v28 he explains "blood of God" by the close union of Christ's divine and human natures. On the parting: "we can scarcely read the account… with dry eyes," and he likens Paul's prayer to Jesus praying with his disciples in John 17. JFB agrees elders and overseers are one office, dating a separate bishop to the second century. Unlike the ESV, JFB prefers the reading "Church of the Lord" in v28. Most modern textual critics read "God," as the ESV does. This is a real disagreement, held calmly. JFB links the wolves to early Gnostic teaching and the insiders to Judaizers.

#### Pointing to Christ
Paul shepherds like the Good Shepherd he serves. He gives himself, warns of wolves (John 10:12), prays before parting (John 17), and sets his face toward Jerusalem knowing suffering waits there (Luke 9:51). The flock he hands over is Christ's, bought "with his own blood."
