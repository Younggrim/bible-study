*Setting:* Chronicles was written after the exile, around 450–400 BC, for Jews rebuilding life in a small Persian province around Jerusalem. Chapter 1 traced the human race from Adam to Esau and set the side branches aside. Now the Chronicler turns to Israel itself. He lists the twelve sons of Jacob and then goes straight to Judah, ahead of the firstborn Reuben, because Judah is the tribe of David and the tribe most of his readers belonged to. The genealogy of Judah runs from 2:3 to 4:23, longer than any other tribe's. This chapter follows Judah's family down to David and then fans out into the clans and towns of Judah. It is a list of names, but it also gives its readers roots. Many of the town names here (Bethlehem, Hebron, Tekoa, Kiriath-jearim, Netophah) were places the returned exiles were living in again.

#### A Genealogy of David (vv1–55)
- **"These are the sons of Israel" (vv1–2).** The twelve sons, roughly in birth order with Leah's sons first, much as in Genesis 35:23–26. All twelve are named. Even though the northern tribes had been scattered by Assyria three centuries earlier, the Chronicler keeps all Israel in view. His book will keep insisting that God's people means all twelve tribes, not just Judah.
- **"Bath-shua the Canaanite" (v3).** The name means "daughter of Shua" (Gen 38:2). Judah married a Canaanite, against the pattern of Abraham and Isaac (Gen 24:3; 28:1). **"Er, Judah's firstborn, was evil in the sight of the LORD, and he put him to death."** The Chronicler does not skip the hard parts. This is the first death the book records, and it is a judgment.
- **"His daughter-in-law Tamar also bore him Perez and Zerah" (v4).** The whole messy story of Genesis 38 sits behind this line. Tamar was Er's widow, and when Judah failed to give her his son Shelah, she disguised herself and conceived by Judah himself. Judah admitted, "She is more righteous than I" (Gen 38:26). From that union came Perez, ancestor of David. Matthew names Tamar in the genealogy of Jesus (Matt 1:3), the first of four women whose stories were irregular.
- **The sons of Zerah (vv6–8).** **Ethan, Heman, Calcol and Dara** appear as famous wise men in 1 Kings 4:31, where Solomon was wiser than they ("Darda" there). Psalms 88 and 89 carry the names of Heman and Ethan "the Ezrahite," which probably means "descendant of Zerah." The Levite musicians named Heman and Ethan in 1 Chronicles 6 and 15 may be different men. Scholars disagree on how the names fit together.
- **"Achan, the troubler of Israel" (v7).** The Hebrew text spells his name "Achar," which means "trouble." In Joshua he is Achan, who stole from the plunder of Jericho that was "devoted" to God, and Israel lost at Ai because of it. He and his family were stoned in the Valley of Achor, "Trouble Valley" (Josh 7:24–26). The Chronicler's spelling turns his name into his record. **"Who broke faith"** is *maal*, to act unfaithfully toward God. It is one of the key words of Chronicles. It explains why Saul died (10:13) and why Judah went into exile (9:1). Here it appears at the very start of Judah's history, a warning set inside the honor roll.
- **"Jerahmeel, Ram, and Chelubai" (v9).** The three sons of Hezron give the outline for the rest of the chapter. Ram's line leads to David (vv10–17). Chelubai is another form of Caleb (vv18–24, 42–55). Jerahmeel's line comes in vv25–41.
- **The line from Ram to Jesse (vv10–12).** This is the same line that ends the book of Ruth (Ruth 4:18–22). **Nahshon, "prince of the sons of Judah"** (v10), led Judah in the wilderness (Num 1:7; 2:3), and his sister married Aaron (Exod 6:23). **Salmon** married Rahab of Jericho (Matt 1:5). **Boaz** married Ruth the Moabite, and their son **Obed** was David's grandfather. Two foreign women, a Canaanite and a Moabite, stand in the background of these five names.
- **"David the seventh" (v15).** 1 Samuel 16:10–11 and 17:12 say Jesse had eight sons. Chronicles lists seven, with David last. The usual explanations are that one son died young without children (JFB's view), or that the Chronicler wants David in the seventh place, the place of completeness. A brother named Elihu appears in 27:18, though that may be another name for Eliab. Samuel names only the first three brothers, so Chronicles is the only source for **Nethanel, Raddai and Ozem**.
- **"Their sisters were Zeruiah and Abigail" (vv16–17).** Only here do we learn that Joab, Abishai and Asahel were David's nephews through his sister. That explains much of 1 and 2 Samuel: these rough, loyal, dangerous men were family. **Amasa**, Absalom's general whom Joab murdered (2 Sam 20:10), was also David's nephew, through Abigail. 2 Samuel 17:25 calls Abigail "daughter of Nahash," which suggests she and Zeruiah were half-sisters of David, perhaps through their mother. **"Jether the Ishmaelite"** is "Ithra the Israelite" in the Hebrew of 2 Samuel 17:25. "Ishmaelite" is the more surprising reading and probably the original one. A foreigner married into David's family.
- **Caleb son of Hezron (vv18–20).** This Caleb lived generations before the famous spy Caleb son of Jephunneh (Num 13:6), and he is a different man. His grandson's grandson was **Bezalel**, the craftsman God filled with his Spirit to build the tabernacle (Exod 31:1–5). The Chronicler cares about worship, so the builder of the first sanctuary gets a mention. Solomon, the builder of the temple, will be his counterpart.
- **Jair's twenty-three cities (vv21–23).** Hezron married a woman of Manasseh, and their descendant Jair won towns in Gilead, east of the Jordan, called **Havvoth-jair**, "the tent villages of Jair" (Num 32:41; Deut 3:14). So a family of Judah was counted with Manasseh through its mother's side. Tribe lines were not as rigid as they look. **"Geshur and Aram took from them"** refers to a later loss of these towns to the Aramean kingdoms north and east of Gilead. When it happened is not said.
- **"Caleb went in to Ephrathah, the wife of Hezron his father" (v24).** The Hebrew of this verse is hard and reads literally "in Caleb-ephrathah." The ESV follows the Greek text, which makes Ephrathah Hezron's widow, whom Caleb then married. **"Ashhur, the father of Tekoa"** means the founder or leading man of the town of Tekoa, later the home of the prophet Amos (Amos 1:1). Throughout these lists "father of" a place usually means its founder or chief, as JFB notes.
- **The line of Jerahmeel (vv25–33).** The Jerahmeelites lived in the Negeb, the dry south of Judah. David, while living among the Philistines, claimed to raid the "Negeb of the Jerahmeelites" and later sent them gifts from his plunder (1 Sam 27:10; 30:29). The list is a careful record of a clan that might otherwise be forgotten. **"Seled died childless"** and **"Jether died childless"** (vv30, 32) are small lines of sadness. In a culture where a man lived on through his children, these were family lines that ended.
- **Sheshan and Jarha (vv34–41).** "Sheshan had no sons, only daughters." (v31 names a son, Ahlai, which may be a daughter's name, or a son who died young. The text does not explain.) So Sheshan gave a daughter in marriage to **Jarha, his Egyptian slave**. A slave from the land of Israel's bondage became the ancestor of a thirteen-generation line in Judah. The law allowed a family without sons to keep its inheritance through daughters (Num 27:1–11), and this marriage did the same. JFB thinks Jarha was first freed and adopted into Judah.
- **Caleb's descendants and towns (vv42–50a).** Many names here are towns in the hill country of Judah, south of Bethlehem: **Ziph** (where David hid from Saul, 1 Sam 23:14), **Mareshah**, **Hebron** (David's first capital), **Tappuah**, **Maon** (home of Nabal, 1 Sam 25:2), **Beth-zur**, **Madmannah**. The list is family tree and map at once. It tells who first settled each town.
- **"The daughter of Caleb was Achsah" (v49).** Achsah was the daughter of the later Caleb, son of Jephunneh, who gave her to Othniel, Israel's first judge, and gave her springs of water at her request (Josh 15:16–19; Judg 1:12–15). Her name seems to have been added here because both Calebs belong to the same clan area. How exactly the two Calebs connect is not clear, and commentators admit it.
- **The sons of Hur (vv50b–55).** Hur's family founded **Kiriath-jearim**, where the ark of the covenant stayed for twenty years before David brought it to Jerusalem (1 Sam 7:1–2; 1 Chr 13:5–6), and **Bethlehem** (v51), David's hometown and the birthplace of Jesus (Mic 5:2). **The Zorathites and the Eshtaolites** (v53) came from Zorah and Eshtaol, Samson's home ground (Judg 13:2, 25). **The Netophathites** (v54) produced two of David's mighty men (2 Sam 23:28–29).
- **"The clans also of the scribes who lived at Jabez… the Kenites" (v55).** The Kenites were a non-Israelite clan, related to Moses' father-in-law, who came into the land with Judah (Judg 1:16). Jael, who killed Sisera, was the wife of a Kenite (Judg 4:17). **"The house of Rechab"** became famous in Jeremiah's day for refusing wine and keeping a nomadic life out of loyalty to their ancestor's command (Jer 35). Jeremiah held them up as an example of faithfulness for Judah. Like Jarha the Egyptian and Jether the Ishmaelite, these outsiders are recorded as part of Judah.
- **Commentators:** Guzik observes that the line that leads to David runs through people with dark stories, Er, Judah and Tamar, and Achan, so that God's purposes clearly move forward through flawed people, not because of them. He notes that "unfaithfulness" is the Chronicler's favorite explanation for disaster, and that Zeruiah and Abigail's place in the family is known only from this list. JFB reads "Achar" as deliberate: "Achar means 'troubler.'" On David as seventh of eight sons, JFB infers that one "had died at an early age, without leaving issue." JFB thinks Jarha was adopted "into the tribe of Judah," and calls the English of the Caleb verses "confused," suggesting a copying error in v50. Henry calls the chapter "the register of the children of Israel," a people meant "to dwell alone, and not be reckoned among the nations," and adds that now "all are welcome to his salvation who come to him." The Tyndale notes agree that Caleb son of Hezron is a different man from Caleb son of Jephunneh.

#### Pointing to Christ
This chapter is the backbone of the family tree Matthew opens his Gospel with: Judah, Perez and Zerah by Tamar, Hezron, Ram, Amminadab, Nahshon, Salmon, Boaz, Obed, Jesse and David (Matt 1:3–6). The same list that keeps the record of Achan the troubler, a Canaanite wife, an Egyptian slave and an Ishmaelite in-law also leads straight to Bethlehem. The line of David's ancestors was full of sin and of outsiders, and God brought the Son of David through it anyway. He came for exactly such people.
