*Setting:* The plains of Moab and Mount Nebo, at the end of the forty years in the wilderness, around the 15th or 13th century BC depending on how the Exodus is dated. Moses has given his last speeches, written the law, taught Israel the song, commissioned Joshua and blessed the tribes. In 32:48–52 God told him to climb the mountain, see the land, and die. This short chapter tells how he did. It closes Deuteronomy and the whole Torah, the five books of Moses, and it ties Genesis to Joshua: the land promised to Abraham is in view, and the people are ready to go in. Most readers, ancient and modern, think someone other than Moses wrote this account of his death. Jewish tradition suggested Joshua. Others suggest a later inspired editor. The phrase "to this day" (v6) suggests some time had passed.

#### The Death of Moses (vv1–12)
- **"Then Moses went up from the plains of Moab to Mount Nebo, to the top of Pisgah" (v1).** Nebo is a peak of the Abarim range, about 2,600 feet high, directly east of the north end of the Dead Sea. Pisgah seems to be the name of the ridge or headland it belongs to. Moses climbs alone, at 120 years old. His life began beside a river and is ending on a mountain. Sinai was the mountain of the law. Nebo is the mountain of the promise seen from far off.
- **"And the LORD showed him all the land, Gilead as far as Dan" (v1).** God himself is the guide, pointing out the land in a sweep from north to south. **Gilead** is the hill country east of the Jordan. **Dan** is in the far north near Mount Hermon, a name the place would only get later, when the tribe of Dan moved there (Judg 18:29). That detail is one sign this chapter was written or updated after Moses' time.
- **"All Naphtali, the land of Ephraim and Manasseh, all the land of Judah as far as the western sea" (v2).** Moses sees the tribes' future territories, the land of the blessings he had just spoken (ch 33). "The western sea" is the Mediterranean, about 60 miles away and not usually visible from Nebo. Some take this as a miraculous view. JFB thinks clear air and height are enough, and that Moses saw toward these regions rather than every part of them. Either way, God "showed" it to him.
- **"The Negeb, and the Plain… the Valley of Jericho the city of palm trees, as far as Zoar" (v3).** The Negeb is the dry south. Jericho, an oasis city with springs and palms, lay right across the river below. Zoar was at the south end of the Dead Sea, the little town Lot fled to when Sodom was destroyed (Gen 19:20–23).
- **"This is the land of which I swore to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob, 'I will give it to your offspring'" (v4).** The promise of Genesis 12:7 is about to be kept, some six centuries after Abraham first walked the land. **"I have let you see it with your eyes, but you shall not go over there."** Moses had begged to cross (3:23–26). God said no, but gave him this sight. Hebrews says the patriarchs "died in faith, not having received the things promised, but having seen them and greeted them from afar" (Heb 11:13). Moses joins them.
- **"So Moses the servant of the LORD died there in the land of Moab" (v5).** Of all his titles, the narrator chooses "the servant of the LORD." It is the title God gives him (Num 12:7; Josh 1:2), and later it is used of Joshua, David, and the coming Servant of Isaiah 42–53. **"According to the word of the LORD"** is literally "at the mouth of the LORD." Jewish tradition took this to mean God drew out Moses' life with a kiss. The plain meaning is that he died exactly when and how God had said.
- **"And he buried him in the valley in the land of Moab opposite Beth-peor" (v6).** The Hebrew says "he buried him," and the most natural subject is the LORD. Some take it as "he was buried," and Guzik allows that God may have used people such as Joshua or Eleazar. But the text points to a unique honor: God himself laid his servant to rest. **Beth-peor** was the place of Israel's sin with the Baal of Peor (Num 25; Deut 4:3). Moses was buried facing the very place of Israel's shame.
- **"But no one knows the place of his burial to this day" (v6).** No tomb became a shrine. Israel was not to worship Moses, or Moses' bones. Jude 9 mentions an old tradition that the archangel Michael disputed with the devil "about the body of Moses." Jude uses it to teach humility in dealing with spiritual powers ("The Lord rebuke you"), without explaining the story. The details probably come from a Jewish writing called the Assumption of Moses, which survives only in part.
- **"Moses was 120 years old when he died. His eye was undimmed, and his vigor unabated" (v7).** "Vigor" is *leach*, "freshness, moisture," the sap of a living tree. Moses did not die of old age or illness. The Tyndale notes put it well, in effect: he did not fail to enter the land because he died; he died because he could not enter the land. In 31:2 he said he could no longer "go out and come in," which most take to mean his role as leader was over, not that his body had failed. Genesis 6:3 had set 120 years as a limit on human life, and Moses lived to it.
- **"And the people of Israel wept for Moses in the plains of Moab thirty days" (v8).** The usual mourning period was seven days (Gen 50:10). Israel mourned thirty days for Aaron (Num 20:29) and for Moses. The people who had grumbled against him for forty years now grieved him. **"Then the days of weeping and mourning for Moses were ended."** Grief has its time, and then the work goes on. Joshua 1 begins, "After the death of Moses…"
- **"And Joshua the son of Nun was full of the spirit of wisdom, for Moses had laid his hands on him" (v9).** Moses had laid hands on Joshua in front of the priest and the people (Num 27:18–23), and God gave him the wisdom he needed. "Spirit of wisdom" is the same phrase used of the skilled craftsmen who built the tabernacle (Exod 28:3). Joshua's skill would be leadership: war, organization and dividing the land. **"So the people of Israel obeyed him and did as the LORD had commanded Moses."** At least for now, they followed. Joshua is not a second Moses. He is a faithful servant carrying out what God gave through Moses.
- **"There has not arisen a prophet since in Israel like Moses, whom the LORD knew face to face" (v10).** This verse looks back from some time later and says no prophet since had equalled him. "Face to face" means direct, personal speech, not literally seeing God's face, which even Moses could not do (Exod 33:20–23). God spoke with other prophets in visions and dreams, but with Moses "mouth to mouth, clearly, and not in riddles" (Num 12:6–8). Moses himself had promised that God would raise up "a prophet like me from among you" (18:15–18). This verse says that prophet had not yet come. So Israel kept waiting. In Jesus' day people still asked, "Are you the Prophet?" (John 1:21; 6:14).
- **"None like him for all the signs and the wonders… and for all the mighty power and all the great deeds of terror" (vv11–12).** The plagues in Egypt, the Red Sea, the manna, the water from the rock. **"In the sight of all Israel"**: the last words of the Torah. The phrase "mighty power" (literally "strong hand") is the language used throughout Deuteronomy for what *God* did in the Exodus (4:34; 26:8). The deeds were God's, done through Moses.
- **Commentators:** Henry sees Moses' climb as a sign he was willing to die, and he compares Moses' acceptance of God's will to Christ in Gethsemane: Christ prayed for the cup to pass, yet "acquiesced with, Father, thy will be done." On v10 he writes: "Moses was greater than any other prophet of the Old Testament. But our Lord Jesus went beyond him, far more than the other prophets came short of him." JFB thinks the chapter "appears from internal evidence to have been written subsequently to the death of Moses," perhaps once as an introduction to Joshua. He suggests the grave was hidden so it would not become an object of "idolatrous veneration," and he says Joshua succeeded Moses not as prophet or lawgiver but as military leader of the conquest. Guzik thinks Joshua or Eleazar most likely wrote the chapter. He suggests God kept Moses' body for a later purpose, fulfilled at the transfiguration, and he mentions the view that Moses and Elijah are the two witnesses of Revelation 11, a view others do not share. He also says being "the servant of the LORD" is a higher title than prince, prophet or miracle worker.

#### Pointing to Christ
The Torah ends with a man on a mountain, seeing the promised land and not going in, and with a line that leaves the door open: "there has not arisen a prophet since in Israel like Moses." The New Testament says that prophet has come. Hebrews says, "Moses was faithful in all God's house as a servant… but Christ is faithful over God's house as a son" (Heb 3:5–6). Moses saw the Lord's glory only from behind (Exod 33:23). Jesus is "the radiance of the glory of God" (Heb 1:3), and "no one has ever seen God; the only God, who is at the Father's side, he has made him known" (John 1:18). Moses finally did stand in the land, on the mount of transfiguration, talking with Jesus about the "departure" (literally *exodos*) he would accomplish at Jerusalem (Luke 9:30–31). Moses led Israel out of Egypt, but his exodus ended outside the land. Jesus' exodus, through death and resurrection, brings his people all the way home. And it was Joshua, *Yehoshua*, whose name in Greek is Jesus, who led Israel in.
