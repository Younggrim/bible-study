*Setting:* Isaiah 40–55 speaks to Judeans who would be exiles in Babylon in the 500s BC. Chapter 44 ended by naming Cyrus, the Persian king God would use to bring them home. Chapter 45 continues straight on with God speaking to Cyrus himself. Cyrus the Great founded the Persian Empire in the 550s BC, conquered Media and Lydia (with its famously rich king Croesus), and took Babylon in October 539 BC. The next year he issued the decree that let the Jews return and rebuild the temple (Ezra 1:1–4). The chapter moves from Cyrus outward, past those who question God's choice of a pagan king, to an invitation to the whole world: "Turn to me and be saved, all the ends of the earth." Its last verses are quoted twice by Paul, once about Jesus.

#### Cyrus, God's Instrument (vv1–13)
- **"Thus says the LORD to his anointed, to Cyrus" (v1).** "Anointed" is *mashiach*, the word behind "Messiah." It was used of Israel's kings and priests, men set apart with oil for God's work (1 Sam 16:13). Only here is it used of a foreign king. It does not mean Cyrus was a believer or *the* Messiah. It means God set him apart for a specific task. A pagan emperor carries a title meant for David's line, which shows that God uses whom he wills.
- **"Whose right hand I have grasped, to subdue nations before him" (v1).** God took Israel's right hand in 41:13. Here he takes the conqueror's hand and leads him. Cyrus's campaigns were famously fast and successful.
- **"To loose the belts of kings, to open doors before him that gates may not be closed" (v1).** "Loose the belts" means to strip kings of their strength. A belted robe meant readiness for action, an unbelted one helplessness. Daniel describes Belshazzar on the night Babylon fell: "his limbs gave way, and his knees knocked together" (Dan 5:6). The Hebrew there uses an idiom close to this one. Babylon was famous for its walls and gates, but Babylonian records say Cyrus's army entered the city without a battle.
- **"I will break in pieces the doors of bronze and cut through the bars of iron" (v2).** Herodotus says Babylon had a hundred gates of bronze. Psalm 107:16 uses the same words of God freeing prisoners.
- **"I will give you the treasures of darkness and the hoards in secret places" (v3).** Ancient kings stored gold in hidden vaults. Cyrus captured the treasuries of Lydia and Babylon, some of the richest in the world. Later writers such as Pliny tell of enormous amounts of gold. The purpose is stated: "that you may know that it is I, the LORD… who call you by your name."
- **"For the sake of my servant Jacob… I name you, though you do not know me" (v4).** The great empire-builder was a tool in God's hand for the sake of a small, defeated people in exile. "Though you do not know me" is repeated in v5. Cyrus's own inscription, the Cyrus Cylinder, credits his victory to Marduk, Babylon's chief god. His decree in Ezra 1:2 credits "the LORD, the God of heaven." Most scholars think he was a shrewd ruler honoring each people's god. Either way, God used him without his knowing the God who used him (Prov 21:1).
- **"I am the LORD, and there is no other" (vv5–6).** The phrase is repeated three times in vv5–6. The goal is "that people may know, from the rising of the sun and from the west," that is, across the whole world.
- **"I form light and create darkness; I make well-being and create calamity" (v7).** "Well-being" is *shalom* and "calamity" is *ra'*, which can mean moral evil but here, opposite *shalom*, means disaster or trouble. The KJV's "I create evil" has confused many readers. The point is not that God is the author of sin (Jas 1:13). It is that nothing happens outside his rule, good times or hard (Amos 3:6; Lam 3:38). Persians are often said to have believed in two rival powers, one good and one evil, though Persian religion in Cyrus's day is not well known. Isaiah allows no rival god of darkness. There is one God, and he rules light and darkness alike.
- **"Shower, O heavens, from above, and let the clouds rain down righteousness" (v8).** Suddenly the prophet bursts into a prayer-like song. Righteousness and salvation are pictured as rain falling from above and crops sprouting from below. They "bear fruit" together. God's saving work and his putting things right always grow side by side. This verse became a well-known Advent text in the Latin church, *Rorate caeli*, "Drop down, ye heavens."
- **"Woe to him who strives with him who formed him, a pot among earthen pots!" (v9).** Some in Israel evidently objected: a pagan king as God's anointed? Isaiah answers with the potter and the clay (29:16; Jer 18:1–6). A pot does not tell the potter what to make, or complain, "Your work has no handles." Paul uses the same image (Rom 9:20–21).
- **"Woe to him who says to a father, 'What are you begetting?'" (v10).** A child cannot demand of its parents why it was born this way. Even less can creatures demand that God explain himself.
- **"Ask me of things to come; will you command me concerning my children?" (v11).** The ESV reads this as a rebuke: are you going to give me orders about my own children? Some translations, and JFB, read it as an invitation: ask me, and even "command" me in prayer. The Hebrew can be read either way. The context of vv9–10 favors the ESV's rebuke.
- **"I have stirred him up in righteousness… he shall build my city and set my exiles free, not for price or reward" (v13).** Cyrus would release the Jews without being paid to. Isaiah later says the same of God's own redemption: "you shall be redeemed without money" (52:3).
- **Commentators:** Guzik notes the irony that all these victories were for the sake of "little Israel." He reports Josephus's claim that Cyrus read this prophecy and was moved to fulfill it, which is Josephus's story and not something the text says. Henry says God could have given this power to a Jew, but "the wealth and power of this world God has seldom seen fit to entrust" to his own people. On v7 JFB is clear that "evil" means "war, disaster," not moral evil, and Guzik agrees: God is not the author of evil, though he rules over it and can use it. JFB reads v9–10 as aimed first at Jews offended by Cyrus, and finally at those offended that God would adopt the Gentiles.

#### The LORD, the Only Savior (vv14–25)
- **"The wealth of Egypt and the merchandise of Cush, and the Sabeans, men of stature" (v14).** These are the same African nations as in 43:3. Herodotus described the Sabeans and Cushites as tall. The picture is of the nations coming over to Israel. "In chains" is surprising, and probably means they bind themselves willingly, coming as servants. Their confession is the point: "Surely God is in you, and there is no other." The goal is not Israel ruling the world but the world finding Israel's God.
- **"Truly, you are a God who hides himself, O God of Israel, the Savior" (v15).** This is one of the most quoted verses in the chapter. Who says it, and what does it mean? It may be the nations' confession, or the prophet's own response. God's ways are hidden: he worked through exile, a pagan king and long silence, and no one could have guessed his plan. Yet the hidden God is "the Savior." Christian writers from Luther to Pascal saw here the God revealed in an unexpected place, most of all in the weakness of the cross.
- **"The makers of idols go in confusion… But Israel is saved by the LORD with everlasting salvation" (vv16–17).** Two outcomes. Idol-makers are put to shame. Those whom God saves will "not be put to shame or confounded to all eternity." The word "everlasting" points past the return from Babylon, which was only partial and temporary, to something final.
- **"He did not create it empty, he formed it to be inhabited!" (v18).** "Empty" is *tohu*, the word for the formless earth of Genesis 1:2. God never meant the world to stay a void. He meant it to be full of life. Some have used this verse to argue for a "gap" between Genesis 1:1 and 1:2, in which a first creation was ruined. Most interpreters, Guzik included, think the verse cannot carry that weight. Its point is God's good purpose for the world.
- **"I did not speak in secret, in a land of darkness" (v19).** Pagan oracles spoke from dark shrines in riddles. God spoke openly through his prophets and his law. "I did not say… 'Seek me in vain.'" Those who seek him will find him (Jer 29:13). Jesus told the high priest, "I have spoken openly to the world… I have said nothing in secret" (John 18:20).
- **"You survivors of the nations… they have no knowledge who carry about their wooden idols" (v20).** "Survivors" are those left after Cyrus's wars. People carry idols, and the idols cannot carry them. Chapter 46 develops that image. They "keep on praying to a god that cannot save."
- **"A righteous God and a Savior; there is none besides me" (v21).** It is a remarkable pairing. One might expect a righteous God to condemn sinners, not save them. God is both. Paul gives the answer: at the cross God shows "that he might be just and the justifier of the one who has faith in Jesus" (Rom 3:26).
- **"Turn to me and be saved, all the ends of the earth!" (v22).** The KJV's "Look unto me" is well known. The Hebrew (*penu*) means to turn the face toward. It is an invitation to the whole world, not only to Israel. Salvation is not earned. It is received by turning to God. The young Charles Spurgeon was converted in 1850 when a lay preacher, in a small chapel on a snowy Sunday, preached on this verse and told him: "Look!" Jesus pointed to the bronze serpent that dying Israelites only had to look at to live (Num 21:8–9; John 3:14–15).
- **"By myself I have sworn… 'To me every knee shall bow, every tongue shall swear allegiance'" (v23).** God has no one greater to swear by, so he swears by himself (Heb 6:13). Paul quotes this verse twice. In Romans 14:11 it is about everyone standing before God's judgment. In Philippians 2:10–11 Paul says that at the name of *Jesus* "every knee should bow… and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord." Paul takes words spoken by the LORD, who says "there is no other," and applies them to Jesus. It is one of the clearest statements in the New Testament that Jesus shares the identity of Israel's one God.
- **"Only in the LORD… are righteousness and strength; to him shall come and be ashamed all who were incensed against him" (v24).** Some bow gladly, and some, who raged against him, bow in shame. Either way every knee bows.
- **"In the LORD all the offspring of Israel shall be justified and shall glory" (v25).** "Justified" is from *tsadaq*, to be in the right, declared righteous. Their righteousness is "in the LORD," not in themselves. Paul's teaching that believers have "a righteousness from God" (Phil 3:9) builds on texts like this. In context, "all the offspring of Israel" includes the people from "the ends of the earth" who turn to God (v22). That is why Paul can call believing Gentiles children of Abraham (Gal 3:7, 29).
- **Commentators:** Henry reads v15 as faith trusting God "further than they can see him." He notes that God sometimes "hides himself from them in displeasure," but never so that he stops being their Savior. JFB rejects an older reading of v15 as Egypt confessing God hidden in human form in Jesus, and reads it as the prophet marveling at God's dark counsels. On v22 JFB writes, "What so simple as a look? Not do something, but look to the Saviour." Guzik makes the same link to the bronze serpent and to Spurgeon's conversion. On v23 the Tyndale notes, JFB and Guzik all point to Romans 14:11 and Philippians 2:10–11, and JFB adds that "I have sworn by myself" equals "As I live," which is how Paul quotes it.

#### Pointing to Christ
God called a pagan king his "anointed" for one task: to set captives free and see the temple rebuilt. Cyrus never knew the God who held his hand. The true Anointed One knew the Father perfectly, freed captives from a far deeper prison, and was himself the temple (John 2:19–21). The "God who hides himself" was most hidden, and most fully revealed, at the cross, where he showed himself both "a righteous God and a Savior." Paul says that the knee that bows to the LORD bows to Jesus (Phil 2:10–11). Until that day the invitation stands for the whole world: turn to him and be saved.
