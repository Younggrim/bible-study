*Setting:* Moses has come down from his second forty days on Sinai with the new tablets and a shining face (34:29–35). The covenant broken by the golden calf has been renewed, and God has promised to go with his people. Now the building begins. Chapters 35–40 tell how the tabernacle described in chapters 25–31 was actually made. Much of it repeats the earlier chapters almost word for word, but with the commands ("you shall make") turned into reports ("he made"). The repetition is the point. The people who tried to make a god their own way with the calf now build God's dwelling exactly as he commanded, and the story ends with his glory filling it (40:34–35).

#### Sabbath Regulations (vv1–3)
- **"Moses assembled all the congregation" (v1).** The last time the whole people gathered, it was around Aaron to demand an idol (32:1). Now they gather around Moses to hear God's word.
- **"Six days work shall be done, but on the seventh day you shall have a Sabbath of solemn rest" (v2).** God's last words on the mountain about the building were about the Sabbath (31:12–17), and Moses' first words in the camp are the same. Before a single board is cut, the people are reminded that God's work must not crowd out God's rest. "Work" here is *melakah*, the same word used for God's work of creation that he finished and rested from (Gen 2:2–3) and for the tabernacle "work" throughout these chapters (35:21, 29, 35; 36:1–8). Even the holiest work stopped on the seventh day.
- **"You shall kindle no fire in all your dwelling places on the Sabbath day" (v3).** This is the only place the Pentateuch spells out this detail. In the context of building, it may have been aimed at the furnaces and forges needed for metalwork. Lighting a fire in the wilderness meant gathering fuel and working hard to start it, which is why a man gathering sticks on the Sabbath was judged (Num 15:32–36). Later Jewish practice read it broadly and prepared Sabbath meals and lamps before sunset on Friday.
- **Commentators:** Henry calls the Sabbath a day "in which a rest from all worldly labour must be very carefully and strictly observed," and reads the fire ban as forbidding fires "for any servile work, as smith's work." Guzik draws the point that work for God should flow out of rest in God, and that obedience comes before service. He distinguishes two Hebrew words for work, with *melakah* as the business-like work banned on the Sabbath. The lexicon does give *melakah* the sense of "occupation, business," but it is also the word used of God's own creation work, so the contrast should not be pressed hard.

#### Contributions for the Tabernacle (vv4–29)
- **"Take from among you a contribution to the LORD. Whoever is of a generous heart, let him bring" (v5).** This repeats the call of 25:1–7. Notice the contrast with the calf. Aaron demanded one thing, gold earrings, and did the work himself (32:2–4). Here God invites everyone to bring whatever they have, and many share in the work. "Generous heart" is literally a heart that is willing or noble. No tax was charged and no amount was set.
- **The materials (vv5–9).** Metals (gold, silver, bronze), dyed yarns and fine linen, goats' hair for the tent cloth, tanned rams' skins and goatskins for the coverings, acacia wood from the desert, oil, spices and gems. The list runs from the precious to the ordinary. Goats' hair mattered as much as gold, because the tent needed both.
- **"Let every skillful craftsman among you come and make all that the LORD has commanded" (v10).** Literally "every wise of heart." Gifts were needed and so were skills. Verses 11–19 list everything to be made, from the tabernacle itself down to **"the pegs… and their cords"** (v18). Nothing was too small to be named.
- **"All the congregation… departed from the presence of Moses" (v20).** There was no pressure, appeal or campaign. Moses told them what God wanted and sent them home to decide.
- **"Everyone whose heart stirred him, and everyone whose spirit moved him" (v21).** The language of willingness runs through the passage: "heart stirred" (vv21, 26), "spirit moved" (v21), "willing heart" (v22), "heart moved them" (v29), and "freewill offering" (v29). The giving began inside. Paul's words fit: "God loves a cheerful giver" (2 Cor 9:7).
- **"Both men and women… brooches and earrings and signet rings and armlets" (v22).** Much of this gold had come from the Egyptians (12:35–36). Some of it had gone into the calf. What was left now went to God's house. The Hebrew may suggest the men came "along with" or even "following" the women, which some take to mean the women led the way.
- **"Every skillful woman spun with her hands" (vv25–26).** Women brought not only gifts but skill. They spun the colored yarns and linen thread for the curtains and garments, and the goats' hair for the tent cloth. Their labor was as much an offering as the gold. Later we learn that women serving at the tent door gave their bronze mirrors for the basin (38:8).
- **"The leaders brought onyx stones and stones to be set… and spices and oil" (vv27–28).** The tribal leaders gave the most costly items, the gems for the high priest's ephod and breastpiece and the spices for the oil and incense. Those with more gave what only they could give.
- **"All the men and women… whose heart moved them… brought it as a freewill offering" (v29).** The section ends where it began, with willing hearts. The next chapter will report that they brought so much that Moses had to tell them to stop (36:5–7).
- **Commentators:** Henry says God was pleased with willing hearts rather than a burdensome tax. JFB notes that "no exciting harangues were made." The people trusted Moses' word and gave their best freely. JFB lists their motives: remembrance of their recent sin, love to God, longing for his presence, and gratitude, and comments that ornaments served as money in that age. Guzik points out that God could have supplied the materials by miracle but chose to work through gifts, which shaped a giving people. He also stresses that every part was needed, so goats' hair given with a right heart was as welcome as gold.

#### Construction of the Tabernacle (vv30–35)
- **"See, the LORD has called by name Bezalel" (v30).** Moses now tells the people what God told him privately (31:1–6). The builders' appointment comes from God, so no one can claim the honor for himself or resent it.
- **"He has filled him with the Spirit of God, with skill, with intelligence, with knowledge" (v31).** The same three gifts named in 31:3: wisdom, understanding and knowledge, the gifts by which God made the world (Prov 3:19–20).
- **"And he has inspired him to teach, both him and Oholiab" (v34).** This detail is new. Chapter 31 did not say it. Bezalel and Oholiab were gifted not only to do the work but to train others. A project of this size needed many hands, and those hands had to be taught. The best gifts multiply.
- **"Engraver… designer… embroiderer… weaver" (v35).** The list of crafts is wide: stonecutting, metalwork, woodwork, embroidery, weaving. Every kind of skilled worker had a place in building God's house.
- **Commentators:** JFB says the reminder of Bezalel's call came at just the right moment, when the materials were in and building was about to start, so that all would accept the leaders' authority. It also notes that skill in craftsmanship is a gift of God, and that Israel's women had likely learned weaving as bondwomen in Egyptian households. Guzik describes Bezalel and Oholiab as the project's general contractors, gifted by the Spirit to carry out a pattern God had already given (25:9).

#### Pointing to Christ
After the calf, the people built again, but this time in God's way, with God's gifts, by God's Spirit, from willing hearts. That is the pattern of the church. Jesus is building his house (Matt 16:18), and he gives gifts to his people, "to each one… according to the measure of Christ's gift" (Eph 4:7), so that every member, whether gold or goats' hair, has a part "for building up the body of Christ" (Eph 4:12). The Spirit who filled Bezalel now lives in every believer, and believers together "are being built together into a dwelling place for God by the Spirit" (Eph 2:22). The giving starts in the heart, because the God who receives it first gave his Son.
