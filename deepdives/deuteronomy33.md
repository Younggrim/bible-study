*Setting:* The plains of Moab, on the last day of Moses' life. In chapter 32 God told Moses to climb Mount Nebo and die. Before he goes, Moses does what Jacob did on his deathbed (Gen 49): he blesses the tribes of Israel one by one. The two blessings are worth reading side by side. Jacob's words to his sons included hard rebukes, for Reuben, Simeon and Levi especially. Four centuries later, Moses' words are almost all blessing, and Levi's curse has turned into honor. The chapter is a poem, framed by praise of the God who came from Sinai (vv2–5) and the God who "rides through the heavens" to help his people (vv26–29). After the warnings of the song in chapter 32, Moses' last word to Israel is blessing.

#### Moses' Final Blessing on Israel (vv1–5)
- **"Moses the man of God" (v1).** This is the first time in the Bible the title appears. It is used later of prophets like Samuel, Elijah and Elisha. Psalm 90 is headed "A Prayer of Moses, the man of God." A blessing from a dying father figure was believed to carry real weight. This one comes from God's prophet.
- **"The LORD came from Sinai and dawned from Seir upon us; he shone forth from Mount Paran" (v2).** Like the sun rising over the mountains. Seir is Edom, south-east of the Dead Sea, and Paran is the wilderness region south of Canaan. The Tyndale notes say this is not a travel route but a picture of God's glory blazing out over the wilderness where he led Israel. Deborah (Judg 5:4–5) and Habakkuk (Hab 3:3) use the same imagery.
- **"He came from the ten thousands of holy ones, with flaming fire at his right hand" (v2).** The "holy ones" are probably angels, God's heavenly army. Stephen and Paul both say the law was delivered through angels (Acts 7:53; Gal 3:19; Heb 2:2). The Hebrew of "flaming fire" is difficult. The older reading "a fiery law" (KJV) takes one word as *dat*, a Persian word for "law," but that is doubtful in such an old poem. Most now read it as fire or lightning, like the fire and smoke at Sinai (Exod 19:18).
- **"Yes, he loved his people… so they followed in your steps, receiving direction from you" (v3).** God's love comes first. Israel sits at his feet to be taught. The Hebrew here is also hard, but the meaning is clear: the God of fire is also the God who loves and teaches.
- **"When Moses commanded us a law, as a possession for the assembly of Jacob" (v4).** The law is described as Israel's inheritance, something precious to be handed down, not a burden. Jewish children still learn this verse among their first words of Scripture.
- **"Thus the LORD became king in Jeshurun" (v5).** "Jeshurun," "the upright one," is a poetic name for Israel (32:15). Some take "king" to be Moses, but most think it is the LORD, who became Israel's king at Sinai when the tribes gathered. Israel had a King long before it had Saul or David.
- **Commentators:** Henry reads the whole chapter as both prayer and prophecy, and says Moses "pronounces them all blessed in what God had done for them already, especially in giving them his law." JFB says God's majesty appeared as "a divine light" at Sinai and that the law was given "in kindness and love." Guzik, following Clarke, says a blessing from God's servant carried the promise of fulfillment.

#### Moses' Final Blessing on Israel (vv6–25)
- **"Let Reuben live, and not die, but let his men be few" (v6).** Reuben was Jacob's firstborn, but he lost his place by sleeping with his father's concubine (Gen 35:22; 49:3–4). Moses prays that the tribe will survive. It did, but it never became prominent. No judge, king or prophet came from Reuben, and its land east of the Jordan was exposed to raiders. Some read the last line as "and let not his men be few," which the Hebrew allows. The ESV's reading is the more natural one.
- **Simeon is missing.** Simeon is the only tribe not named. The tribe shrank the most of any during the wilderness years, from 59,300 to 22,200 (Num 1:23; 26:14), possibly because of the sin at Baal-peor, whose ringleader was a Simeonite (Num 25:14). In Canaan Simeon received towns inside Judah's land and was gradually absorbed (Josh 19:1–9). This fits Jacob's word that Simeon would be "scattered in Israel" (Gen 49:7). Some manuscripts of the Greek Old Testament add "and let Simeon be many in number" to v6, but that is almost certainly a later attempt to fill the gap. Even without Simeon, there are twelve tribes in the chapter, because Joseph is counted as two, Ephraim and Manasseh (v17).
- **"Hear, O LORD, the voice of Judah, and bring him in to his people" (v7).** A short prayer for Judah as a warrior tribe. **"Bring him in"** may mean bring him home safely from battle. Judah led Israel's march (Num 2:3; 10:14) and went first in the fighting after Joshua (Judg 1:1–2). Jacob had promised that the scepter would not depart from Judah (Gen 49:10). The kings of David's line, and in the end Jesus, "the Lion of the tribe of Judah" (Rev 5:5), came from here.
- **"Give to Levi your Thummim, and your Urim to your godly one" (v8).** The Urim and Thummim were objects kept in the high priest's breastpiece, used to ask God for guidance (Exod 28:30; Num 27:21). Their exact form and how they worked are unknown. The names probably mean "lights" and "perfections." **"Whom you tested at Massah… at the waters of Meribah"**: Massah and Meribah, "testing" and "quarreling," were where Israel demanded water (Exod 17:1–7; Num 20:1–13). Levi, through Moses and Aaron, was tested there with the rest.
- **"Who said of his father and mother, 'I regard them not'" (v9).** This recalls the golden calf. When Moses cried, "Who is on the LORD's side?" the Levites rallied to him and executed the idolaters, even their own relatives (Exod 32:26–29). Later Phinehas, a Levite, acted for God at Baal-peor (Num 25:7–13). Their loyalty to God came before family. Jacob had cursed Levi's anger (Gen 49:5–7). Now that same fierce zeal, turned toward God, became the reason for their honor. Jesus calls for the same first loyalty (Luke 14:26).
- **"They shall teach Jacob your rules… they shall put incense before you" (v10).** Levi had two jobs: to teach the law and to serve at the altar. Teaching comes first in the list. The priests' task was not only sacrifice but making God's word known (Lev 10:11; Mal 2:6–7). **Scattered among the tribes in forty-eight towns, the Levites were placed to teach everywhere** (Num 35:1–8). Jacob's "I will scatter them" became a gift to the whole nation.
- **"Crush the loins of his adversaries" (v11).** Korah's rebellion against Aaron (Num 16) showed that the priests would have enemies, sometimes inside Israel.
- **"The beloved of the LORD dwells in safety… and dwells between his shoulders" (v12).** Benjamin was Jacob's youngest and his "beloved" son by Rachel. The picture may be a father carrying a child on his back, or the LORD dwelling among Benjamin's hills. Jerusalem lay on the border of Benjamin and Judah (Josh 18:16, 28), and the temple stood on its ridge. Benjamin gave Israel its first king, Saul, and later the apostle Paul (Phil 3:5).
- **"Blessed by the LORD be his land" (v13).** Joseph receives the longest blessing, as in Genesis 49:22–26, which these verses echo. The word "choicest" (*meged*, "excellent things") is repeated five times in vv13–16: the best gifts of heaven (dew and rain), of the deep (springs), of the sun and the months (crops through the seasons), of the ancient hills, and of the earth. The central highlands of Ephraim and Manasseh were the most fertile hill country in the land.
- **"The favor of him who dwells in the bush" (v16).** The only place outside Exodus 3 where the burning bush is mentioned. The God who met Moses in the bush is the one whose favor rests on Joseph. **"Prince among his brothers"**: literally "the one set apart" (*nazir*, the same root as Nazirite), recalling how Joseph was separated from his brothers and then raised over them.
- **"A firstborn bull… his horns are the horns of a wild ox" (v17).** The bull and wild ox picture strength. "Wild ox" is *re'em*, an aurochs, now extinct. The KJV's "unicorn" came from the Greek translation. **"The ten thousands of Ephraim… the thousands of Manasseh"**: the younger son Ephraim comes first, as when Jacob crossed his hands to bless him (Gen 48:13–20). Ephraim became the leading northern tribe. Joshua was an Ephraimite (Num 13:8).
- **"Rejoice, Zebulun, in your going out, and Issachar, in your tents" (v18).** Two brother tribes in the north. "Going out" and "tents" mean their trade and home life. **"They shall call peoples to their mountain"** (v19), probably Mount Tabor, which stood on their borders. **"The abundance of the seas and the hidden treasures of the sand"** may point to sea trade and to the purple dye and glass made along that coast, as JFB suggests.
- **"Blessed be he who enlarges Gad! Gad crouches like a lion" (v20).** Gad settled east of the Jordan in good pasture land (Num 32). Its men were fierce fighters. David's Gadite warriors had "faces like the faces of lions" (1 Chr 12:8). **"With Israel he executed the justice of the LORD"** (v21): Gad kept its promise to cross the Jordan and fight beside the other tribes (Num 32:20–22; Josh 22:1–4).
- **"Dan is a lion's cub that leaps from Bashan" (v22).** Dan's first allotment was on the coast near the Philistines, but the tribe could not hold it, and part of it moved to the far north near Bashan, taking the town of Laish by surprise (Judg 18). It is a sad story. They set up an idol there, and Dan later became a center of the golden calf worship under Jeroboam (1 Kgs 12:29–30). Samson was a Danite. Dan is also missing from the list of tribes in Revelation 7:4–8.
- **"O Naphtali, sated with favor… possess the lake and the south" (v23).** "Lake" is *yam*, "sea" or "west." Naphtali's land ran along the west side of the Sea of Galilee. Isaiah promised that this region, "Galilee of the nations," would see a great light (Isa 9:1–2), and Matthew says that light dawned when Jesus made his home in Capernaum, in the land of Zebulun and Naphtali (Matt 4:13–16).
- **"Most blessed of sons be Asher… let him dip his foot in oil" (v24).** Asher's name means "happy" or "blessed." Its land in the western Galilee hills and along the coast was rich in olive groves, so much oil that one could bathe one's feet in it.
- **"Your bars shall be iron and bronze, and as your days, so shall your strength be" (v25).** "Bars" are the bolts of city gates. Asher's border lay toward Phoenicia, so it needed strong defenses. The word translated "strength" (*dove'*) occurs only here, and its meaning is uncertain. Some suggest "rest" or "security." The ESV's traditional rendering has comforted many: God gives strength day by day, enough for each day's need (compare 2 Cor 12:9).
- **Commentators:** Guzik and JFB both mention the Greek addition about Simeon. Guzik stresses that Simeon shrank more than any other tribe. He also notes that Levi's scattering became a blessing while Simeon's became loss, and he reads Dan's lion-cub blessing with some irony, given Dan's later idolatry. On Levi, Henry says the prayer "has its full accomplishment in Jesus Christ." On Benjamin, Henry and JFB agree that "between his shoulders" points to the temple on Mount Moriah, which Henry says lay in Benjamin's lot. Joshua 18:28 does list Jerusalem among Benjamin's towns, though Judah also lived there (Judg 1:21; Josh 15:63). On Asher's "as your days," Guzik follows Clarke and Spurgeon: strength given day by day, in proportion to need. JFB sees Asher's iron and bronze "shoes" (the KJV reading) as suited to a rocky coast "from Carmel to Sidon."

#### Moses' Final Blessing on Israel (vv26–29)
- **"There is none like God, O Jeshurun, who rides through the heavens to your help" (v26).** The tribes have been blessed one by one. Now Moses turns from the tribes to their God. Canaanite poems called Baal "the rider on the clouds." Moses says it is the LORD who truly rides the skies, and he rides to help his people (Ps 68:4, 33).
- **"The eternal God is your dwelling place, and underneath are the everlasting arms" (v27).** One of the best-loved verses in the Old Testament. "Dwelling place" (*me'onah*) is a home, a refuge, even a lair. Psalm 90, the only psalm credited to Moses, opens with the same word: "Lord, you have been our dwelling place in all generations." "Everlasting arms" pictures a father holding a child from underneath. No one can fall below them. The hymn "Leaning on the Everlasting Arms" (1887) takes its title from this verse.
- **"And he thrust out the enemy before you and said, 'Destroy'" (v27).** Security is not only rest but victory. God himself goes ahead into Canaan.
- **"So Israel lived in safety, Jacob lived alone" (v28).** "Alone" means set apart, secure, not dependent on other nations (compare Balaam's words in Num 23:9). **"Whose heavens drop down dew"**: the same picture Moses began his song with (32:2).
- **"Happy are you, O Israel! Who is like you, a people saved by the LORD" (v29).** "Happy" is *'ashre*, the same word that opens Psalm 1: "Blessed is the man." Israel's happiness is not its own strength but its God, **"the shield of your help, and the sword of your triumph."** **"You shall tread upon their backs"**: "backs" is *bamot*, which can also mean "high places," the hilltop shrines of Canaanite worship. Either way it pictures full victory. These are the last words of Moses recorded in the Bible.
- **Commentators:** Henry calls this a picture of Israel's security under God's protection, reading v27 as "the eternal God is thy refuge." JFB connects the God who "rideth upon the heaven" with the pillar of cloud and fire. Guzik quotes Spurgeon's point that no believer ever sinks lower than the everlasting arms, and on v29 that being "saved by the LORD" is a finished work.

#### Pointing to Christ
Moses' last words are blessing, not warning. That is fitting, because God's final word to his people is always blessing. The tribes Moses blessed point beyond themselves. From Judah came the King, the Lion of Judah who opens the scroll (Rev 5:5). Levi's role as priest and teacher, which Henry says finds "its full accomplishment in Jesus Christ," is completed in the one who is our great high priest and teacher (Heb 4:14–16). In the land of Zebulun and Naphtali the light of the gospel first shone (Matt 4:13–16). The "everlasting arms" are the arms of the Father that Jesus made known, from which no one can snatch his sheep (John 10:28–29). And "Happy are you, O Israel… a people saved by the LORD" becomes the song of everyone in Christ, blessed "with every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places" (Eph 1:3).
