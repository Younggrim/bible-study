*Setting:* The people have given more than enough (36:5–7), and the tent of the tabernacle, with its curtains, frames and veil, has been made (36:8–38). Now Bezalel makes the furniture that will stand inside: the ark and mercy seat for the Most Holy Place, and the table, lampstand and incense altar for the Holy Place. Every item was first described in God's instructions (25:10–40; 30:1–10, 22–38), and this chapter repeats those descriptions closely, changing "you shall make" to "he made." The repetition is deliberate. After the golden calf, Israel's worship is being built exactly according to the pattern God showed on the mountain (25:9, 40). This chapter is a list of things made, so the notes here focus on what each item was for and what it pointed to.

#### Making the Ark (vv1–9)
- **"Bezalel made the ark of acacia wood" (v1).** Bezalel is named here for the first time in the building account. The Tyndale notes suggest that "he made" means he was in charge and directed the other craftsmen, though as master craftsman he may well have made the most sacred pieces with his own hands. The ark was the first item God described (25:10) and the most important, the place where God would meet with Moses (25:22).
- **"Two cubits and a half… a cubit and a half… a cubit and a half" (v1).** About 45 inches long and 27 inches wide and high (113 by 68 by 68 cm), a wooden chest about the size of a large trunk. "Ark" here means a box or chest. It is a different Hebrew word from Noah's ark.
- **"Overlaid it with pure gold inside and outside" (v2).** Wood from the desert, covered in gold. Many Christian interpreters have seen in this a picture of Christ's humanity and deity together, though the text itself does not draw that line.
- **"Four rings of gold… poles… to carry the ark" (vv3–5).** The ark was carried on the shoulders of the Levites (Num 4:15; 7:9), never touched directly. When David tried moving it on a cart, Uzzah reached out to steady it and died (2 Sam 6:6–7), and only then did David return to the way God had prescribed (1 Chr 15:13–15).
- **"He made a mercy seat of pure gold" (v6).** "Mercy seat" is *kapporet*, from the same root as *kipper*, "to make atonement." It was the lid of the ark, the place where the high priest sprinkled blood on the Day of Atonement (Lev 16:14–15). William Tyndale's English Bible coined "mercy seat," following Luther's German. The Greek Old Testament calls it *hilastērion*, "place of atonement," and Paul uses that word of Christ, "whom God put forward as a propitiation by his blood" (Rom 3:25). Inside the ark lay the tablets of the law (25:16; 1 Kgs 8:9). The blood on the lid covered the broken law beneath.
- **"Two cherubim of gold… of hammered work… of one piece with the mercy seat" (vv7–8).** The cherubim were beaten out of the same slab of gold as the lid, not made separately and attached. Cherubim in Scripture are the mighty guardians of God's holiness (Gen 3:24; Ezek 1; 10). God is described as the one "enthroned on the cherubim" (1 Sam 4:4; Ps 80:1). The ark was his footstool (1 Chr 28:2), and the space above the cherubim was his throne.
- **"Their faces one to another; toward the mercy seat were the faces of the cherubim" (v9).** They faced each other and looked down at the place where the blood would be sprinkled. Some hear an echo of this when Peter says that the salvation in Christ includes "things into which angels long to look" (1 Pet 1:12).
- **Commentators:** JFB says the repetition of chapter 25 is not useless, because it shows how closely the work matched "the pattern shown on the mount." It calls it remarkable that figures with outstretched wings were beaten "out of a solid piece of gold," a feat few craftsmen could match. Henry treats the chapter briefly, saying only that "Bezaleel and his workmen are still busy," and pointing back to the instructions given earlier. Guzik also stays close to the text here and adds little comment.

#### Making the Table (vv10–16)
- **"He also made the table of acacia wood" (v10).** About 36 inches long, 18 wide and 27 high (90 by 45 by 68 cm), overlaid with gold, with a raised **rim a handbreadth wide** (v12), about 3 inches (7.5 cm), to keep things from sliding off when it was carried.
- **"Rings… as holders for the poles to carry the table" (vv13–15).** Like the ark, it was carried on poles.
- **"Its plates and dishes for incense, and its bowls and flagons with which to pour drink offerings" (v16).** On this table lay the "bread of the Presence," twelve loaves, one for each tribe, set out fresh every Sabbath and eaten by the priests (25:30; Lev 24:5–9). The name means bread set before God's face. It was a standing sign of the covenant meal: God's people always before him, and God always providing for them. David and his men once ate this bread when they were hungry, and Jesus cited it to show that God's law was given for human good (1 Sam 21:1–6; Mark 2:25–27). Jesus called himself "the bread of life" (John 6:35).

#### Making the Lampstand (vv17–24)
- **"The lampstand of pure gold… of hammered work" (v17).** It was not cast in a mold but beaten out of a single piece of gold, a long, painstaking job. Its **base, stem, cups, calyxes and flowers** were all **of one piece with it**, and the description is repeated for emphasis: **"The whole of it was a single piece of hammered work of pure gold"** (v22).
- **"Six branches going out of its sides" (v18).** Three branches on each side of a central stem, seven lamps in all (v23). The design is like a stylized tree. Many see an echo of the tree of life in Eden (Gen 2:9), standing again in the dwelling of God (compare Rev 22:2).
- **"Cups made like almond blossoms" (v19).** The almond is the first tree in Israel to blossom in late winter. Its Hebrew name, *shaqed*, sounds like the word for "watching," and God makes that wordplay with Jeremiah: "I see an almond branch… for I am watching over my word to perform it" (Jer 1:11–12). Aaron's staff later blossomed with almonds to show God's choice of his priesthood (Num 17:8), and it was kept before the ark (Num 17:10; Heb 9:4).
- **"Its seven lamps and its tongs and its trays of pure gold" (v23).** The lamps were small gold bowls, shaped like the clay oil lamps of the time, filled with olive oil. The tongs trimmed the wicks and the trays held the trimmings. The Holy Place had no windows, so the lampstand was its only light. The priests tended it every evening and morning so it never went out (27:20–21; 30:7–8).
- **"Out of a talent of pure gold" (v24).** A talent weighed about 75 pounds (34 kg). The lampstand with all its tools used that much gold. A carving on the Arch of Titus in Rome shows Roman soldiers carrying a seven-branched lampstand from Herod's temple after Jerusalem fell in AD 70, though that was a much later lampstand, not this one.
- **Light in God's house.** In Revelation, the risen Christ walks among seven golden lampstands, which he explains are the churches (Rev 1:12–13, 20). Jesus said, "I am the light of the world" (John 8:12), and also, to his followers, "You are the light of the world" (Matt 5:14). The light in God's house shines by oil, and in Zechariah's vision of a lampstand the explanation is, "Not by might, nor by power, but by my Spirit" (Zech 4:2–6).
- **Commentators:** JFB asks why the lampstand had to be hammered rather than cast and answers simply that it "was done according to order," for reasons not revealed. It calls the tabernacle a "schoolmaster to Christ" (compare Gal 3:24), with furnishings meant as symbols, and sees God's wisdom and goodness in even the ornaments.

#### Making the Altar of Incense (vv25–29)
- **"He made the altar of incense of acacia wood… a cubit… a cubit… two cubits" (v25).** About 18 inches square and 3 feet high (45 by 45 by 90 cm), with horns at the corners, overlaid with gold. It stood in front of the veil, so the incense rose toward the ark behind it (30:6). Incense was burned on it every morning and evening, a picture Scripture itself links with prayer (Ps 141:2; Rev 8:3–4).
- **"Two rings of gold… poles… overlaid them with gold" (vv27–28).** Like everything else, it was made to travel with the people.
- **"He made the holy anointing oil also, and the pure fragrant incense, blended as by the perfumer" (v29).** One line sums up the long recipes of 30:22–38. The oil would anoint the tabernacle and priests, and the incense would burn on this altar. Both were holy, never to be copied for private use (30:32–33, 37–38).

#### Pointing to Christ
Every piece in this chapter stood in the presence of God, and every piece finds its meaning in Jesus. He is the mercy seat, the place where God's justice and mercy meet in blood (Rom 3:25). He is the bread of the Presence, the bread of life that feeds his people (John 6:35). He is the light of the world (John 8:12), and he makes his people lights by his Spirit. He is the priest whose intercession rises before God like incense (Heb 7:25). Bezalel hammered these things out of gold because they pictured something precious. What they pictured came in Christ, whose body was the true temple (John 2:19–21).
